# Scanner engine verification on the November runners

The local engine replay covered all six presets and seven owner-selected tickers during November 16–20, 2026. All times below are US Eastern. The price tests used historical Alpaca SIP records. The event tests used four timestamped wire releases and the Nasdaq halt records for all five trading dates. The corporate-actions read returned no split or name change for these tickers over the reference period. Market caps came from the captured Scanner roster.

## Custom scans and corrections

- **Fast price and volume moves.** The old five-second history buckets discarded earlier observations inside the bucket. On the same one-second timeline this caused 129 missed and 96 false ticker/threshold observations across 30%, 50% and 80% moves over one, three and five minutes with a 10,000-share minimum. The engine now retains value changes at their actual timestamps, coalesces identical updates and records when a gap exceeds the existing ten-second reference allowance. The same 126,120 custom queries produced zero disagreements with the exact as-of reference. This includes a price/float/RVOL scan that withheld matches when dated float was unavailable.
- **GMM and other fast runners.** GMM passed 30% over one minute at 8:30:19 a.m., 50% over one minute at 8:30:26 and 80% over three minutes at 8:34:09 on Nov 16. VIVS, AKAN and CHSN also passed the 30% one-minute rule. No sampled symbol met 80% over one minute with the volume minimum. Separately, the ten-minute 5% move and 10,000-share rule matched the independent calculation on all 22,285 completed-minute observations, with 1,573 qualifying observations across the seven tickers.
- **PR followed by a VWAP reclaim.** From the wire publication time, GMM qualified at 8:31 a.m. within two minutes. VIVS qualified at 8:08 a.m. within five minutes. SEAT at 6:54 a.m. and MEDS at 4:30 p.m. needed the 30-minute window. The rule requires the reclaim after the release.
- **Trade resumption followed by volume.** Both CLRO resumptions on Nov 18 qualified within five minutes, at 9:57 a.m. and 10:57 a.m. Their completed one-minute volumes were 14.69× and 2.76× the preceding baseline. The other eight resumptions produced no verified qualifying observation. Some GMM observations lacked sufficient qualifying baseline bars. The ordered PR and halt rules agreed with separate arithmetic on all 203 cohort event/window observations.
- **Session RVOL and grouped rules.** All seven had calculable same-time RVOL in premarket, regular and after hours on covered sessions. At 256 quarter-hour cutoffs the grouped rule combined a price range with either RVOL of at least 2× or known float of at most 5M. Membership agreed with independent minute-volume sums against the same elapsed period in three preceding sessions. Historical float was unavailable, so positive matches came through the RVOL branch. A separate six-ticker daily SMA50 swing scan matched AKAN, CLRO, GMM and VIVS. The cold history read published in approximately 0.75 seconds, with six symbol reads shared across 30 readers.
- **Prices on a busy tape.** When two eligible prints had different prices and fell within the same millisecond, an earlier print arriving last could replace the newer price. The session book now compares the provider’s fractional seconds. Reversing delivery for 19,846 such historical print pairs across all seven tickers preserved both the true open and latest price. This injected reordering tests the calculation. The capture does not contain original network arrival timestamps.
- **Halt matches after resumption.** The master match stream now applies the Workspace card’s active halt window, including trade resumption, one-hour expiry and trading-date checks. A fractional Eastern halt time can identify the event when UTC is absent. Unreadable halt timing keeps coverage partial. An eligible release can remain visible after the halt clears.

## How fast it updated

The Alpaca event-driven engine schedules at most one evaluation start per second. The polling path waits five seconds after a scan completes. A saved swing scan uses a five-minute cycle, and newly available history can refresh an incomplete result sooner. Each eligible update is shared across readers.

| Local workload | Timing |
| --- | --- |
| Five price presets combined on individual runner snapshots | 0.22 ms at the 95th percentile |
| Five price presets with 1,415 synthetic active symbols | 30 ms at the 95th percentile |
| Five price presets with 4,000 synthetic active symbols | 93 ms at the 95th percentile |
| Custom price/volume scan, 4,000 symbols, 30 sequential readers of the same definition, 181 updates | 6.4 ms at the 95th percentile with 40 changing symbols and 8.9 ms with all 4,000 changing. One evaluation per update. |

The busy-tape path reuses the New York date and minute for trades in the same UTC minute. The original four-query workload took 6.42 seconds before the fixes. The final exact-history run processed the same 13,020 updates and 50,448 queries in 3.37 seconds. This is elapsed time for the replay harness as a whole. The exact-history custom load retained 11,200 value changes with 40 moving symbols versus 724,000 when all symbols changed on every update. History is shared and clears at a session, date or configuration change. Sustained all-symbol activity will use more memory than the three-minute load sample.

These timings measure local processing. The real event records establish what was eligible at the exchange or wire timestamps. They cannot establish when TraderLink originally received each article, when a production gateway delivered a tick, or when a phone painted the update. Point-in-time float was unavailable. The seven-ticker sample provides real positive examples for a subset of the 154 selectable rules. The saved financial scans have separate filing-specific evidence.
