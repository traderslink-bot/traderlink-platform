# 1. Category Purpose

Category 18 recognizes how a trader wants an already-valid answer presented:
more concisely, in the normal explanatory form, with more supporting detail,
as a table, as bounded coaching, or as an audit trail. It gives the future
language layer a stable presentation preference without changing what question
was asked or what Journal evidence can truthfully answer it.

This category applies only after the underlying intent and query have been
selected and resolved, server-derived account authorization has been enforced,
and filters, dimensions, metric definitions, capability state, clarification
requirements, and evidence boundaries have been resolved by their owners. A
mode may change presentation and amount of detail; it must never change the
selected/resolved underlying query, server-derived account authorization,
mathematical truth, account scope, evidence, coverage, privacy, clarification
need, supported/unavailable state, or causation and advice guardrails.

The category does not own analytic intents, metrics, dimensions, filters,
operators, data capabilities, permissions, result calculations, evidence
selection, or any write action. It also does not treat a preference cue as a
request to bypass a limitation or disclose additional private data.

# 2. Category Boundaries

## Included

Category 18 owns the six ordered response-presentation preferences from the
controlling plan:

- `brief`: a concise answer that retains essential numbers and material
  limitations;
- `standard`: conclusion, evidence, interpretation, and limitation;
- `detailed`: additional available breakdowns, methodology, examples, and
  supporting trades without fabrication;
- `table`: formatting for a grouping, comparison, or ranking already supported
  by the validated result; it never creates a grouping, comparison, or ranking;
- `coach`: an evidence-backed observation plus one focused improvement, with
  no invented trader motive, causation, or prediction and no personalized
  financial advice; and
- `audit`: definitions, exact filters, exclusions, formulas, and privacy-safe
  evidence identifiers, never raw internal, broker, account, or source
  identifiers.

It also owns recognition planning for the source-plan cues `just tell me`,
`give me the details`, `show the math`, `put it in a table`, `explain it
simply`, and `show the trades`, including their ambiguity and overlap rules.

## Excluded

The following remain outside this category:

- determining the user's analytic, explanatory, comparison, ranking, or
  coaching intent: Category 1;
- metrics, formulas, units, fee treatment, populations, and eligibility:
  their owning metric and Journal Analytics contracts;
- account, currency, ticker, date, timezone, provenance, selected-trade, and
  other dimensions or filters: their owning language categories and
  server-authoritative data contracts;
- query operators, comparison/ranking meaning, conversation context, trader
  terminology, and ambiguity resolution: Categories 12 through 17 as
  applicable;
- access control, privacy/redaction policy, evidence authorization,
  unsupported-state policy, causation policy, and financial-advice guardrails:
  Category 19 and server-authoritative policy; and
- the AI Chat runtime, tool routing, UI controls, persistence, database
  schema, analytics implementation, or Journal mutations.

## Cross-Category References

Category 18 references, but does not redefine:

- Category 1 for the primary underlying intent and the existing bounded
  `generate_coaching` intent;
- Categories 2 through 8 and later metric categories for deterministic facts,
  formulas, coverage, and unsupported states;
- Categories 11 through 17 for dimensions, time, operators,
  comparison/ranking, context, slang, and ambiguity;
- Category 19 for privacy, account isolation, evidence disclosure, causation,
  advice, and capability policies; and
- the response/result contract and presentation layer for whether any extra
  breakdown, example, trade reference, table, formula, or evidence identifier
  is actually available and safe to display.

`show the trades` is deliberately cross-category: it can express a desire for
more detail, but it is also an evidence-access request. It must not map to a
response preference alone or reveal trade data before the selected/resolved
underlying query, server-derived account authorization, privacy policy, and
evidence availability are satisfied.

## 3.1 Required Planning Questions

1. **What exact problem does this category solve?**

   It separates a request for answer form or level of detail from the request's
   factual meaning, so presentation wording never silently alters the selected
   and resolved query, server-derived account authorization, or answer.

2. **What canonical concepts belong here?**

   Exactly the six ordered source-plan modes in Section 4: `brief`,
   `standard`, `detailed`, `table`, `coach`, and `audit`.

3. **What related concepts belong elsewhere?**

   Every fact-selection and interpretation concept belongs with its existing
   owner: intents, metrics, dimensions, filters, context, evidence access,
   policy, and permissions are not response preferences.

4. **What data is required?**

   No mode creates a data requirement. It consumes only the already authorized
   result, its definitions, limitations, coverage, and privacy-safe evidence
   references. Detailed, table, coach, and audit output additionally require
   the requested material to exist in that validated result contract. No mode
   may reselect or reinterpret the query or account authorization to obtain
   more data.

5. **Which deterministic tools will answer these requests?**

   Existing deterministic Journal/analytics tools answer the underlying
   request, not the preference. A future validated response formatter may
   render a selected mode after a tool result; the AI Chat interpreter, router,
   and formatter remain planned.

6. **Which concepts are directly observed?**

   None. A user's wording may directly express a preference, but the mode is
   an instruction for presentation rather than a Journal observation.

7. **Which concepts are deterministically derived?**

   None as financial facts. A safe formatter may deterministically select an
   allowed layout from a resolved mode, but it cannot derive missing evidence,
   explanations, causation, or advice.

8. **Which concepts are proxy indicators?**

   None. A preference does not indicate confidence, skill, risk, performance,
   causation, or a future outcome.

9. **Which concepts are user-labelled?**

   The selected response preference may be user-supplied or saved as a
   presentation setting under a future policy, but it does not label a trade,
   metric, or evidence row and cannot alter their meaning.

10. **Which concepts are not measurable?**

    Whether a mode is clearer, more helpful, or persuasive is not a Journal
    metric. A request for details that the evidence does not contain remains
    unavailable or limited; the formatter must state that boundary rather than
    invent content.

11. **Which terms are ambiguous?**

    `explain it simply` may mean a brief plain-language answer or a standard
    explanation; `show the math` may seek methodology, a formula, or an audit
    trail; `show the trades` may seek supporting examples, a trade list, or
    evidence access. These cues require the underlying request and policy
    context before final mode selection.

12. **What defaults are safe?**

    With no explicit or trusted saved preference, use `standard` presentation
    if the underlying answer can be rendered. It must still include an
    applicable limitation, cannot suppress required clarification or
    unavailable state, and cannot add evidence beyond the underlying result.

13. **What conditions require clarification?**

    Ask a focused clarification only when the preference wording leaves a
    material choice unresolved after the underlying request is known, such as
    whether `show the trades` means privacy-safe supporting examples or the
    underlying trade search/list. Do not ask a presentation question when the
    underlying intent, scope, data availability, or policy already requires a
    higher-priority clarification.

14. **What combinations are invalid?**

    A primary response mode cannot override another primary mode in the same
    answer without an explicit composition policy. `table` may only format a
    grouping, comparison, or ranking already supported by the validated result;
    it cannot create one. `coach` is invalid where no verified performance
    evidence supports one focused improvement, and it never permits stock
    picks, trade signals, personalized investment instructions, invented trader
    motive, causal claims, or predictions. `audit` cannot disclose raw
    internal, broker, account, or source identifiers or unavailable evidence;
    it uses only privacy-safe evidence identifiers. No mode may change the
    selected/resolved underlying query, server-derived account authorization,
    or an unsupported result into a supported one.

15. **What evaluation coverage proves completion?**

    Sections 5 through 7 cover every canonical mode, each
    source cue, preference-only versus underlying-intent interpretation,
    mode conflicts, `show the trades` evidence-access overlap, unavailable
    detail, table-ineligible results, privacy-safe audit identifiers,
    coaching guardrails, and the rule that a preference preserves the
    selected/resolved query, server-derived account authorization, all facts,
    scope, coverage, clarification, and limitations.

## 3.2 Dependencies

- **Required earlier category:** locked Category 1 supplies the underlying
  intent and bounded coaching route; Category 18 does not substitute for it.
- **Required data/services:** a server-authorized deterministic result with
  its capability status, definitions, scope, coverage, limitations, and only
  authorized evidence references.
- **Required analytics tools:** the tool selected for the already resolved
  underlying request. This category introduces no analytics query or formula.
- **Required UI context:** any eventual UI may expose a preference, but the
  preference must be applied after query validation and cannot bypass trusted
  selected-page or selected-entity context.
- **Required user-defined vocabulary:** none for Version 1. A future saved
  answer preference requires a separate policy and persistence contract.
- **Required external data:** none.
- **Unsupported dependencies:** production AI Chat routing/formatting,
  arbitrary trade-evidence disclosure, personalized financial advice, and
  causal conclusions are not provided by this inventory.

## 3.3 Risks

- **Ambiguity risk:** ordinary detail cues can collide with explanation,
  evidence access, methodology, or conversation context.
- **Synonym-collision risk:** `coach` may be mistaken for the coaching intent;
  Category 18 selects presentation only after that intent is valid.
- **Duplicate-concept risk:** `table` must only format a grouping, comparison,
  or ranking already supported by the validated result and must not create one;
  `audit` must not become a new analytics capability.
- **Unsupported-data risk:** detailed, audit, and trade-example requests can
  imply facts or evidence that the result does not contain.
- **False-positive mapping risk:** `show the trades` must not automatically
  expose rows, and `show the math` must not claim a formula is available.
- **Privacy and account-boundary risk:** expanded detail, tables, and audits
  must preserve server-derived account authorization and use only privacy-safe
  evidence identifiers, never raw internal, broker, account, or source
  identifiers.
- **Causation/advice risk:** coaching language can invent trader motive,
  causation, or predictions, overstate historical association, or turn a
  focused improvement into personal financial advice.
- **Sample-size risk:** concision never permits omission of a material small-
  sample, partial-coverage, or unavailable-evidence limitation.

The two registry batches contain complete registries for all six

Interpretation priority is fixed for every registry: resolve the locked
Category 1 intent, selected query, server-derived authorization/account scope,

formats the validated result. It never changes facts, mathematics, evidence,

clear later correction replaces an earlier presentation preference for the same
validated result. When multiple explicit response modes conflict and no

modes. Preserve the validated result and ask one focused presentation
clarification only after every higher-priority intent, query, authorization,
capability, clarification, privacy, and safety issue is resolved.

## `brief` Language Registry

### Exact Definition

Render the already validated result in one or two concise sentences while
retaining essential numbers and every material limitation. `brief` removes
optional elaboration only; it cannot omit a clarification, refusal,
authorization boundary, unsupported/unavailable state, coverage warning,
privacy rule, confirmation requirement, or safety guardrail.

### Formal Wording

- Provide a brief response.
- Return a concise answer with the essential figures and limitations.
- Summarize the validated result without additional elaboration.

### Normal Conversational Wording

- Just tell me.
- Keep it short.
- Give me the short answer.
- Can you make that quick?

### Trader Slang

- Give me the quick take.
- Bottom line only.
- What's the quick read?
- Give me the TLDR.

### Abbreviations

- `TL;DR` and `TLDR` may indicate `brief` only in clear answer-format context.
- Bare `short` is not an abbreviation for this mode because it may mean a short
  position or short-side trade.
- `$TLDR`, a ticker-like token, or a user-defined label named `TLDR` must not be
  normalized to `brief` without explicit response-format context.

### Common Misspellings

- `breif answer`
- `shrot answer`
- `keep it breif`
- `tl dr`

### Noisy or Incomplete Input

- `quick answer pls`
- `just numbers short`
- `tldr?`
- `same question but shorter`

### Singular and Plural Forms

- `a brief answer`; `brief answers`
- `a short response`; `short responses`
- Plural wording applies the preference to each validated answer part; it does
  not merge or discard underlying parts.

### Full Questions

- Can you give me a brief answer with the main number?
- What is the bottom line, including anything important I should know?
- Can you answer that in one or two sentences?

### Commands

- Just tell me the result.
- Keep the answer short.
- Give me the TLDR, but include the limitation.
