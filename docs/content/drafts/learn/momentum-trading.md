# Momentum Trading

This lesson is part of the **Trading Styles And Playbooks** course. It follows [Short Selling Basics](/learn/short-selling-basics/) and leads into [Pullbacks And Dip-Buy Setups](/learn/pullbacks-and-dip-buy-setups/).

Momentum trading focuses on stocks with strong directional movement, participation, and attention. The style can involve breakouts, high relative volume, news reactions, or continuation attempts.

Momentum is not the same as chasing. A momentum trade can be planned. Chasing usually happens after the clean plan is gone.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define momentum trading as a style.
- Understand participation and volume context.
- Separate planned momentum from late chasing.
- Review entry location and extension risk.
- Connect momentum to risk and invalidation.

## Quick Definition

Momentum trading is a style focused on trading strong price movement when participation and context support the idea.

- Price is moving with speed or range expansion.
- Volume or relative volume may increase.
- Catalysts can attract attention.
- Breakouts, reclaims, and continuation attempts may appear.
- Risk can increase quickly if entries are late.

Momentum describes movement and participation. It does not guarantee continuation.

## How This Style Works

Momentum traders often review:

- What caused attention or activity.
- Whether volume supports the move.
- Where key levels are.
- Whether the entry is early, structured, or late.
- How far price is from support or invalidation.
- Whether the next level leaves enough room for the idea.

## What This Style Requires

Momentum requires selectivity and chase control.

- Clear reason the stock is moving.
- Volume and liquidity review.
- Defined entry area and invalidation.
- Awareness of extension from levels.
- Position size that respects volatility.
- A rule for missed entries.

## When It Can Mislead

Momentum can mislead when traders assume a strong move will keep going. The stronger a move looks, the easier it is to enter late without realistic risk.

## Realistic Example

A stock breaks above resistance on high volume after news. A planned momentum trader reviews the catalyst, volume, level, and risk before entry. A chasing trader enters several candles later near the next resistance because the stock looks strong. The same stock can create two very different decisions.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is entering because the stock is moving without knowing the setup.

Another mistake is ignoring distance from the clean risk area.

Traders also make mistakes when they use high volume as a guarantee.

Another mistake is adding size after missing the first move.

A final mistake is refusing to mark the trade as a chase in review.

## Practical Checklist

- The source of momentum is understood.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- Nearby support and resistance are mapped.
- The entry is not far from a realistic risk area.
- The trade is not only based on urgency.
- Position size respects volatility.
- There is a plan for missed entries.

## Apply This In Review

- What created the momentum?
- Was volume actually supporting the move?
- Was my entry structured or late?
- Where was the clean risk area?
- Did I chase after missing the first move?
- Did I ignore resistance because the stock looked strong?
- Would I take the same entry again?

Momentum review should separate strong stock behavior from strong trade quality.

## FAQ

### What is momentum trading?

Momentum trading focuses on stocks with strong movement and participation, usually reviewed with price, volume, catalyst, levels, and risk.

### Is momentum trading the same as chasing?

No. Momentum can be planned, while chasing usually means entering late because of urgency.

### What makes momentum risky?

Fast movement, extension, wide spreads, volatility, and emotional pressure can all increase risk.

### Does high volume guarantee continuation?

No. Volume shows participation, but price can still fail, fade, or reverse.

### What should momentum traders review?

Review catalyst, volume, levels, entry timing, extension, risk, and whether the trade was planned or reactive.

### Can momentum trades fail quickly?

Yes. Momentum can fade or reverse, especially when entries are late or liquidity changes.
