# What Are Trading Indicators?

This lesson is part of the **Technical Indicators And Tools** course. It follows [Holding Through News](/learn/holding-through-news/) and leads into [Why Indicators Lag Price](/learn/why-indicators-lag/).

Trading indicators are tools that transform price, volume, volatility, or time into a visual reference. They can help traders organize context, but they should not be treated as automatic instructions.

This course teaches indicators as measurement tools. The purpose is to understand what each tool adds, what it misses, and how to review it beside price, volume, levels, and risk.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what trading indicators are.
- Separate indicator context from trade signals.
- Understand why indicators need price, volume, and risk context.
- Recognize that indicators can lag or conflict.
- Review whether an indicator helped or distracted from the plan.

## Quick Definition

A trading indicator is a chart or calculation tool that uses market data to show context.

- Trend context.
- Momentum context.
- Volatility context.
- Volume context.
- Average price context.
- Distance from recent ranges.
- Possible areas to review after trades.

An indicator is not a guarantee. It is one input in a larger decision process.

## What It Measures

Indicators measure past and current market data. Some smooth price movement, some compare recent momentum, some show volatility, and some show volume or average price. Because they are based on data that already happened, they should be reviewed as context rather than certainty.

## How Traders Use It As Context

A trader may use indicators to ask better questions:

- Is price extended from an average?
- Is momentum increasing or fading?
- Is volatility expanding or contracting?
- Is price above or below a widely watched reference area?
- Does the indicator agree with price structure and volume?
- Did the indicator add clarity or create conflict?

The best use is usually comparison, not obedience.

## When It Can Mislead

Indicators can mislead when traders treat them as standalone signals. A moving average can be crossed in chop. RSI can stay elevated during a strong trend. VWAP can be reclaimed and lost again. The indicator must be reviewed with structure, volume, liquidity, and risk.

## Realistic Example

A stock is pushing higher and RSI is elevated. A weak review says, "RSI was high, so the move had to reverse."

A better review asks whether price was extended from support, whether volume was still expanding, whether there was nearby resistance, whether the trader had a risk plan, and whether RSI actually changed the decision.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is using indicators as buy or sell commands.

Another mistake is stacking too many indicators until the chart becomes hard to read.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore price structure because an indicator looks interesting.

Another mistake is changing indicator settings until the chart confirms what the trader already wants to believe.

A final mistake is not reviewing whether the indicator actually helped the trade decision.

## Practical Checklist

- I know what the indicator measures.
- I know what the indicator does not measure.
- I checked price structure and levels separately.
- I checked volume or liquidity where relevant.
- I did not treat the indicator as a guarantee.
- I had a risk plan independent of the indicator.
- I can review whether the indicator helped or distracted.

## Apply This In Review

- What indicator did I use?
- What did it measure?
- Did it agree with price and volume?
- Did it conflict with key levels?
- Did I enter because of the indicator alone?
- Did I ignore risk because of the indicator?
- Did the indicator help the review after the trade?
- Should this tool stay in my process?

The goal is to make indicators useful, not magical.

## Trader Intelligence Bridge

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review completed decisions. For indicators, that means reviewing whether a tool supported context, distracted from the plan, or appeared in repeated mistakes.

## FAQ

### What are trading indicators?

Trading indicators are chart tools or calculations that use price, volume, volatility, or time data to show market context.

### Are indicators signals?

They should not be treated as automatic signals. They are better used as context alongside price, volume, levels, and risk.

### Can indicators be wrong?

Indicators can be misleading, delayed, or noisy, especially in choppy markets or without context.

### What should beginners learn first?

Beginners should learn price levels, volume, risk, and what an indicator measures before relying on it.

### How many indicators should I use?

Use only tools that add clear review value. Too many indicators can create confusion.

### How should indicators be reviewed?

Review what the indicator showed, whether it matched price and volume, and whether it improved or weakened the decision.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice. Trading indicators can support review and context, but they do not predict price or guarantee trading results.
