# Trade Review And Improvement

This lesson is part of the **Trade Review And Improvement** course. It follows [Averaging Down](/learn/averaging-down/) and leads into [Trade Risk Review](/learn/trade-risk-review/).

Trade review is the process of studying completed trades so the trader can understand what actually happened: the plan, the risk, the execution, the management decisions, and the behavior pattern.

This course is the bridge between learning concepts and improving from real trades. Reading lessons can build knowledge, but review turns that knowledge into feedback.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain why review is different from only checking profit and loss.
- Separate trade outcome from decision quality.
- Identify the main categories a useful review should cover.
- Use review to find repeated behavior patterns.
- Understand how Trader Intelligence fits as a review tool, not a prediction tool.

## Quick Definition

Trade review and improvement means studying completed trades in a structured way so the trader can find what was planned, what actually happened, and what should be adjusted.

- Plan review.
- Risk review.
- Execution review.
- Trade-management review.
- Behavior-pattern review.
- Context review.
- Specific next-step improvement notes.

The goal is not to prove that a trade was good or bad because it made or lost money. The goal is to understand decision quality.

## Why It Matters

A trader can have a green trade with poor process and a red trade with good process. Without review, the trader may reinforce bad habits because the outcome felt good, or abandon good habits because one trade lost money.

Structured review helps traders slow down the feedback loop. Instead of guessing what went wrong, they can compare the original plan with the actual decisions.

## Practical Review Process

A useful review process checks the trade in layers:

- Start with the original plan: setup, context, risk, and intended management.
- Compare planned risk with actual risk.
- Compare planned entry and exit with actual execution.
- Review the chart, volume, liquidity, and news context.
- Identify any rule break or emotional decision.
- Write one specific lesson, not a vague complaint.
- Decide whether a rule, checklist, or behavior tag should change.

This makes review practical. The trader is not trying to write a long essay after every trade. They are trying to capture the decision points that can improve future review.

## Realistic Example

A trader takes a breakout trade that closes green. A basic review says, "Good trade, made money."

A structured review shows more detail: the entry was late, the stop was not defined, and the trader exited early because the position size felt uncomfortable. The trade made money, but the review shows three things to improve.

That is the point of the course: make the real lesson visible.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is reviewing only red trades. Winning trades can include chasing, oversizing, poor exits, or broken rules.

Another mistake is writing vague notes like "bad trade" or "good trade." Those notes do not explain what should change.

Traders also make mistakes when they review too late and rewrite the story with hindsight.

Another mistake is tracking too many details but never turning them into a practical next step.

A final mistake is treating review like punishment. Review should be honest, but it should also be useful and repeatable.

## Practical Checklist

- The trade has an original plan to compare against.
- Risk and position size are reviewed separately from outcome.
- Execution quality is reviewed separately from setup quality.
- The review names any behavior pattern clearly.
- The final lesson is specific enough to use next time.
- The review avoids blaming the market for every result.
- The next improvement step is small and practical.

## Apply This In Review

- What was the trade plan?
- What actually happened?
- Where did risk change?
- Was the entry planned or emotional?
- Was the exit planned or emotional?
- What context did I miss?
- What repeated pattern showed up?
- What one rule or checklist item should I improve?

Good review is not about perfect hindsight. It is about building a clearer feedback loop.

## Trader Intelligence Bridge

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders organize completed trades into reviewable patterns: risk behavior, execution quality, trade management, missed rules, and repeated mistakes.

For trade review, the product bridge is simple: the tool should help the trader see their own decisions more clearly after the trade, not tell them what the next trade will do.

## FAQ

### What is trade review?

Trade review is the process of studying completed trades to understand plan quality, risk, execution, management decisions, and repeated behavior patterns.

### Why is trade review important?

It helps traders learn from decisions instead of only reacting to profit and loss.

### Should winning trades be reviewed?

Yes. Winning trades can still contain poor risk, late entries, emotional exits, or broken rules.

### What should a trade review include?

A useful review can include setup, risk, position size, entry, exit, execution, context, behavior, and one specific improvement note.

### Is trade review the same as journaling?

A journal is one way to record review. The key is the review process, not the label.

### Can review guarantee better results?

No. Review can improve awareness and process, but it cannot guarantee outcomes.
