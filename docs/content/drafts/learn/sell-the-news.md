# Sell The News

This lesson is part of the **Trading Styles And Playbooks** course. It follows [News Fade](/learn/news-fade/) and leads into [Multi-Day Runner](/learn/multi-day-runner/).

Sell the news is a phrase traders use when a stock declines or fades after a catalyst becomes public. The phrase can describe a reaction, but it should not be treated as an instruction.

Sometimes the market already expected the news. Sometimes details disappoint. Sometimes supply, valuation, liquidity, or positioning matter more than the headline.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define sell-the-news as a reaction concept.
- Understand why expected catalysts can fade.
- Review expectation versus actual detail.
- Avoid using the phrase as a trade command.
- Connect reaction review to volume, levels, and filings.

## Quick Definition

Sell the news describes a market reaction where price weakens after a public catalyst, often because the news was expected, priced in, or not strong enough to sustain the prior move.

- The stock may have run before the event.
- The catalyst becomes public.
- The reaction may be muted, choppy, or negative.
- Volume and levels show whether participants continue or exit.
- Related filings or guidance can change interpretation.

The phrase describes what happened. It does not tell traders what to do.

Source check: SEC EDGAR provides free public access to company filings, and SEC after-hours risk materials warn that news outside regular hours can interact with lower liquidity, wider spreads, and uncertain prices. Sell-the-news review should compare expectation, actual announcement details, related filings, volume, and level behavior rather than treating the phrase as a trade rule.

## How This Style Works

Sell-the-news review usually compares:

- What traders may have expected before the event.
- What the actual announcement said.
- Whether guidance, terms, or details were weaker than hoped.
- Whether price already ran into the catalyst.
- Whether volume confirmed continued interest or distribution.
- Whether important support or VWAP areas failed.

## What This Style Requires

This concept requires expectation review.

- Pre-event price context.
- Catalyst detail review.
- Related filings or earnings detail if relevant.
- Volume and level review.
- Risk and invalidation planning.
- Awareness that news can still continue higher.

## When It Can Mislead

Sell-the-news thinking can mislead when traders assume good news should fade only because price already moved. Sometimes strong details and participation can support continuation.

## Realistic Example

A stock rallies for several days before an expected product update. When the news arrives, the details are not materially better than expected, volume fades, and price loses a key intraday level. That may be reviewed as sell-the-news behavior. If details are stronger than expected and buyers continue participating, the label may not fit.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is using sell the news as a trade instruction.

Another mistake is ignoring whether the catalyst was expected or surprising.

Traders also make mistakes when they skip the actual announcement details.

Another mistake is ignoring volume and levels after the news.

A final mistake is fighting continued strength because the phrase sounds familiar.

## Practical Checklist

- Pre-news price movement is reviewed.
- The actual catalyst details are read.
- Expectations versus actual details are compared.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed after the release.
- Support, resistance, and VWAP behavior are checked.
- Related filings are checked if relevant.
- Invalidation is defined.

## Apply This In Review

- Did price run before the news?
- Was the catalyst expected or surprising?
- Were details stronger, weaker, or in line with expectations?
- What did volume do after the announcement?
- Which level mattered after the news?
- Did I use a phrase instead of analysis?
- Was invalidation respected?

Sell-the-news review should explain why the reaction happened, not simply repeat the phrase.

## FAQ

### What does sell the news mean?

Sell the news describes price weakness after a public catalyst, often when the news was expected or not enough to sustain the prior move.

### Is sell the news a trade signal?

No. It is a reaction concept, not an instruction.

### Why can good news fade?

The news may have been priced in, details may disappoint, volume may fade, or traders may reduce exposure after the event.

### Can news continue instead of fading?

Yes. Strong details and continued participation can support continuation.

### What should traders review?

Review pre-news run-up, expectations, actual details, filings, volume, levels, and risk.

### How is sell the news different from news fade?

News fade is broad reaction weakness after news; sell the news often emphasizes expected catalysts and prior positioning.
