# Day Trading Session Review

This lesson completes the **Day Trading Workflow** course. It follows [After-Hours Trading](/learn/after-hours-trading/) and connects next to [Swing Trading For Beginners](/learn/swing-trading-for-beginners/).

A day trading session review is the process of studying the day after trades are complete. It turns the workflow into feedback.

The goal is not to relive every candle. The goal is to identify what was planned, what was reactive, what was managed well, and what needs a rule or adjustment.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Review a day trading session by process, not only outcome.
- Separate preparation, timing, risk, execution, and behavior.
- Identify repeated mistakes by session segment.
- Use review notes to improve the next workflow.
- Connect day trading review to broader risk management.

## Quick Definition

A day trading session review is a structured review of completed trades and decisions from one trading day.

It can include:

- Watchlist quality.
- Session segment.
- Setup clarity.
- Entry and exit timing.
- Risk and sizing.
- Spread and slippage.
- Emotional state.
- Rule breaks.
- What to improve next session.

Review is where the trading workflow becomes measurable.

## The Review Framework

A practical review can use five questions:

- Preparation: Did I know what I was watching and why?
- Timing: Did the trade fit the session conditions?
- Risk: Was the risk defined before entry?
- Execution: Did the fill and exit match the plan?
- Behavior: Did I follow my rules after pressure increased?

This is more useful than asking only whether the trade was green or red.

## Review By Session Segment

Day trading mistakes often cluster by time of day.

- Premarket may reveal thin-liquidity entries or headline reactions.
- The open may reveal chasing, over-sizing, or fast exits.
- Midday may reveal boredom trades or low-volume chop.
- Power hour may reveal late-day forcing.
- After-hours may reveal headline risk or overnight exposure without a plan.

Tagging the session segment can make patterns easier to see.

## Realistic Example

A trader ends the day slightly green but realizes most gains came from one planned open trade. Later trades were smaller but reactive, especially during midday. The review shows the trader did not lose much money, but the process weakened as the day went on.

The improvement note is not "trade better." It is more specific: reduce midday activity unless volume returns and the setup matches the watchlist plan.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is reviewing only the largest win or loss.

Another mistake is treating a profitable day as automatically well-executed.

Traders also make mistakes when they skip review after a frustrating day.

Another mistake is writing vague notes that cannot change future behavior.

A final mistake is reviewing only charts while ignoring spread, slippage, sizing, and emotional state.

## Practical Checklist

- I reviewed the original watchlist.
- I tagged each trade by session segment.
- I compared planned versus actual behavior.
- I checked risk, size, spread, and slippage.
- I identified one repeated mistake or strength.
- I wrote one practical adjustment for the next session.
- I did not confuse outcome with process quality.

## Apply This In Review

- What was my best-planned decision today?
- What was my most reactive decision?
- Which session segment created the most trouble?
- Did I respect my risk limits?
- Did execution quality affect the result?
- Did I stop when the plan was gone?
- What is the one rule or filter I will carry forward?

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review completed sessions by setup, timing, execution, risk, and repeated behavior patterns. It should support honest review, not replace judgment or promise future outcomes.

## FAQ

### Why review a day trading session?

Session review helps traders identify repeated decisions, mistakes, strengths, and conditions that affected performance.

### Should a green day still be reviewed?

Yes. A positive outcome can still include weak process, and a difficult day can still include good decisions.

### What should a day trader review first?

Start with whether the trades matched the plan, then review risk, timing, execution, and behavior.

### How detailed should session review be?

It should be detailed enough to create useful improvement notes, but not so long that it becomes impossible to repeat.
