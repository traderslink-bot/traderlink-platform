# Swing Trading Risk Management

This lesson is part of the **Swing Trading Workflow** course. It follows [Swing Trading For Beginners](/learn/swing-trading-for-beginners/) and leads into [Swing Trading Support Resistance](/learn/swing-trading-support-resistance/).

Swing trading risk management is the process of deciding how much risk belongs in a multi-session trade before the trade is already under pressure.

The key difference is overnight exposure. A day trader can often close before the session ends. A swing trader may still be exposed to earnings, filings, offerings, sector headlines, broad-market weakness, and gaps before the next regular-hours open.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain why swing risk is different from intraday risk.
- Define invalidation before entry.
- Connect position size to distance from invalidation and gap risk.
- Recognize when an event changes the risk profile.
- Review whether risk decisions matched the original plan.

## Quick Definition

Swing trading risk management means planning position size, invalidation, event exposure, and review rules for a trade held across multiple sessions.

It usually includes:

- Entry area.
- Invalidation area.
- Position size.
- Maximum acceptable loss.
- Gap and overnight risk.
- Event checks.
- Liquidity and spread review.
- Rules for holding, reducing, adding, or exiting.

Risk management does not make a trade safe. It makes the risk visible enough to review.

## Invalidation Comes Before Position Size

A common risk mistake is choosing size first and then looking for a level that makes the size feel acceptable.

The better order is:

1. Identify the trade idea.
2. Mark the area or condition that would weaken the idea.
3. Estimate the risk between the entry area and invalidation.
4. Decide whether the position size still makes sense.
5. Adjust or skip if the risk is too large.

For swing trades, invalidation is often a zone or condition, not a perfect tick.

Examples:

- A breakout loses the prior resistance zone and cannot reclaim it.
- A pullback loses the support area on rising volume.
- A catalyst stock fails to hold the level created by the news reaction.
- New filings change the risk enough that the original thesis no longer applies.

## Overnight Risk Changes The Math

Overnight risk means the trader may not be able to exit at the exact planned level.

A position that looks controlled during regular hours can open far away from the previous close after:

- Earnings.
- Offering news.
- SEC filings.
- Clinical or regulatory updates.
- Analyst changes.
- Sector news.
- Broad-market futures movement.
- Company-specific headlines.

That means swing position size should usually consider both chart risk and gap risk.

## Realistic Example

A trader is considering a swing trade near $12.00. The support zone is around $11.20 to $11.35, and the next resistance area is near $14.00.

Before entry, the trader reviews:

- Is $11.20 to $11.35 the actual thesis area or just a convenient number?
- Would a move below that zone invalidate the idea?
- Is there earnings or a filing deadline this week?
- Is the stock liquid enough for the planned size?
- Could an overnight gap create a larger loss than the chart plan suggests?

If the trader cannot accept the gap risk, the plan needs adjustment before entry.

## Risk Changes While The Trade Is Open

Swing risk is not static. A trade can become riskier even if the entry was reasonable.

Risk may change when:

- Price approaches major resistance.
- Volume fades after the first move.
- A scheduled event gets closer.
- A filing changes dilution or cash-runway context.
- Broad market conditions weaken.
- The stock starts closing below the level that supported the thesis.

Reviewing risk during the hold is part of the workflow.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is using the same size for every swing trade regardless of volatility, level distance, or overnight risk.

Another mistake is moving the invalidation area after the trade starts failing. Sometimes the trader is updating the plan, but often they are avoiding the review.

Traders also make mistakes when they treat stop placement as the whole risk plan. Stops, alerts, and planned exits can help, but gaps may not respect the planned level.

Another mistake is holding through earnings or filings without acknowledging that the trade has become an event-risk trade.

A final mistake is turning a failed swing trade into an unplanned long-term hold.

## Practical Checklist

- The invalidation area is defined before entry.
- Position size fits the distance to invalidation.
- Overnight gap risk is considered.
- Earnings, filings, and known events are checked.
- Liquidity and spread are acceptable for the planned size.
- The trader knows what would change the thesis.
- Risk is reviewed after each session while the trade is open.

## Apply This In Review

- Did I define risk before entering?
- Did my position size match the real invalidation area?
- Did I account for overnight or event risk?
- Did I move risk because the chart changed or because I hoped?
- Did a filing, headline, or market event change the thesis?
- Did I reduce risk when the trade became less clean?
- Did I turn a swing trade into an unplanned hold?

The best swing risk review studies whether the risk decision was planned, updated for valid reasons, and followed consistently.

## FAQ

### What is swing trading risk management?

Swing trading risk management is planning position size, invalidation, gap risk, event exposure, and review rules before holding a trade across multiple sessions.

### Why is swing trading risk different from day trading risk?

Swing trades can be affected by overnight news, gaps, earnings, filings, and market moves while the trader may not be able to react at the planned level.

### What is invalidation in swing trading?

Invalidation is the price area, condition, or new information that shows the original swing thesis is no longer working or needs review.

### Should swing traders size around stops?

Many traders size around planned risk, but they should also remember that overnight gaps may not execute at the planned stop or alert level.

### Can a swing trade still lose with good risk management?

Yes. Risk management does not prevent losses. It helps keep losses planned, reviewable, and aligned with the trader's risk limits.

### How should swing risk be reviewed?

Review the original risk plan, size, invalidation area, event checks, overnight decisions, and whether the trader followed the plan when conditions changed.
