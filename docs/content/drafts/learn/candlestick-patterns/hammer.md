# Hammer Candlestick

This lesson is part of the **Candlestick Patterns In Context** course. It follows [Engulfing Candle](/learn/candlestick-patterns/engulfing-candle/) and leads into [Inside Bar](/learn/candlestick-patterns/inside-bar/).

A hammer candle has a small body near the top of its range and a long lower wick. It shows that price sold off and recovered before the candle closed.

A hammer near support may be meaningful, but it still needs context and a failure plan.

![Candlestick chart showing a hammer candle near support with volume and follow-through context.](/images/learn/chart-reading/hammer-support-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize the pattern without treating the name as a trade plan.
- Read the candle beside levels, volume, trend, and liquidity.
- Identify common failure conditions.
- Review entry, exit, and risk behavior after the trade.
- Use the pattern as context rather than prediction.

## Quick Definition

A hammer is a candle with a small body near the top of the range and a long lower wick.

- The lower wick is the defining feature.
- The body usually sits near the upper part of the candle.
- The candle often appears after selling pressure or a pullback.
- The wick low can help review invalidation.
- Follow-through matters after the candle closes.

A hammer shows recovery from lower prices during that candle, not proof of reversal.

## What The Candle Shows

Hammer candles show a test below the body and recovery into the close.

- Possible support reaction.
- Possible failed breakdown.
- Possible temporary demand after selling pressure.
- Possible liquidity flush.
- Possible pause before another move lower.

## Context That Matters

Hammer context depends heavily on location.

- Support or prior low nearby.
- Volume on the hammer candle.
- Trend before the candle.
- Distance from the wick low to entry.
- Next candle follow-through.
- News, float, and liquidity context.

## When It Can Mislead

Hammer candles can mislead when traders assume the lower wick created support. Price can break below the wick low and continue lower.

## Realistic Example

A stock pulls back toward a support zone, flushes slightly below it, then closes back above the zone with a long lower wick. The review asks whether the next candle held above support and whether risk was defined below the wick.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is entering every hammer candle.

Another mistake is ignoring the broader downtrend.

Traders also make mistakes when they enter far above the wick and accept too much risk.

Another mistake is ignoring volume.

A final mistake is holding after the wick low fails.

## Practical Checklist

- The candle is identified correctly.
- The pattern formed near a meaningful area, not random noise.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- The broader trend and nearby levels are checked.
- Risk is defined without relying on the candle name.
- The next candles are reviewed for follow-through or failure.
- The trade can be reviewed honestly after completion.

Hammer review should focus on level reaction, wick low, volume, and whether the idea failed.

## FAQ

### What is a hammer candlestick?

It is a candle with a small body near the top and a long lower wick.

### Is a hammer automatically bullish?

No. It depends on support, volume, trend, and follow-through.

### Why do hammers fail?

They fail when support breaks, volume fades, or the broader trend remains weak.

### Where is a hammer more useful?

Near meaningful support or after a controlled pullback.

### What should traders review?

Review location, volume, wick low, entry timing, invalidation, and follow-through.

### Is a hammer the same as a bottoming tail?

They are related; bottoming tail is a broader lower-wick rejection concept.
