# Green-To-Red Move

This lesson is part of the **Candlestick Patterns In Context** course. It follows [Red-To-Green Move](/learn/candlestick-patterns/red-to-green-move/) and leads into [Chart Patterns](/learn/chart-patterns/).

A green-to-red move happens when a stock that was above its previous close or session reference moves back into negative territory. Traders often watch it as a sign of weakening intraday control.

The color change is not a complete thesis. It needs levels, volume, VWAP, catalyst, and risk context.

![Candlestick chart showing red-to-green and green-to-red intraday transitions with VWAP and volume context.](/images/learn/chart-reading/red-green-transition-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize the pattern without treating the name as a trade plan.
- Read the candle beside levels, volume, trend, and liquidity.
- Identify common failure conditions.
- Review entry, exit, and risk behavior after the trade.
- Use the pattern as context rather than prediction.

## Quick Definition

A green-to-red move is an intraday transition where price moves from positive territory into negative territory relative to a reference such as the prior close.

- Price starts above the reference.
- Price loses the reference area.
- Volume and level behavior matter.
- VWAP or prior close may be watched.
- The move can continue, reclaim, or chop.

The transition shows a change in session position, not guaranteed downside.

## What The Candle Shows

Green-to-red moves show price losing an intraday reference.

- Possible shift from strength to weakness.
- Possible failed morning move.
- Possible news fade context.
- Possible reclaim risk if price recovers.
- Possible chase risk after the transition.

## Context That Matters

This move needs level and reaction context.

- Prior close or session reference.
- VWAP and support areas.
- Volume during the loss.
- Nearby support or low of day.
- Spread and liquidity.
- Whether price reclaims the reference.

## When It Can Mislead

Green-to-red moves can mislead when traders assume losing the reference means clean continuation. Price can reclaim the level or chop around it.

## Realistic Example

A stock opens strong after news, fails near resistance, then loses the prior close with increasing volume. A review asks whether the catalyst was fading, whether support was nearby, and whether any entry was late after the clean loss.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating the color change as confirmation by itself.

Another mistake is ignoring nearby support.

Traders also make mistakes when they enter late after the move is extended.

Another mistake is ignoring a quick reclaim.

A final mistake is not checking catalyst quality.

## Practical Checklist

- The candle is identified correctly.
- The pattern formed near a meaningful area, not random noise.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- The broader trend and nearby levels are checked.
- Risk is defined without relying on the candle name.
- The next candles are reviewed for follow-through or failure.
- The trade can be reviewed honestly after completion.

## FAQ

### What is a green-to-red move?

It is when price moves from positive territory into negative territory relative to a session reference such as prior close.

### Is green-to-red a signal?

No. It is context and needs volume, levels, catalyst, and risk review.

### Why can it fail?

Price can reclaim the reference, hold support, or chop instead of continuing.

### What should traders review?

Review reference level, VWAP, support, volume, catalyst, reclaim behavior, and entry timing.

### How does it relate to news fades?

It can appear when an early news reaction loses strength, but the catalyst still needs review.

### How should it be used in review?

As a tag for session transition and behavior, not as a command.
