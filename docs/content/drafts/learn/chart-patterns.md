# Chart Patterns

This lesson is part of the **Chart Patterns In Context** course. It follows [Green-To-Red Move](/learn/candlestick-patterns/green-to-red-move/) and leads into [Bull Flag Pattern](/learn/chart-patterns/bull-flag/).

Chart patterns are recurring price structures traders use to organize what they see on a chart. They can help explain consolidation, pressure, rejection, continuation attempts, and failed moves.

The pattern name is not the edge. The useful part is the structure it gives to levels, risk, and review.

![Educational chart showing patterns as combinations of levels, trend, volume, and risk context.](/images/learn/chart-reading/chart-pattern-context-map.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize the pattern without treating the name as a trade plan.
- Identify the levels, trend, volume, and risk areas that give the pattern context.
- Understand common failure conditions.
- Review whether the trade was planned, late, or reactive.
- Use the pattern as a review framework rather than prediction.

## Quick Definition

A chart pattern is a recognizable price structure made from support, resistance, trend, candles, volume, and failed or successful attempts around levels.

- Patterns can describe continuation attempts.
- Patterns can describe reversal attempts.
- Patterns can describe compression or range behavior.
- Patterns can fail, fake out, or become choppy.
- Patterns should create review questions, not certainty.

A pattern is a map of behavior, not a promise.

## Pattern Structure

Most chart patterns are built from a few basic pieces.

- Support and resistance.
- Higher lows or lower highs.
- Range highs and range lows.
- Breakouts, breakdowns, reclaims, and rejections.
- Volume expansion or volume fade.
- Clear failure areas.

## Context That Matters

Pattern context matters more than the pattern name.

- Level quality.
- Trend before the pattern.
- Volume and relative volume.
- Liquidity, spread, and slippage.
- Catalyst or filing context.
- Distance from invalidation.

## When It Can Mislead

Chart patterns mislead when traders force a clean label onto messy price action or enter late because the pattern name sounds familiar.

## Realistic Example

A stock runs after news, consolidates under high of day, and holds higher lows. One trader calls it a bull flag, another calls it an ascending triangle. The useful review is not the label debate; it is whether the level, volume, entry, risk, and failure plan were clear.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating patterns as guarantees.

Another mistake is forcing a pattern onto a messy chart.

Traders also make mistakes when they enter after the pattern has already played out.

Another mistake is ignoring volume and liquidity.

A final mistake is holding after the pattern clearly fails.

## Practical Checklist

- The pattern is visible without forcing it.
- Key support and resistance areas are mapped.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- The entry is not far from a realistic risk area.
- The failure condition is defined before the trade.
- The broader catalyst or market context is checked.
- The completed trade can be reviewed honestly.

## Apply This In Review

- What pattern did I think I was trading?
- Was the pattern clean or forced?
- Where was the key level?
- What did volume do?
- Was my entry early, structured, or late?
- Where did the pattern fail or hold?
- Did I follow the plan after failure?

Chart-pattern review should separate structure quality from outcome.

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review completed trades by pattern label, level quality, volume behavior, entry timing, risk location, failed-pattern behavior, and repeated chase or hold patterns.

## FAQ

### What are chart patterns?

Chart patterns are recurring price structures used to organize market behavior.

### Do chart patterns always work?

No. They can fail, fake out, or become choppy.

### What matters most?

Level quality, volume, trend, risk, and execution matter more than the pattern name.

### Are patterns useful for small caps?

They can be, but float, liquidity, news, and filings matter.

### Should patterns be reviewed?

Yes. Review whether the pattern was clean and whether the trade followed a plan.

### Can patterns predict price?

No. They provide context, not prediction.

## Educational Disclaimer

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice. Chart patterns can support review, but they do not predict price, guarantee trading results, or replace risk management.
