# Cash Runway

This lesson is part of the **Small-Cap Stocks, Float And Dilution** course. It follows [Forward Split](/learn/forward-split/) and leads into [Going Concern](/learn/going-concern/).

The goal is not to make small-cap trading look easy or exciting. The goal is to understand the company, share structure, financing context, liquidity, and risk before treating any move as meaningful.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain the core idea behind Cash Runway.
- Identify the parts of the concept that matter most in small-cap stocks.
- Review the concept in context without assuming a guaranteed price reaction.
- Connect the concept to filings, float, dilution, liquidity, and trade risk.
- Use the lesson as part of a structured review process instead of a standalone trading signal.

Cash runway is an estimate of how long a company can keep operating before it may need more money, based on its cash balance and burn rate. Traders review cash runway because low cash can increase financing risk, especially in small-cap and biotech stocks.

Cash runway does not guarantee a company will raise money. It also does not guarantee dilution or a price move. It is a risk review tool that helps traders understand whether a company may need capital soon.

## Quick Definition

Cash runway estimates how much time a company has before its cash may run low.

A simple version looks at:

1. Cash on hand.
2. Operating losses.
3. Cash used in operations.
4. Quarterly burn rate.
5. Debt obligations.
6. Upcoming trial or product costs.
7. Financing history.
8. Available credit or funding sources.

For traders, the key question is whether the company has enough cash to fund its plans without raising more money soon.

## Why It Matters To Traders

Cash runway matters because companies with short runway may need to raise capital. That can lead to stock offerings, private placements, ATM sales, convertible notes, or other financing activity.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A stock may have exciting news, but if the company only has a few months of cash, the news may create an opportunity for financing.

A careful review asks:

1. How much cash does the company have?
2. How much cash is it using each quarter?
3. Is revenue covering expenses?
4. Are clinical trials or operations expensive?
5. Does the company have debt due?
6. Is there going concern language?
7. Is there an active shelf or ATM?
8. Has the company raised after news before?

Cash runway helps traders connect fundamentals with dilution risk.

## How It Works

A rough cash runway estimate compares cash to cash burn.

For example:

1. Cash on hand: $12 million.
2. Quarterly cash burn: $4 million.
3. Rough runway: about 3 quarters.

This is only an estimate. Real runway can change if expenses rise, revenue changes, debt comes due, or the company raises capital.

## Example Scenario

A biotech company reports $8 million in cash and used $6 million in operations last quarter. The company has a trial planned and no meaningful revenue.

A stronger review asks:

1. How many quarters of runway remain?
2. Is trial spending expected to increase?
3. Does the company have an active shelf registration?
4. Did the stock just move on clinical news?
5. Would a financing be expected by the market?
6. Is there going concern language in filings?

This helps traders understand why cash runway can matter even during a strong news move.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is looking only at cash without checking burn rate. A company with $20 million in cash may still have short runway if it burns $10 million per quarter.

Another mistake is assuming low cash always means immediate dilution. Timing is not guaranteed.

Traders also get into trouble when they ignore future spending. A company may need more cash for trials, manufacturing, launches, or acquisitions.

A fourth mistake is not checking whether the company already has an active financing tool.

## Review Questions

A structured trade review can help connect cash runway to trade decisions.

Useful review notes include:

3. Estimated runway.

## FAQ

### What is cash runway?

Cash runway estimates how long a company can operate before it may need more money, based on cash and burn rate.

### How do traders estimate cash runway?

Traders often compare cash on hand with quarterly cash burn, while also reviewing debt, revenue, expenses, and upcoming costs.

### Does short cash runway mean dilution is guaranteed?

No. Short runway increases financing risk but does not guarantee dilution or a specific financing event.

### Why is cash runway important for small-cap stocks?

Many small-cap companies need capital to fund operations, trials, or growth, so short runway can affect risk.

### Where can traders find cash runway information?

Traders often review Form 10-Q, Form 10-K, cash flow statements, management discussion, and company press releases.

### Can cash runway change quickly?

Yes. Runway can change after financing, revenue changes, cost increases, debt payments, or new spending plans.

## Apply This In Review

When reviewing a small-cap trade or watchlist idea connected to Cash Runway, ask:

- What did I know about the company, float, share structure, and filings before the move?
- Was the move driven by news, filings, volume, social attention, sector momentum, or thin liquidity?
- Did the concept actually matter in this case, or was I forcing it onto the chart?
- What changed after the move: volume, spread, dilution risk, offering risk, or liquidity?
- Did I account for the downside scenario, or did I focus only on the exciting version of the setup?
- What would I want to notice faster the next time this situation appears?
