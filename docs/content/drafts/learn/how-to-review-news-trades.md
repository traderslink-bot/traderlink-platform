# How To Review News Trades: Catalyst, Reaction, Risk, And Execution

Learning how to review news trades can help traders understand whether a trade was based on a real catalyst, a clean setup, and a defined plan, or whether it was just a reaction to a headline.

This lesson closes the **News, Catalysts And SEC Filings** course. It brings together [Stock Catalysts](/learn/stock-catalysts/), [Press Releases](/learn/press-releases/), [SEC Filings](/learn/sec-filings/), earnings, FDA news, clinical trial news, contract news, partnership news, and merger news.

A news trade should not be judged only by profit or loss. The better review looks at catalyst quality, details behind the news, chart reaction, volume, liquidity, risk, entry timing, trade management, and what should be improved next time.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Review a news-driven trade in a structured way.
- Separate catalyst quality from trade execution.
- Identify headline-chasing, late entries, and missed filing details.
- Review whether volume and price supported the move.
- Turn a news trade into a lesson for future decisions.

## Quick Definition

Reviewing a news trade means looking back at a trade connected to a company announcement, filing, press release, earnings report, FDA update, contract, partnership, merger, or other catalyst.

A good review asks:

- What was the news?
- Was the catalyst specific or vague?
- Did the stock already move before entry?
- Did volume confirm real attention?
- Did price hold key levels or fade?
- Was risk defined before entry?
- Was the exit based on the plan or emotion?
- What should be improved next time?

This is not about proving the trade was good or bad because of the outcome. It is about understanding the decision-making process.

## Step 1: Review The Catalyst

Start with the actual news. Read the full release, filing, or report.

Ask:

- What type of catalyst was it?
- Was the news new or recycled?
- Was it specific or vague?
- Were names, numbers, dates, or terms included?
- Was there risk language?
- Did related filings add important context?
- Was the catalyst expected or surprising?

This step tells you whether the trade was based on substance or headline emotion.

## Step 2: Review The Details Behind The Headline

The headline is rarely enough.

Review:

- Press release body.
- SEC filings.
- Exhibits.
- Offering terms.
- Contract or partnership terms.
- Trial data or endpoints.
- Earnings numbers and guidance.
- Cash runway and dilution risk.

Many weak news trades begin with a trader reacting before understanding the details.

## Step 3: Review The Chart Reaction

Next, review how the stock responded.

Ask:

- Did the stock gap, spike, fade, reclaim, or consolidate?
- Did volume expand?
- Did volume continue or dry up?
- Did price hold premarket highs, VWAP, support, or resistance?
- Did the move fail after the first reaction?
- Was the stock already extended before entry?

A strong headline with weak price action may still create a poor trade.

## Step 4: Review Liquidity And Execution

News trades can move quickly, and fast movement can hide execution problems.

Review:

- Spread at entry.
- Slippage.
- Fill quality.
- Level 2 or time-and-sales context if used.
- Whether position size matched liquidity.
- Whether the order type fit the conditions.

A trade can have a real catalyst but still be poorly executed.

## Step 5: Review Risk And Trade Management

A news trade still needs risk.

Ask:

- Was risk defined before entry?
- Where was the invalidation area?
- Did the trade size match the risk?
- Did the setup change after entry?
- Did the exit follow the plan?
- Did you stay after the reason for the trade failed?

This separates trade planning from reaction.

## Step 6: Write The Actual Lesson

The best review note is specific.

Instead of:

```text
Bad news trade.
```

Write:

```text
I entered after the third candle of a press-release spike without checking whether the release had financial terms. Volume faded on the breakout attempt and spread widened before my fill.
```

That note can change future behavior.

## Realistic Example

A company announces a partnership before the open. The stock gaps up and volume spikes. You enter after the stock breaks the premarket high, but it quickly fails and fades below VWAP.

A useful review asks:

- Did the partnership include a named partner?
- Were financial terms disclosed?
- Was there a related 8-K?
- Was the stock already extended from premarket lows?
- Did the breakout hold for more than one candle?
- Did volume increase or fade during the breakout attempt?
- Did the spread widen?
- Did the stop or exit plan make sense before entry?
- Did you stay in after the setup failed?

This turns the trade into a lesson instead of only a red or green result.

## Common Mistakes

Common news-trade review mistakes include:

- Reviewing only the headline.
- Ignoring related filings.
- Ignoring catalyst quality.
- Ignoring whether the move was already extended.
- Reviewing only profit or loss.
- Ignoring execution quality.
- Not recording the lesson soon enough.
- Calling a profitable chase a good process.
- Calling a planned loss a bad process.

Review should judge decision quality, not just outcome.

## Practical Checklist

Use this checklist after news trades:

- What was the exact catalyst?
- What type of news was it?
- Was the catalyst specific or vague?
- Did I read the full release or filing?
- What details mattered most?
- What details were missing?
- Did volume expand and continue?
- Did price hold, fade, reject, or reclaim key levels?
- Was liquidity clean enough for my size?
- Was risk defined before entry?
- Did the exit follow the plan?
- What is the specific lesson?

## FAQ

### What is a news trade?

A news trade is a trade connected to a company announcement, filing, press release, earnings report, regulatory update, contract, partnership, merger, or other catalyst.

### Should news trades be judged only by profit or loss?

No. Profit or loss matters, but review should also examine catalyst quality, entry timing, execution, risk, and whether the trade followed a plan.

### What is the most important part of reviewing a news trade?

The most important part is identifying whether the trade was based on a clear catalyst and planned setup or a fast reaction to a headline.

### Why do news trades often go wrong?

News trades often go wrong because traders react before reading details, enter after the move is extended, ignore filings, overlook liquidity, or trade without defined risk.

### How soon should traders review news trades?

Review them as soon as practical while the details, chart behavior, and decision process are still fresh.

## Educational Disclaimer

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice. Reviewing news trades does not guarantee future performance or profitable trades. Always review risk, liquidity, filings, news quality, and your own trading plan.
