# How To Use TradersLink Academy

This lesson is part of the **Trading Foundations** course. It follows [Welcome To TradersLink Academy](/learn/start-here/) and leads into [What Is A Stock And How Does A Trade Work?](/learn/what-is-a-stock-and-how-does-a-trade-work/).

TradersLink Academy is meant to feel like guided education without forcing users into a locked path. You can follow the recommended course order, jump to a specific lesson, or return to a lesson after a trade review exposes a gap.

The best way to use the Academy is to treat it as a living learning path: learn, apply, review, return, and keep building.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand the Academy navigation model.
- Use courses and lessons without feeling locked in.
- Know how previous and next lessons support learning flow.
- Use review prompts correctly.
- Know how glossary and related lessons support the main journey.

## Quick Definition

Using TradersLink Academy means moving through courses and lessons in a way that builds understanding over time.

- Course pages show the main path.
- Modules group related lessons.
- Previous and next links provide recommended flow.
- Related lessons support cross-topic learning.
- Glossary terms clarify vocabulary.
- Completion tracking helps users resume.
- Review prompts connect learning to completed trades.

The Academy is flexible. The order helps, but the user stays in control.

## Why It Matters

A strong learning system needs both structure and freedom. If everything is locked, users get frustrated. If everything is random, users get lost.

The Academy should give users enough direction to keep learning while allowing them to follow their immediate questions.

## Practical Learning Process

A simple Academy workflow is:

- Pick the recommended course if you are new.
- Read the lesson objective before the lesson.
- Use the lesson content and examples to learn the concept.
- Use the checklist to confirm the key idea.
- Use Apply This In Review after real or paper trades.
- Open related lessons when a term or concept needs more context.
- Mark the lesson complete and continue when ready.

This makes progress feel useful without turning the Academy into a test.

## Realistic Example

A user studies FOMO Trading after noticing late entries in their own trades. The related lessons point to Chasing Stocks, Overtrading, and Trading Discipline. The user can follow that psychology path, then return to Trade Review when they want to analyze repeated behavior.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is trying to finish everything quickly. The purpose is learning and return visits, not rushing through checkmarks.

Another mistake is reading setup lessons without the risk and review lessons that make them practical.

Users may also treat related lessons as distractions. They are support links, not required detours.

Another mistake is ignoring review prompts because they are not quizzes. Review prompts are where lessons connect to real decisions.

A final mistake is expecting the Academy to tell users what to trade. It should teach concepts and review process.

## Practical Checklist

- Choose a course or lesson based on your current need.
- Read the objective first.
- Use previous and next links when you want guided flow.
- Use related lessons when a concept needs support.
- Use glossary terms for definitions.
- Mark completion when the lesson is actually read.
- Return after trade review exposes a repeated gap.

## Apply This In Review

- Which course am I in?
- What lesson did I just complete?
- What concept still feels unclear?
- Which related lesson would help?
- What real trade or example connects to this lesson?
- What should I read next?

The Academy is strongest when users move between learning and review instead of only consuming lessons.

## Trader Intelligence Bridge

Trader Intelligence can later help users see which Academy concepts are showing up in their completed trades. If a user repeatedly tags late entries, the Academy can support that review with FOMO, chasing, execution, and risk lessons.

## FAQ

### Do I have to follow the Academy in order?

No. The order is recommended, but users should be able to move freely.

### What is the best way to start?

Start with Trading Foundations if you are new, then move into chart reading, volume, news, risk, psychology, and review.

### Are there quizzes?

The current plan focuses on lessons, progress, checklists, and review prompts rather than required quizzes.

### What are related lessons for?

They help users connect topics across courses when a concept needs more context.

### Should I use review prompts after every lesson?

Use them when you have a real or practice trade that connects to the lesson.

### Does completion mean I am ready to trade?

No. Completion means educational progress, not trading readiness or guaranteed skill.
