# Execution Review

This lesson is part of the **Trade Review And Improvement** course. It follows [Planned Vs Actual Trade Review](/learn/planned-vs-actual-trade-review/) and leads into [Mistake Pattern Review](/learn/mistake-pattern-review/).

Execution review studies how the trade was actually entered, filled, managed, and exited. It asks whether the trader got into the trade cleanly, whether the order choice fit the conditions, and whether spread, slippage, or timing changed the result.

A good idea can still be executed poorly. A poor idea can sometimes get a lucky fill. Execution review keeps those two things separate.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what execution review covers.
- Review entry timing, fill quality, slippage, spread, and order type.
- Separate setup quality from execution quality.
- Identify repeated execution mistakes.
- Connect execution review to liquidity and order-flow lessons.

## Quick Definition

Execution review means studying how orders were placed and filled, and whether entry and exit decisions matched the conditions.

- Entry timing.
- Order type.
- Fill price versus expected price.
- Spread at entry and exit.
- Slippage.
- Liquidity and volume context.
- Exit timing and fill quality.
- Whether execution matched the plan.

The goal is to learn whether the trade was handled cleanly, not to expect perfect fills.

## Why It Matters

Execution matters because small differences can become large over repeated trades. Wide spreads, late entries, marketable orders in thin liquidity, and rushed exits can change the real risk of a trade.

Execution review is especially important for small-cap, news-driven, fast-moving, or low-liquidity stocks.

## Practical Review Process

A practical execution review checks:

- What price was expected before entry?
- What price was actually filled?
- What was the spread?
- Was the order type appropriate for the conditions?
- Was there slippage?
- Did the trade move before the order was filled?
- Was the exit planned or rushed?
- Did execution issues repeat across trades?

This review should be factual. The trader is not trying to make every fill perfect. They are trying to understand recurring execution cost.

## Realistic Example

A trader plans an entry near .00 with a tight spread. The stock starts moving quickly after a news headline. The trader sends a marketable order and gets filled at .12 while the spread widens.

The trade later pulls back to .95. The setup may still be worth reviewing, but execution changed the risk. The real entry was not near the planned level, and slippage made the stop distance larger.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is blaming the setup when the real issue was a poor fill.

Another mistake is ignoring spread because the chart looked clean.

Traders also make mistakes when they use the same order style in every liquidity condition.

Another mistake is reviewing entry but not exit. Exit slippage can matter too.

A final mistake is not tracking repeated execution issues by ticker type, time of day, or liquidity level.

## Practical Checklist

- Expected entry and actual fill are compared.
- Spread was recorded or estimated.
- Slippage was reviewed.
- Order type was reviewed in context.
- Liquidity and volume were considered.
- Exit fill quality was reviewed.
- Repeated execution issues are tagged.

## Apply This In Review

- What price did I expect?
- What price did I get?
- What was the spread?
- Did slippage change the trade?
- Was my order type appropriate?
- Was the stock liquid enough for my size?
- Did I enter late because of urgency?
- Did my exit execution follow the plan?

Execution review helps traders see whether their process works in the actual market conditions they trade.

## Trader Intelligence Bridge

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review execution quality after trades: entry timing, expected versus actual fills, spread, slippage, exit quality, and repeated conditions where execution gets worse.

For execution review, the value is not prediction. It is better visibility into how trades were actually handled.

## FAQ

### What is execution review?

Execution review studies entries, exits, fills, slippage, spread, order type, and whether the trade was handled according to the plan.

### Why does execution review matter?

Because poor fills, wide spreads, and rushed orders can change the real risk and result of a trade.

### Is execution review only for losing trades?

No. Winning trades can still have poor execution that should be reviewed.

### What should I track for execution?

Track expected entry, actual fill, spread, slippage, order type, liquidity, exit fill, and whether timing matched the plan.

### Can execution be perfect?

No. The goal is not perfection. The goal is to understand recurring execution cost and avoid preventable mistakes.

### How does execution connect to liquidity?

Thin liquidity and wide spreads can make fills worse, especially when size is too large or price is moving quickly.
