# EFFECT Notice

This lesson is part of the **News, Catalysts And SEC Filings** course. It follows [Form 424B4](/learn/sec-filings/form-424b4/) and leads into [Form 3](/learn/sec-filings/form-3/).

The goal is not to turn EFFECT Notice into a trading signal. The goal is to understand what the filing says, what it does not say, and how it changes the review context around news, price, volume, liquidity, and risk.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what EFFECT Notice is used for.
- Identify the parts of the filing that matter most for traders.
- Review registration effectiveness related registration statements resale eligibility and timing context without assuming a guaranteed market reaction.
- Connect the filing to related news, chart reaction, liquidity, and risk context.
- Use the filing as part of a structured review process instead of a standalone prediction tool.

An EFFECT notice is an SEC notice showing that a registration statement has become effective. Traders often watch EFFECT notices because effectiveness can make registered securities eligible for an offering or resale process, depending on the filing involved.

An EFFECT notice does not automatically mean shares are being sold immediately. It also does not guarantee dilution or a price move. The notice should be reviewed with the related registration statement, prospectus, selling shareholder table, and any later offering documents.

## What This Filing Means

An EFFECT notice means the SEC has declared a registration statement effective. In simple terms, the registration statement has cleared an important procedural step.

An EFFECT notice may relate to filings such as:

1. Form S-1.
2. Form S-3.
3. Form F-1.
4. Form F-3.
5. Form S-4.
6. Resale registration statements.
7. Public offering registration statements.
8. Shelf registration statements.

The notice itself is usually short. The real details are in the registration statement and prospectus documents.

## Why Companies Receive It

Companies receive an EFFECT notice after the SEC declares a registration statement effective. This can allow the registered securities process to move forward.

Depending on the filing, effectiveness may relate to:

1. IPO securities.
2. Public offering shares.
3. Resale shares.
4. Merger securities.
5. Shelf registration securities.
6. Securities from a prior private placement.

For traders, the key question is what registration statement became effective and what securities it covers.

## What Traders Usually Look For

Traders often review EFFECT notices for timing and supply context.

Important areas include:

1. The related registration statement.
2. Securities covered.
3. Selling shareholders.
4. Whether the company receives proceeds.
5. Whether the filing is primary or resale.
6. Whether a prospectus supplement follows.
7. Share count involved.
8. Lock-up or restriction language.
9. Prior private placement terms.
10. Potential resale pressure.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They see the word effective and assume immediate selling, but the context can vary.

## What It Does Not Automatically Mean

An EFFECT notice does not automatically mean an offering happens that day. It also does not automatically mean every registered share will be sold immediately.

It means the registration statement is effective. What happens next depends on the structure, holders, market conditions, and any additional filing requirements.

## Dilution Or Risk Considerations

An EFFECT notice can matter for dilution and resale risk because it may make registered securities available for sale or resale.

Traders may want to check:

1. Whether shares are newly issued or resale shares.
2. Whether warrants are involved.
3. Whether selling shareholders are named.
4. Whether the registration follows a private placement.
5. Whether the company receives proceeds.
6. Whether the share count is large relative to float.
7. Whether a later 424B filing adds terms.

The notice does not guarantee dilution pressure, but it can mark an important point in the registration timeline.

## Practical Checklist

Structured review notes can help connect EFFECT notice details to trading decisions.

Useful review notes include:

1. EFFECT notice date.
2. Related registration statement.
3. Securities covered.
4. Primary or resale structure.
5. Selling shareholders, if any.
6. Float and share count context.
7. Related 424B filings.
8. Stock reaction after effectiveness.

This helps traders avoid reacting to the notice without reading the underlying filing.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes with EFFECT Notice include:

- Reacting to the form name without reading the filing details.
- Assuming the filing is automatically bullish or bearish.
- Ignoring related filings, exhibits, amendments, or prospectus documents.
- Forgetting to compare the filing with price, volume, spread, and liquidity.
- Treating social-media summaries as enough without checking the source document.
- Failing to review what happened after the filing instead of only what the headline suggested.

## FAQ

### What is an EFFECT notice?

An EFFECT notice is an SEC notice showing that a registration statement has been declared effective.

### Does an EFFECT notice mean dilution is immediate?

No. It does not automatically mean immediate dilution. Traders need to review what securities were registered and whether selling or offering activity follows.

### Why do traders watch EFFECT notices?

Traders watch them because effectiveness can be an important step in the offering or resale registration process.

### What filing should traders read with an EFFECT notice?

Traders should read the related registration statement, prospectus, selling shareholder table, and any later 424B filings.

### Can an EFFECT notice affect stock price?

It can, but it does not guarantee a move. The reaction depends on the registration details, expectations, float, liquidity, and market context.

### Is an EFFECT notice the same as an offering?

No. It means the registration statement is effective. An actual offering or resale activity depends on the filing structure and follow-up actions.

## Apply This In Review

When reviewing a trade or watchlist idea connected to EFFECT Notice, ask:

- What exact filing was released, and what event or disclosure did it contain?
- Was the market reacting to the filing itself, a related press release, or a prior expectation?
- What did price and volume do before, during, and after the filing became visible?
- Did liquidity, spread, or volatility make the move harder to execute cleanly?
- Did I read the filing details, or did I rely on a headline, scanner alert, or social post?
- What would I want to notice faster the next time this filing appears?
