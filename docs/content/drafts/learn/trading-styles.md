# Trading Styles Overview

This lesson is part of the **Trading Styles And Playbooks** course. It follows [VWAP Reclaim](/learn/chart-patterns/vwap-reclaim/) and leads into [Day Trading](/learn/day-trading/).

A trading style is a way to organize timeframe, setup type, risk, execution speed, and review. It is not an identity and it is not a promise that one approach is better than another.

This course helps traders understand styles as playbooks. A playbook gives structure to what the trader is looking for, what can go wrong, and how the result should be reviewed.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what a trading style is.
- Compare day trading, swing trading, scalping, momentum, pullbacks, fades, and multi-day context.
- Understand why style affects risk and review.
- Avoid jumping between styles without a plan.
- Use styles as review categories rather than guaranteed edges.

## Quick Definition

A trading style is a repeatable way to define trade timeframe, setup type, risk rules, and review criteria.

- Day trading closes positions during the same session.
- Swing trading holds across multiple sessions.
- Scalping focuses on very short holding periods and execution quality.
- Momentum trading focuses on strong movement and participation.
- Pullback setups review controlled pauses after a move.
- News fades and sell-the-news ideas review reaction quality after catalysts.
- Multi-day runner context reviews continuation risk and exhaustion over several sessions.

The style should shape the plan before the trade and the review after the trade.

## How This Style Works

Trading styles work best when they are connected to concrete process:

- What market condition the trader is watching.
- What setup category is being reviewed.
- What timeframe the trade belongs to.
- What invalidates the idea.
- What execution quality matters.
- What mistake patterns commonly appear in that style.

## What This Style Requires

A style requires rules because each style creates different pressure.

- Day trading requires intraday focus and fast risk control.
- Swing trading requires overnight and news-risk planning.
- Scalping requires execution discipline and liquidity awareness.
- Momentum trading requires chase-risk awareness.
- Pullbacks require patience and level context.
- News fades require catalyst and reaction review.
- Multi-day context requires supply, volume, and exhaustion review.

## When It Can Mislead

Trading styles can mislead when traders use the label to justify behavior. Calling something momentum does not fix a late entry. Calling something swing trading does not make holding a failed day trade acceptable.

## Realistic Example

A trader starts the morning looking for a planned intraday momentum trade, but misses the clean entry. Instead of labeling the next late entry as momentum, the trader reviews whether the setup is still structured. Later, a different stock pulls back calmly into a known level with volume cooling. That belongs in a different playbook and needs different review questions.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is switching styles mid-trade because the first plan failed.

Another mistake is choosing a style because it sounds exciting instead of because it fits schedule, risk tolerance, and review ability.

Traders also make mistakes when they treat style names as trade signals.

Another mistake is using day-trading risk on swing trades or swing-trading patience on failed day trades.

A final mistake is never tracking which style produces the trader's repeated mistakes.

## Practical Checklist

- I know the style before the trade.
- The timeframe matches the plan.
- The setup category is clear.
- Risk rules match the style.
- Liquidity and execution needs are understood.
- I know what invalidates the idea.
- I can review the trade under the correct playbook.

## Apply This In Review

- What style was this trade supposed to be?
- Did the timeframe change after entry?
- Did I switch styles to avoid taking a loss?
- Did the setup match the style label?
- Which mistake patterns appeared?
- Did the style fit my available attention and risk?
- Which style should I study next?

A style is useful only if it makes the plan and review clearer.

## FAQ

### What are trading styles?

Trading styles are ways to organize timeframe, setup type, risk rules, and review criteria.

### Is one trading style best?

No. Different styles require different skills, schedules, risk controls, and review habits.

### Can traders use more than one style?

Yes, but mixing styles without clear rules can create confusion and style drift.

### What is a trading playbook?

A playbook is a repeatable structure for a type of trade, including setup context, risk, execution, and review questions.

### Why does style matter for review?

The same mistake can look different in day trading, swing trading, scalping, or momentum trading, so the review should match the style.

### Should beginners choose a style right away?

Beginners can study several styles first, then narrow focus based on schedule, risk, attention, and review results.
