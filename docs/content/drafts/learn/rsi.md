# RSI

This lesson is part of the **Technical Indicators And Tools** course. It follows [Anchored VWAP](/learn/anchored-vwap/) and leads into [MACD](/learn/macd/).

RSI stands for Relative Strength Index. It is a momentum oscillator that compares recent gains and losses to show whether momentum has been strong, weak, stretched, or fading.

RSI is often described with overbought and oversold labels, but those labels are not trading instructions.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what RSI measures.
- Understand overbought and oversold as context, not commands.
- Review RSI with trend, levels, and volume.
- Recognize divergence without treating it as certainty.
- Avoid using RSI to fight strong price movement without risk.

## Quick Definition

RSI is a momentum indicator that compares recent upward and downward price movement over a selected period.

- Higher RSI can show stronger recent upward momentum.
- Lower RSI can show stronger recent downward momentum.
- Elevated RSI can show extension or strength.
- Depressed RSI can show weakness or possible exhaustion.
- Divergence can show momentum changing before price fully confirms.

RSI describes momentum context. It does not guarantee reversal or continuation.

## What It Measures

RSI measures the speed and size of recent price changes. It does not measure catalyst quality, liquidity, float, spread, or whether a trade has a defined invalidation point.

RSI is commonly calculated from average gains and average losses and normalized on a 0 to 100 scale, with 14 periods often used as the default setting. Overbought and oversold thresholds are conventional context labels, not guaranteed reversal points, and platform values can vary with smoothing, lookback, and data history.

## How Traders Use It As Context

Traders may use RSI to review:

- Whether momentum was expanding or fading.
- Whether price was extended from recent movement.
- Whether a pullback relieved momentum.
- Whether divergence appeared near a meaningful level.
- Whether the trader exited too early because RSI looked high.

## When It Can Mislead

RSI can mislead when traders assume overbought means an immediate drop or oversold means an immediate bounce. In strong trends, RSI can stay stretched while price continues.

## Realistic Example

A stock breaks above resistance on strong volume and RSI becomes elevated. A trader exits immediately because RSI is overbought, but price holds the breakout and continues trending. The review lesson is that RSI showed strong momentum, and the trader used it as an exit command without checking price structure.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating overbought or oversold as automatic entry or exit instructions.

Another mistake is shorting strength only because RSI is high.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore the trend and nearby levels.

Another mistake is treating divergence as a guaranteed reversal.

A final mistake is using RSI to justify an entry without defined risk.

## Practical Checklist

- I know what RSI was showing about momentum.
- I checked whether the market was trending or ranging.
- I reviewed support and resistance.
- I checked volume and catalyst context.
- I did not treat overbought or oversold as a command.
- I had a risk plan independent of RSI.
- I reviewed whether RSI helped or hurt the decision.

## Apply This In Review

- What was RSI showing before entry?
- Was price trending or ranging?
- Was RSI elevated because momentum was strong?
- Did I exit early because RSI looked high?
- Did I enter against trend because RSI looked stretched?
- Did divergence appear near a real level?
- Did RSI improve the trade review?

RSI review should explain how momentum affected the decision, not pretend the indicator knew the future.

## FAQ

### What is RSI?

RSI, or Relative Strength Index, is a momentum indicator that compares recent gains and losses.

### Does overbought mean sell?

No. Overbought is a context label and does not guarantee reversal.

### Does oversold mean buy?

No. Oversold can show weakness or extension, but price can continue lower.

### What is RSI divergence?

Divergence happens when RSI and price move differently, which may show momentum changing but does not guarantee a reversal.

### When can RSI mislead?

RSI can mislead in strong trends, weak trends, or when used without levels, volume, and risk.

### How should RSI be reviewed?

Review whether RSI showed momentum context, whether price structure agreed, and whether the trader used RSI responsibly.
