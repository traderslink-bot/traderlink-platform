# Multi-Day Runner

This lesson is part of the **Trading Styles And Playbooks** course. It follows [Sell The News](/learn/sell-the-news/) and leads into [Chasing Stocks](/learn/chasing-stocks/).

A multi-day runner is a stock that keeps attracting attention across more than one session. It may be driven by news, low float, short interest, sector attention, unusual volume, or repeated intraday continuation attempts.

The phrase can create excitement, but the Academy approach is sober: multi-day runners are volatile contexts to review, not continuation setups with guaranteed outcomes.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define multi-day runners.
- Understand attention, volume, float, and catalyst context.
- Review continuation versus exhaustion.
- Recognize chase risk after several sessions.
- Connect multi-day moves to supply and risk.

## Quick Definition

A multi-day runner is a stock that continues drawing price movement, volume, and trader attention over multiple sessions.

- The move may start with a catalyst or volume event.
- Attention can build across sessions.
- Low float or supply constraints may increase volatility.
- Dilution, offerings, or filings can change context.
- Late-stage continuation can become crowded and risky.

A runner describes a historical move and current attention. It does not guarantee the next session.

## How This Style Works

Multi-day runner review usually includes:

- Original catalyst and whether it still matters.
- Volume trend across sessions.
- Float, dilution, and offering risk.
- Key daily and intraday levels.
- Whether price is extended from support.
- Signs of exhaustion, failed breakouts, or lower highs.

## What This Style Requires

This context requires extra risk awareness.

- Catalyst and filing review.
- Volume and relative volume review.
- Float and supply context.
- Daily chart level mapping.
- Position size matched to volatility.
- A plan for halts, gaps, and failed continuation.

## When It Can Mislead

Multi-day runners can mislead when traders assume yesterday's strength should continue today. Attention can reverse quickly, especially when the move is crowded or supply appears.

## Realistic Example

A low-float stock runs for two sessions after news and holds high volume. On the third day, it opens strong but fails near a prior high and volume becomes erratic. A review should ask whether the runner still had clean continuation structure or whether the trade became a late chase into exhaustion.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is entering late because the stock has already run for several days.

Another mistake is ignoring dilution or offering filings.

Traders also make mistakes when they use yesterday's strength as today's plan.

Another mistake is oversizing because the stock feels popular.

A final mistake is holding through failure because of runner hype.

## Practical Checklist

- The original catalyst is understood.
- Volume across sessions is reviewed.
- Float and supply risks are checked.
- Daily and intraday levels are mapped.
- Extension from support is considered.
- Halt and gap risk are respected.
- The trade has a clear failure plan.

## Apply This In Review

- What started the multi-day move?
- Is the catalyst still relevant?
- What did volume do across sessions?
- Were there dilution or filing risks?
- Was my entry early, structured, or late?
- Did I chase because of attention?
- Did the stock show continuation or exhaustion?

Multi-day runner review should measure context, not excitement.

## FAQ

### What is a multi-day runner?

A multi-day runner is a stock that continues attracting price movement, volume, and attention across multiple sessions.

### Do multi-day runners keep going?

Not always. They can continue, chop, fade, halt, or reverse sharply.

### Why do multi-day runners happen?

They may be driven by catalysts, low float, short interest, sector attention, unusual volume, or repeated trader interest.

### What risks matter?

Extension, crowded attention, dilution, offering filings, halts, gaps, spread, and liquidity all matter.

### How should runners be reviewed?

Review catalyst, volume trend, float, supply risk, levels, extension, entry timing, and exhaustion signs.

### Is multi-day runner a signal?

No. It is a context label for review, not a trade instruction.
