# Candlestick Pattern Basics: How Traders Read Candle Behavior

Candlesticks turn a period of trading into one visual.

Each candle shows four prices:

* **Open:** where the candle period started
* **High:** the highest price reached during the period
* **Low:** the lowest price reached during the period
* **Close:** where the candle period finished

Those four prices create the body and the wicks. The body shows the distance between open and close. The wicks show where price traveled before finishing somewhere else.

![Educational candlestick chart showing candle anatomy, wick, body, support, resistance, and volume context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/candlestick-anatomy-context.svg)

## Body

The body is the area between the open and close.

A large body shows that price moved with direction during that candle. A small body shows that buyers and sellers finished closer to balance.

For example, a candle that opens near the low and closes near the high shows strong buyer control inside that candle. A candle that opens near the high and closes near the low shows strong seller control inside that candle.

## Wick

The wick shows where price traveled but did not close.

An upper wick shows that price traded higher than the body and then moved back down before the close.

A lower wick shows that price traded lower than the body and then moved back up before the close.

Long wicks are important because they make rejection behavior visible.

## Range

The candle range is the distance from high to low.

A wide-range candle shows expansion. A narrow-range candle shows compression or quiet movement.

Range becomes more useful when compared with the candles around it.

## Close Location

Close location means where the candle closed inside its full range.

A close near the high shows a different behavior than a close near the low, even if the candle has the same high and low.

Beginners should look at where the candle finished before worrying about the candle name.

## Candle Color

A green candle usually means the close was above the open.

A red candle usually means the close was below the open.

Color helps, but shape matters too. A green candle with a large upper wick shows a different behavior than a green candle with a large body and almost no wick.

## Timeframe

A candle only describes the timeframe being viewed.

A one-minute candle, a five-minute candle, and a daily candle can all show different versions of the same stock. Always know the timeframe before reading the candle.

## Volume

Volume helps show participation.

A strong candle with higher volume shows a different behavior than the same candle shape on quiet volume.

Volume is especially useful when studying breakout candles, breakdown candles, high-volume green candles, and high-volume red candles.

## Location

The same candle can matter more or less depending on where it forms.

A long lower wick near support is different from a long lower wick in the middle of a messy range. A large green candle breaking a level is different from a large green candle running into resistance.

The candle shows behavior. The chart location explains why that behavior is worth studying.

## Single Candle Versus Multi-Candle Behavior

Some lessons focus on one candle shape, like a hammer, shooting star, doji, spinning top, marubozu, or high-wave candle.

Other lessons require more than one candle, like engulfing patterns, inside bars, outside bars, morning stars, evening stars, rising three methods, falling three methods, and session color shifts.

The category lessons separate those ideas so the visual shape is easier to understand.

## What To Watch On Any Candle

Before naming a candle, ask:

* What is the body size?
* Where are the wicks?
* Where did the candle close inside its range?
* Is the candle wide or narrow compared with recent candles?
* Did volume expand or fade?
* What level or session reference area was nearby?

## Key Takeaway

Candlestick patterns are easier to understand when they are grouped by behavior.

Learn the candle parts first. Then use the five candle behavior categories to organize the names.

## FAQ

### What are candlestick patterns?

Candlestick patterns are candle shapes or groups of candles used to organize price behavior.

### What does a candlestick show?

A candlestick shows the open, high, low, and close for a specific period of time.

### What matters most when reading candles?

Body, wick, range, close location, timeframe, volume, and chart location matter more than the candle name alone.

### Are red-to-green and green-to-red moves candlestick patterns?

No. They are session behaviors. They belong with gap and session behavior because they depend on price moving around a session reference area.
