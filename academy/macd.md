# MACD

MACD stands for Moving Average Convergence Divergence. It is a momentum and trend tool built from moving averages, which means it can help describe changing momentum but also carries lag.

Many traders notice MACD crosses, but a cross by itself is not a trade plan.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what MACD measures.
- Understand MACD line, signal line, and histogram at a practical level.
- Recognize that MACD is based on moving averages and can lag.
- Review MACD crosses without treating them as commands.
- Use MACD as momentum context, not prediction.

## Quick Definition

MACD is an indicator that compares moving averages to show changes in momentum.

- The MACD line reflects the difference between moving averages.
- The signal line smooths the MACD line.
- The histogram shows distance between MACD and signal line.
- Crosses can show momentum shifts.
- Histogram expansion or contraction can show momentum strengthening or fading.

MACD is a momentum context tool, not a guaranteed timing system.

## What It Measures

MACD measures the relationship between moving averages. Because it is built from averages, it usually reacts after price has already started changing.

Standard MACD is commonly built from the difference between 12-period and 26-period exponential moving averages, with a 9-period EMA of the MACD line used as the signal line. Those are common defaults, not universal rules, and different settings can change sensitivity and lag.

## How Traders Use It As Context

Traders may use MACD to review:

- Whether momentum was increasing or fading.
- Whether a trend was strengthening or weakening.
- Whether a cross occurred near a meaningful level.
- Whether a histogram shift appeared before or after the best entry.
- Whether waiting for MACD made the trade late.

## When It Can Mislead

MACD can mislead in choppy markets where repeated crosses happen with little follow-through. It can also confirm a move after price has already traveled far from a clean risk area.

## Realistic Example

A stock breaks above a range and runs for several candles. MACD crosses later, after price is already extended near resistance. A trader enters because of the cross and has poor risk. The review lesson is that MACD confirmed momentum after the move, but the entry location was late.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating MACD crosses as automatic trade signals.

Another mistake is ignoring lag because MACD looks clean after a move.

Traders also make mistakes when they enter into resistance because MACD is positive.

Another mistake is using MACD in choppy price action without recognizing repeated false crosses.

A final mistake is not reviewing whether MACD improved timing or made the trader late.

## FAQ

### What is MACD?

MACD is a momentum indicator based on moving averages that shows changes in trend and momentum.

### What does a MACD cross mean?

A cross can show a momentum shift, but it is not an automatic trade signal.

### Does MACD lag?

Yes. MACD is based on moving averages, so it often reacts after price has already moved.

### What is the MACD histogram?

The histogram shows the distance between the MACD line and signal line.

### When can MACD mislead?

MACD can mislead in choppy markets or when confirmation appears after the clean risk area is gone.

### How should MACD be reviewed?

Review whether MACD clarified momentum, conflicted with price, or caused a late entry.
