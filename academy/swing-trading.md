# Swing Trading

This lesson is part of the **Trading Styles And Playbooks** course. It follows [Day Trading](/academy/day-trading/) and leads into [Scalping Stocks](/academy/scalping-stocks/).

Swing trading means holding a trade across more than one session. The trade may last days or weeks depending on the plan, but the defining feature is exposure beyond the current trading day.

Swing trading gives trades more time to develop, but it also adds overnight, gap, news, and position-management risk.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define swing trading as a trading style.
- Understand overnight and multi-day risk.
- Separate swing plans from failed day trades.
- Review position management across sessions.
- Connect swing trading to catalysts, levels, and risk.

## Quick Definition

Swing trading is a style where positions are held across multiple sessions to participate in a larger move or thesis.

- Trades may last several days or longer.
- Overnight gaps can affect risk.
- News and filings can change context while the trader is holding.
- Position size often needs to account for wider movement.
- The plan should define why the trade is held beyond one session.

A swing trade should be planned as a swing trade before the hold, not renamed after a day trade fails.

Overnight exposure can change the risk profile because news, gaps, lower-liquidity periods, wider spreads, and uncertain execution can affect the position while the trader is not actively managing it. Swing trading should be reviewed as a planned hold, not as a way to avoid intraday discipline.

## How This Style Works

Swing trading usually depends on broader context:

- Daily chart levels and trend structure.
- Catalyst quality and filing context.
- Volume and liquidity over multiple sessions.
- Risk level, invalidation, and expected hold plan.
- Overnight and weekend exposure review.
- Rules for adding, reducing, or exiting.

## What This Style Requires

Swing trading requires patience and risk tolerance for gaps.

- A written hold plan.
- Position size that respects overnight risk.
- Awareness of earnings, filings, offerings, and company events.
- Defined invalidation rather than hope.
- Review of whether the thesis remains valid.
- Willingness to exit when the original idea fails.

## When It Can Mislead

Swing trading can mislead when traders use longer timeframes to avoid admitting a failed intraday idea. Holding longer does not make a weak plan stronger.

## Realistic Example

A trader plans a swing trade after a stock holds a higher low on the daily chart and has a clear catalyst. The trader sizes smaller than a day trade because overnight risk matters. During review, the trader checks whether the hold followed the plan or whether new news changed the context.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is turning a failed day trade into a swing trade.

Another mistake is using day-trade size for overnight exposure.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore earnings, filings, or offering risk.

Another mistake is holding after the thesis changes.

A final mistake is not reviewing adds, reductions, and overnight decisions separately.

## Practical Checklist

- The trade was planned as a swing before holding.
- Daily chart context is clear.
- Catalyst and filing risk are reviewed.
- Position size accounts for overnight risk.
- Invalidation is defined.
- News calendar and company events are checked.
- The review will track each hold decision.

## Apply This In Review

- Was this planned as a swing trade from the start?
- What was the multi-day thesis?
- What invalidated the idea?
- Did overnight news or gaps affect the trade?
- Did I add, reduce, or hold according to plan?
- Did I use too much size for the timeframe?
- What did I learn about my swing-trade behavior?

Swing review should include every decision made while the position was open, not only entry and exit.

## FAQ

### What is swing trading?

Swing trading is holding trades across multiple sessions according to a planned thesis and risk framework.

### How is swing trading different from day trading?

Day trades close during the same session, while swing trades carry overnight and multi-day exposure.

### What risks matter in swing trading?

Overnight gaps, news, filings, liquidity, position size, and thesis changes all matter.

### Can a failed day trade become a swing trade?

Changing the label after a failed day trade is a common style-drift mistake.

### What should swing traders review?

Review the thesis, hold decisions, overnight risk, adds, reductions, exits, and whether new information changed the setup.

### Does swing trading reduce risk?

No. It changes the type of risk and requires its own planning.
