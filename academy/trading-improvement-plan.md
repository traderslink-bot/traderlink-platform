# Trading Improvement Plan

This lesson completes the **Practice And Improvement** course. It follows [Forward Testing Trading](/academy/forward-testing-trading/) and leads into [Trading Halts](/academy/trading-halts/) when the Academy continues into high-volatility event risk.

A trading improvement plan turns review into action. It connects what the trader notices after trades with what they will practice next.

The plan does not need to be complicated. It needs to be specific enough to repeat.

An improvement plan should define what counts as better process, not promise better market outcomes.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Build a practical trading improvement plan.
- Choose one priority behavior or skill.
- Connect review notes to practice drills.
- Use samples instead of emotional conclusions.
- Keep improvement realistic and non-hype.

## Quick Definition

A trading improvement plan is a written plan for what the trader will practice, track, and review next.

It can include:

- Current issue or skill focus.
- Evidence from recent reviews.
- Practice method.
- Rule or checklist change.
- Forward-test sample.
- Review schedule.
- Success criteria based on process.

The plan should guide the next learning cycle.

## The Improvement Plan Structure

A simple structure:

- Pattern: what repeated issue or skill matters most?
- Evidence: where did it show up?
- Practice: what drill or method will train it?
- Rule: what specific behavior will change?
- Sample: how many sessions or examples will be reviewed?
- Review: when will the plan be assessed?

This keeps the plan practical.

## Choose One Priority

Improvement slows down when the trader tries to fix everything at once.

Examples of one-priority plans:

- Reduce late entries after scanner alerts.
- Improve risk definition before decisions.
- Stop trading low-volume midday ranges.
- Review every trade with a process grade.
- Capture screenshots before the outcome is obvious.

One priority creates cleaner feedback.

## Realistic Example

A trader reviews 20 trades and finds that the weakest grades come from reactive trades not on the watchlist. The improvement plan becomes:

- Pattern: reactive ticker additions.
- Evidence: 7 of 20 trades came from unplanned scanner alerts.
- Practice: watchlist review plus one-rule drill.
- Rule: no new ticker without catalyst, spread, liquidity, and level notes.
- Sample: next 10 sessions.
- Review: compare planned versus reactive trade grades.

That is specific enough to test.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is writing a motivational plan instead of a reviewable plan.

Another mistake is changing the plan every day.

Traders also make mistakes when they measure only outcome and ignore process.

Another mistake is choosing too many focus areas.

A final mistake is skipping the review date.

## Practical Checklist

- The plan names one priority.
- The priority comes from review evidence.
- The practice method is clear.
- The rule or behavior change is observable.
- The sample size or time window is defined.
- Success is measured by process quality.
- The plan has a review date.

## Apply This In Review

- What repeated pattern matters most right now?
- What evidence supports that priority?
- What practice method fits it?
- What rule will I test?
- How many examples will I collect?
- What would show process improvement?
- When will I review the plan?

## FAQ

### What is a trading improvement plan?

A trading improvement plan is a written plan for what the trader will practice, track, and review based on recent evidence.

### How many things should an improvement plan focus on?

Usually one main priority is best, especially when the goal is behavior change.

### Should improvement be measured only by profit and loss?

No. Process quality, rule adherence, risk control, and execution behavior can be more useful for learning.

### How often should the plan be reviewed?

Use a defined sample or time window, such as a number of sessions or trades, instead of changing the plan after every result.
