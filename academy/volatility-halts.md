# Volatility Halts

This lesson is part of the **Halts And High-Volatility Events** course. It follows [Trading Halts](/academy/trading-halts/) and leads into [Halt Resume](/academy/halt-resume/).

A volatility halt is a pause caused by extreme or rapid price movement under market structure rules. Traders often encounter this concept in fast-moving small-cap or news-driven stocks.

Volatility halts are not rewards, warnings, or predictions by themselves. They are market structure events that can create serious risk.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a volatility halt in plain language.
- Understand why fast movement can trigger a pause.
- Recognize why low-float and high-volume names can be halt-prone.
- Review the risk before and after a volatility halt.
- Avoid treating halt behavior as a trading signal.

## Quick Definition

A volatility halt is a temporary pause connected to rapid price movement.

Traders may see volatility halts in stocks with:

- Sudden catalyst attention.
- High relative volume.
- Low float.
- Large candle expansion.
- Thin liquidity.
- Wide spread.
- Emotional crowding.

The exact rules and bands can vary by security, exchange, and market structure. Traders should verify current official rules.

Under LULD market-structure rules, price bands are designed to prevent trades in NMS stocks outside specified bands during regular trading hours. If a security remains in a Limit State for 15 seconds, the primary listing exchange can declare a five-minute trading pause, and the pause can be extended. Rights, warrants, OTC securities, and some timing windows are treated differently, so rule-specific details should be checked before publication or trading review.

## Why Volatility Halts Happen

Volatility halts are designed to pause trading during extreme movement so the market can process information and order interest.

For traders, the practical issue is interruption. A trade that looked manageable before the halt can become harder to manage after the halt.

## Why They Matter In Small Caps

Small-cap and low-float stocks can move quickly because fewer shares may be available for public trading. When attention spikes, price can accelerate faster than the trader expects.

That does not mean low float guarantees a halt. It means halt risk should be part of the review when candles expand quickly and volume surges.

## Realistic Example

A stock with a small float releases a headline and runs sharply. Volume spikes, the spread changes quickly, and the chart begins printing large candles. Price pauses in a volatility halt.

Before resume, nobody knows exactly where clean liquidity will appear. After resume, the stock may continue, reject, widen, or chop. The responsible review is about risk planning, not prediction.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is assuming a volatility halt is bullish or bearish by itself.

Another mistake is ignoring halt risk in fast low-float names.

Traders also make mistakes when they size too large before a potential halt.

Another mistake is forgetting that stop orders or exit plans may not behave normally during a pause and resume.

A final mistake is chasing immediately after resume without reviewing spread and depth.

## Practical Checklist

- The stock is checked for low-float or thin-liquidity context.
- Volume and candle expansion are reviewed.
- Spread and slippage risk are considered.
- The trader has a plan for interruption risk.
- The halt is not treated as a directional signal.
- Resume conditions are reviewed before any new decision.
- The event is logged for later risk review.

## Apply This In Review

- What conditions appeared before the halt?
- Was the stock already extended?
- Did I account for halt risk?
- Was position size realistic for interruption risk?
- What happened to spread and liquidity after resume?
- Did I assume continuation or reversal?
- What rule would reduce halt-related risk next time?

## FAQ

### What is a volatility halt?

A volatility halt is a temporary trading pause connected to rapid or extreme price movement.

### Does a volatility halt predict direction?

No. A volatility halt does not predict what happens after resume.

### Why do low-float stocks halt?

Low-float stocks can halt when attention, volume, and price movement become extreme, but low float alone does not guarantee a halt.

### What should traders review after a volatility halt?

Review pre-halt conditions, position size, spread, liquidity, resume behavior, and whether the trade plan accounted for interruption risk.
