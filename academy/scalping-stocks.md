# Scalping Stocks

Scalping is a very short-term trading style focused on small price movements, fast decisions, and execution quality. Because holding periods are short, spread, slippage, fills, and discipline matter heavily.

Scalping is not easier because trades are shorter. The shorter the trade, the less room there is for sloppy execution.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define scalping as a trading style.
- Understand why liquidity and spread matter.
- Review execution quality in fast trades.
- Recognize overtrading and fee/slippage pressure.
- Avoid treating speed as skill.

## Quick Definition

Scalping is a short-term style where traders attempt to capture small moves over brief holding periods.

- Trades may last seconds or minutes.
- Execution quality matters heavily.
- Wide spreads can damage the idea quickly.
- Slippage can erase expected edge.
- High frequency can create overtrading risk.

The style is execution-sensitive and requires strict review.

In fast-moving stocks, the price a trader sees on screen may not be the price they receive. That is why scalping review should keep spread, slippage, order type, and fill quality central.

## How This Style Works

Scalping usually depends on immediate market conditions:

- Tight spread and enough liquidity.
- Clear level or micro-structure context.
- Fast entry and exit planning.
- Small but defined invalidation.
- Awareness of order type and fill quality.
- Stopping rules to prevent rapid overtrading.

## What This Style Requires

Scalping requires discipline because decisions happen quickly.

- Fast but planned execution.
- Awareness of bid, ask, and last price.
- Realistic expectations about fills.
- Immediate risk control.
- Trade count limits if overtrading appears.
- Detailed execution review after the session.

## When It Can Mislead

Scalping can mislead when traders think short holding periods automatically mean lower risk. In fast or thin names, a few poor fills can create outsized damage.

## Realistic Example

A trader tries to scalp a fast-moving small-cap stock with a wide spread. The chart looks active, but the entry fills near the ask and the exit slips below the bid. The review shows the trade was less about chart direction and more about poor execution conditions.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is scalping wide-spread stocks.

Another mistake is ignoring slippage because the chart looks active.

Traders also make mistakes when they take too many small trades after one loss.

Another mistake is using market orders without understanding fill risk.

A final mistake is reviewing only win rate instead of execution quality.

## FAQ

### What is scalping stocks?

Scalping is a very short-term trading style focused on small moves and fast execution.

### Is scalping lower risk?

Not necessarily. Poor fills, wide spreads, slippage, and overtrading can create meaningful risk.

### What matters most in scalping?

Liquidity, spread, execution, speed, discipline, and review quality matter heavily.

### Can beginners scalp?

Beginners should first understand spread, slippage, risk, and execution before studying fast styles.

### Why is overtrading common in scalping?

Short trades can make it easy to keep clicking after losses or missed moves.

### How should scalps be reviewed?

Review fill quality, spread, slippage, plan quality, trade count, and whether the stock matched the style.
