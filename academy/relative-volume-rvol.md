# Relative Volume RVOL: How Traders Review The Scanner Metric

Relative volume RVOL is a scanner metric that compares current trading volume with a stock's normal trading volume. Traders use it to spot stocks that are unusually active.


## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain relative volume RVOL in volume, liquidity, or order-flow context.
- Identify where it appears on a realistic chart or trading dashboard.
- Review the concept with price levels, execution quality, spread, liquidity, and risk in mind.
- Avoid treating it as guaranteed confirmation.

RVOL is helpful because it turns the relative-volume idea into a number. But the number is only context. High RVOL does not mean a trade is clean, safe, or likely to continue.

## What You Should Understand Before Reading This

Before studying RVOL, remember:

- Volume measures shares traded.
- Relative volume compares current activity with normal activity.
- RVOL is usually a platform or scanner metric.
- Different platforms may calculate RVOL differently.
- High RVOL means unusual activity, not automatic direction.
- RVOL should be reviewed with catalyst, level quality, liquidity, spread, and execution.

The goal is not to trade a number. The goal is to understand what the number is comparing and whether the trade still had structure.

## Quick Definition

RVOL stands for relative volume.

In many scanners, RVOL is shown as a multiple:

- `1.0x` means activity is near normal.
- `2.0x` means activity is about twice normal.
- `5.0x` means activity is about five times normal.
- `10.0x` or higher means activity is far above the normal baseline.

![Candlestick chart showing current volume compared with a normal time-of-day baseline.](/academy/images/chart-reading/rvol-time-of-day-comparison.svg)

The exact number depends on what the platform uses as the baseline.

## Why Time Of Day Matters

RVOL is more useful when it compares volume at the same point in the trading day.

A stock that trades 1 million shares by 10:00 a.m. may be extremely active if it normally trades only 100,000 shares by that time. The same 1 million shares may be less unusual if the stock normally trades heavy volume at the open.

This is why some platforms use time-of-day RVOL. They compare today's volume so far with the stock's typical volume by the same time.

Time-of-day context can help traders avoid two mistakes:

- Underestimating unusual early activity.
- Overestimating end-of-day volume that simply accumulated over time.

## Why RVOL Calculations Can Differ

Different platforms may show different RVOL values because they may use different formulas.

Platform differences can include:

- Average daily volume versus time-of-day average volume.
- Ten-day, twenty-day, thirty-day, or custom lookback periods.
- Inclusion or exclusion of premarket volume.
- Inclusion or exclusion of after-hours volume.
- Different handling of abnormal news days.
- Different data providers.

This does not mean RVOL is useless. It means the trader should know what their tool is comparing.

If one platform shows `8x` RVOL and another shows `5x`, both may still be telling the same practical story: the stock is unusually active.

RVOL is a platform or data-vendor metric, not a single standardized number. Before relying on an RVOL value, traders should know the scanner's lookback period, time-of-day handling, data feed, and whether premarket or after-hours volume is included.

## RVOL On A Scanner

Scanners often rank stocks by RVOL because unusual volume can bring trader attention.

![Candlestick chart with a scanner-style context panel showing high RVOL, catalyst, spread, and volume fade review.](/academy/images/chart-reading/rvol-scanner-context-review.svg)

A high-RVOL scanner result should lead to review questions:

- What caused the unusual activity?
- Is there real news, a filing, earnings, or sector attention?
- Is price near a meaningful level?
- Is volume continuing or fading?
- Is liquidity clean enough for the trader's size?
- Is the spread reasonable?
- Is the move already extended?

The scanner can point at activity. The trader still has to evaluate quality.

## High RVOL Is Not Always Bullish

High RVOL can happen during many different conditions:

- Strong upward momentum.
- Panic selling.
- Failed breakouts.
- Short-lived news spikes.
- Halt resumptions.
- Dilution or offering reactions.
- Earnings reactions.
- Sector-wide volatility.

This is why the candle chart matters. A high-RVOL stock that is holding key levels behaves differently from a high-RVOL stock that is fading below them.

RVOL tells you attention is unusual. It does not tell you the direction is clean.

## Realistic Example

A stock normally trades 800,000 shares per day. Today it trades 4 million shares in the first hour after a press release. A scanner shows high RVOL.

A trader reviewing the alert might write:

- RVOL was high compared with normal activity.
- The press release caused attention.
- Price opened above premarket high but failed to hold the level.
- Volume was heaviest during the first push and faded afterward.
- The spread widened during the pullback.
- The high RVOL made the stock worth watching, but the trade required level and liquidity review.

This review is more useful than simply writing "high RVOL stock."

## Common Mistakes

Common RVOL mistakes include:

- Treating high RVOL as an automatic trade trigger.
- Assuming high RVOL is always bullish.
- Ignoring the calculation method behind the scanner number.
- Comparing two platforms without understanding their baselines.
- Ignoring premarket volume rules.
- Chasing a scanner result after the move is already extended.
- Ignoring spread, liquidity, float, catalyst quality, and nearby levels.
- Forgetting that RVOL can stay high even after participation starts fading.

RVOL should create curiosity, not urgency.

## FAQ

### What does RVOL mean?

RVOL means relative volume. It compares current trading volume with normal trading volume.

### What does high RVOL mean?

High RVOL means a stock is trading much more actively than usual. It shows unusual activity, but it does not guarantee direction or trade quality.

### Is RVOL the same on every platform?

No. Platforms may use different average periods, time-of-day comparisons, data providers, and premarket or after-hours rules.

### Is high RVOL bullish?

Not automatically. High RVOL can appear during buying, selling, panic, failed moves, news reactions, and exits.

### Why do day traders watch RVOL?

Day traders watch RVOL because it can help identify stocks with unusual attention and possible liquidity changes.

### Should traders buy only because RVOL is high?

No. RVOL is context, not a trade signal. Traders still need level quality, catalyst review, liquidity, risk control, and execution discipline.

### Should RVOL go in a review notes?

Yes, if it affected the trade idea. Recording RVOL can help traders review whether scanner activity supported the trade or encouraged chasing.
