# Inside Bar

This is a reference lesson inside the **Chart Reading And Market Structure** course. It follows [Hammer Candlestick](/academy/candlestick-patterns/hammer/) and leads into [Outside Bar](/academy/candlestick-patterns/outside-bar/).

An inside bar forms when a candle stays inside the high and low of the prior candle. It can show compression or a temporary pause.

The inside bar gives traders a range to review. It does not promise a clean break.

![Candlestick chart showing inside bar compression and outside bar range expansion with volume context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/inside-outside-bar-context.svg)

## What It Looks Like

An inside bar is a candle whose high is lower than the prior candle's high and whose low is higher than the prior candle's low.

- The prior candle is often called the mother candle.
- The inside candle stays inside that range.
- The range high and low become review points.
- Volume may contract during the pause.
- Breaks can follow through or fail.

An inside bar shows contraction inside a prior range.

## What It Usually Shows

Inside bars show temporary compression.

- A pause after a move.
- A small range inside a larger candle.
- Potential balance between buyers and sellers.
- A possible false break setup.
- A range that needs follow-through review.

## Where It Matters Most

Inside bars need range, volume, and level context.

- Mother candle size.
- Trend before compression.
- Volume contraction or expansion.
- Nearby support and resistance.
- Whether the break holds outside the range.
- Timeframe alignment.

## What Makes It Weak Or Misleading

Inside bars can mislead when traders assume compression must produce a large move. Price can fake out or keep moving sideways.

## Example Chart Read

After a momentum candle, price forms a smaller candle inside the prior range while volume contracts. A trader reviews the mother candle high and low, then studies whether any later break holds or fails back inside.

## Beginner Traps

One common mistake is entering before the range breaks.

Another mistake is assuming every inside bar leads to expansion.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore nearby levels.

Another mistake is trading inside bars in dead, low-quality chop.

A final mistake is using a timeframe that does not match the trade plan.

## Key Takeaway

An inside bar is not important because it predicts a breakout. It is important because it defines a smaller range inside a larger candle. Before moving on, make sure you can identify the mother candle high and low and explain what would count as a hold, failure, or fakeout.

In trade review, inside bars are useful when they explain compression before a decision. They are not useful when they are forced onto random sideways candles.

## FAQ

### What is an inside bar?

It is a candle contained within the high and low of the prior candle.

### What is the mother candle?

The mother candle is the prior candle whose high and low contain the inside bar.

### Is an inside bar bullish or bearish?

It is neutral by itself and needs context.

### Does an inside bar guarantee a breakout?

No. It can break, fail, or continue sideways.

### Why does volume matter?

Volume can help review whether activity is contracting or expanding around the range.

### How should inside bars be reviewed?

Review the mother candle range, break direction, volume, levels, invalidation, and follow-through.
