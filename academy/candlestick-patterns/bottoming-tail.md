# Bottoming Tail Candle

This lesson is part of the **Chart Reading And Market Structure** course. It follows [Pin Bar](/academy/candlestick-patterns/pin-bar/) and leads into [Topping Tail Candle](/academy/candlestick-patterns/topping-tail/).

A bottoming tail candle has a long lower wick that shows price dropped during the candle and recovered before the close. It can appear near support, after a flush, or during a failed breakdown attempt.

The candle can be useful, but only if the trader reviews whether the lower area actually mattered afterward.

![Candlestick chart showing a hammer-style lower wick near support with volume and follow-through context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/hammer-support-context.svg)

## What It Looks Like

A bottoming tail is a candle with a long lower wick and a close above the low.

- Price tested lower during the candle.
- The candle closed away from the low.
- The lower wick may show rejection below.
- It often overlaps with hammer-style candles.
- The wick low becomes an important review point.

A bottoming tail shows lower-price rejection during the candle. Follow-through decides whether that rejection mattered.

## What It Usually Shows

Bottoming tails show recovery from lower prices.

- Potential demand near support.
- A possible failed breakdown.
- A possible temporary bounce after selling pressure.
- A possible liquidity flush.
- A need to watch whether the wick low holds.

## Where It Matters Most

Bottoming tails need support and follow-through context.

- Support or low-of-day area.
- Volume during the wick.
- Whether price reclaims a level.
- Whether the wick low holds.
- Spread and liquidity.
- Broader trend and catalyst context.

## What Makes It Weak Or Misleading

Bottoming tails can mislead during downtrends because price can print several lower wicks and continue lower.

## Example Chart Read

A stock flushes below the prior low, recovers, and closes back above the level with a long lower wick. The review asks whether volume increased, whether the level held afterward, and whether the trader chased far from risk.

## Beginner Traps

One common mistake is assuming the bottom is in.

Another mistake is ignoring the broader downtrend.

Traders also make mistakes when they chase far above the wick low.

Another mistake is ignoring volume and liquidity.

A final mistake is holding after the wick low fails.

## Key Takeaway

A bottoming tail is a lower-wick recovery candle. Before moving on, make sure you can identify the wick low, the nearby support or prior low, and what would prove that the lower-wick idea failed.

If you review a completed trade around a bottoming tail, focus on whether the entry respected risk near the wick and whether the stock actually held the recovery.

## FAQ

### What is a bottoming tail candle?

It is a candle with a long lower wick and a close above the low.

### Is a bottoming tail bullish?

It can be constructive near support, but it is not automatically bullish.

### Does it guarantee reversal?

No. Price can break below the wick low.

### Is it the same as a hammer?

They are related; a hammer is one form of lower-wick rejection.

### Why does volume matter?

Volume helps review whether the rejection had meaningful participation.

### How should it be reviewed?

Review level, volume, wick low, reclaim behavior, entry timing, and failure.
