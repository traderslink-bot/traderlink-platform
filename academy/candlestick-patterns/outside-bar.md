# Outside Bar

This lesson is part of the **Chart Reading And Market Structure** course. It follows [Inside Bar](/academy/candlestick-patterns/inside-bar/) and leads into [Candle Volume Confirmation](/academy/candlestick-patterns/candle-volume-confirmation/).

An outside bar forms when a candle trades above the prior high and below the prior low. It shows range expansion, volatility, and a wider battle between buyers and sellers.

An outside bar can look powerful, but it can also mark exhaustion, a trap, or messy volatility.

![Candlestick chart showing inside bar compression and outside bar range expansion with volume context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/inside-outside-bar-context.svg)

## What It Looks Like

An outside bar is a candle whose high is above the prior candle's high and whose low is below the prior candle's low.

- It expands beyond the prior candle range.
- The close location matters heavily.
- It can appear near highs, lows, or after compression.
- Volume can help explain participation.
- The pattern can fail quickly if the close is weak.

An outside bar shows range expansion, not guaranteed direction.

## What It Usually Shows

Outside bars show that price swept both sides of the prior candle range.

- Volatility expansion.
- A possible failed break in one direction.
- A strong close after a range sweep.
- Possible exhaustion after a move.
- A need to review where the candle closed.

## Where It Matters Most

Outside bars need close-location and level context.

- Where the candle formed.
- Which side of the range held at close.
- Volume and spread.
- Nearby support and resistance.
- Prior compression or trend.
- Follow-through after the outside bar.

## What Makes It Weak Or Misleading

Outside bars can mislead when traders focus only on the large candle and ignore where it closed or whether the move is already extended.

## Example Chart Read

A stock breaks above a prior high, reverses sharply, sweeps below the prior low, and closes near support. The outside bar shows volatility, but the review must ask whether the candle created clarity or just noise.

## Beginner Traps

One common mistake is treating a large outside bar as automatic confirmation.

Another mistake is entering after the candle is too extended.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore close location.

Another mistake is overlooking spread and volatility.

A final mistake is holding after the outside-bar idea fails.

## Key Takeaway

An outside bar is a range-expansion candle. Before moving on, make sure you can explain which side of the prior range was tested, where the candle closed, and whether the size of the candle improved or worsened risk.

If a completed trade was based on an outside bar, review whether the candle gave useful structure or simply pulled you into volatility.

## FAQ

### What is an outside bar?

It is a candle whose high is above and low is below the prior candle's range.

### Is an outside bar bullish or bearish?

It depends on where it forms and where it closes.

### What does an outside bar show?

It shows range expansion and volatility.

### Can outside bars fail?

Yes. They can mark traps, exhaustion, or noisy price action.

### Why does close location matter?

The close helps show which side held control by the end of the candle.

### How should outside bars be reviewed?

Review level, close, volume, range, entry timing, and follow-through.
