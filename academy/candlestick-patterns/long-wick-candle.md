# Long Wick Candle

This lesson is part of the **Candlestick Patterns In Context** course. It follows [Candlestick Patterns](/academy/candlestick-patterns/) and leads into [Doji Candle](/academy/candlestick-patterns/doji/).

A long wick candle has a wick that stands out compared with the body. It can show rejection, volatility, or a failed attempt to hold an extreme price area.

A wick is useful only when the trader asks where it formed and what happened after it.

![Candlestick chart showing long upper and lower wick rejection around support and resistance with volume context.](/images/academy/chart-reading/long-wick-rejection-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize the pattern without treating the name as a trade plan.
- Read the candle beside levels, volume, trend, and liquidity.
- Identify common failure conditions.
- Review entry, exit, and risk behavior after the trade.
- Use the pattern as context rather than prediction.

## Quick Definition

A long wick candle is a candle with a noticeably long upper or lower wick compared with its body.

- A long upper wick shows price pushed higher and closed below the high.
- A long lower wick shows price pushed lower and closed above the low.
- The wick marks an intraperiod extreme.
- The body shows where price opened and closed.
- The next candles help review whether rejection mattered.

The wick shows movement away from an extreme, not a guaranteed reversal.

## What The Candle Shows

Long wicks show that price explored one area and closed away from it.

- Possible rejection at a level.
- Possible volatility around news.
- Possible spread or liquidity distortion.
- Possible failed breakout or breakdown attempt.
- Possible emotional flush or squeeze.

## Context That Matters

The same wick can mean different things in different places.

- Support or resistance nearby.
- Volume on the wick candle.
- Spread and liquidity.
- Prior trend and extension.
- Timeframe and time of day.
- Next candle follow-through.

## When It Can Mislead

Long wicks can mislead in thin or volatile stocks. A wick created by a wide spread is not the same as a clean rejection at a high-volume level.

## Realistic Example

Price pushes above resistance, prints a long upper wick, and closes back below the zone. The review asks whether sellers truly rejected the area, whether volume expanded, and whether the next candle stayed below or reclaimed the zone.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating every wick as reversal.

Another mistake is ignoring spread and liquidity.

Traders also make mistakes when they enter before the candle closes.

Another mistake is overvaluing wicks in the middle of a range.

A final mistake is ignoring the next candle after the wick.

## Practical Checklist

- The candle is identified correctly.
- The pattern formed near a meaningful area, not random noise.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- The broader trend and nearby levels are checked.
- Risk is defined without relying on the candle name.
- The next candles are reviewed for follow-through or failure.
- The trade can be reviewed honestly after completion.

## Apply This In Review

- What candle pattern did I think I saw?

Long wick review should connect wick direction, level location, volume, and follow-through.

## FAQ

### What is a long wick candle?

It is a candle with a wick that is large compared with the body.

### What does a long upper wick show?

It shows price traded higher during the candle and closed away from the high.

### What does a long lower wick show?

It shows price traded lower and recovered before the close.

### Does a long wick mean reversal?

No. It needs context and can fail.

### Why does liquidity matter?

Thin liquidity and wide spreads can exaggerate wicks.

### How should long wicks be reviewed?

Review wick direction, level, volume, spread, entry timing, and follow-through.
