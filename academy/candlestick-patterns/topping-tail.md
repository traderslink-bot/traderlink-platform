# Topping Tail Candle

This lesson is part of the **Candlestick Patterns In Context** course. It follows [Bottoming Tail Candle](/academy/candlestick-patterns/bottoming-tail/) and leads into [Spinning Top Candle](/academy/candlestick-patterns/spinning-top/).

A topping tail candle has a long upper wick that shows price pushed higher during the candle and closed away from the high. It can appear near resistance, after an extension, or during a failed breakout attempt.

The candle should be reviewed as rejection context, not a prediction that price must reverse.

![Candlestick chart showing upper wick rejection near resistance with volume context.](/images/academy/chart-reading/long-wick-rejection-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize the pattern without treating the name as a trade plan.
- Read the candle beside levels, volume, trend, and liquidity.

- Review entry, exit, and risk behavior after the trade.
- Use the pattern as context rather than prediction.

## Quick Definition

A topping tail is a candle with a long upper wick and a close below the high.

- Price tested higher during the candle.
- The candle closed away from the high.
- The upper wick may show rejection above.
- It can form near resistance or after extension.
- The wick high becomes an important review point.

A topping tail shows rejection of higher prices during that candle.

## What The Candle Shows

Topping tails show that price could not hold the upper extreme.

- Possible resistance rejection.
- Possible failed breakout.
- Possible exhaustion after a fast move.
- Possible profit-taking or supply.
- Possible spread or liquidity distortion.

## Context That Matters

Topping tails need resistance and follow-through context.

- Resistance or high-of-day area.
- Volume during the wick.
- Whether price reclaims the wick high.
- Extension from support.
- Spread and liquidity.
- Catalyst and float context.

## When It Can Mislead

Topping tails can mislead when traders assume the move is finished. Price can reject once, consolidate, and later break higher.

## Realistic Example

A stock runs into a prior high, spikes above it, then closes back below with a long upper wick. The review asks whether volume showed real rejection, whether price later reclaimed the level, and whether any entry was late.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is assuming every upper wick means a top.

Another mistake is ignoring strong trend context.

Traders also make mistakes when they react before the candle closes.

Another mistake is ignoring volume and liquidity.

A final mistake is using the candle to justify a trade without invalidation.

## Practical Checklist

- The candle is identified correctly.
- The pattern formed near a meaningful area, not random noise.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed.
- The broader trend and nearby levels are checked.
- Risk is defined without relying on the candle name.
- The next candles are reviewed for follow-through or failure.
- The trade can be reviewed honestly after completion.

## FAQ

### What is a topping tail candle?

It is a candle with a long upper wick and a close below the high.

### Does it mean the top is in?

No. It needs context and can be reclaimed.

### Where is it more useful?

Near resistance or after an extended move.

### Why does volume matter?

Volume helps review whether rejection had participation.

### Can topping tails fail?

Yes. Price can reclaim the wick high or continue later.

### How should it be reviewed?

Review level, volume, wick high, reclaim behavior, entry timing, and risk.
