# Pin Bar

This is a reference lesson inside the **Chart Reading And Market Structure** course. It follows [Outside Bar](/academy/candlestick-patterns/outside-bar/) and leads into [Bottoming Tail Candle](/academy/candlestick-patterns/bottoming-tail/).

A pin bar is a candle with a long wick and a small body, often discussed as rejection from an extreme. It overlaps with long wick candles, hammers, and topping or bottoming tails.

The pattern is not useful because of the name. It is useful only when the rejection happened in a meaningful area.

![Candlestick chart showing long upper and lower wick rejection around support and resistance with volume context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/long-wick-rejection-context.svg)

## What It Is

A pin bar is a rejection-style candle with a small body and a long wick.

- The wick points toward the rejected area.
- The body is usually smaller than the wick.
- Upper-wick pin bars can show rejection above.
- Lower-wick pin bars can show rejection below.
- The pattern needs level and follow-through context.

A pin bar shows rejection during the candle, not guaranteed direction after it.

## What It Shows

Pin bars show a failed attempt to hold an extreme price area.

- Potential support or resistance reaction.
- Potential liquidity sweep.
- Potential failed breakout or breakdown.
- Potential spread distortion in thin names.
- Potential reversal attempt if follow-through appears.

## Where It Matters

Pin bars need more than shape.

- The level where the wick formed.
- Volume behind the rejection.
- Spread and liquidity.
- Trend and extension.
- Close location.
- Next candle behavior.

## When It Misleads

Pin bars can mislead when traders see a long wick and assume the market has chosen a direction.

## Example

A stock spikes above resistance, prints a long upper wick, and closes back below the zone. The review asks whether this was real rejection or a thin-liquidity wick, then checks follow-through.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is entering only because a pin bar appeared.

Another mistake is ignoring where the wick formed.

Traders also make mistakes when they overread thin-stock wicks.

Another mistake is entering before the candle closes.

A final mistake is ignoring the next candle.

## Before You Move On

A pin bar is a rejection candle, but the rejection only matters if the location matters. Before moving on, make sure you can identify the rejected area, the close location, and whether the wick was clean or caused by thin trading.

In a journal, a pin bar should explain rejection quality. It should not be used as a shortcut for "I saw a wick, so I took a trade."

## FAQ

### What is a pin bar?

A pin bar is a candle with a small body and a long wick that may show rejection.

### Is a pin bar a signal?

No. It is context that needs levels, volume, and risk review.

### What does the wick show?

It shows price moved to an extreme and closed away from it.

### Why do pin bars fail?

They fail when rejection does not follow through or when the wick was caused by noise or thin liquidity.

### Where are pin bars more useful?

Near meaningful support or resistance.

### How should pin bars be reviewed?

Review wick direction, level, volume, spread, close, and follow-through.
