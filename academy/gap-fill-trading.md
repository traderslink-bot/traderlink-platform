# Gap Fill Trading: How Traders Review Gap Areas

Gap fill trading is based on the idea that a stock may move back into a price gap left between one trading session and another. Traders watch gap areas because they can become important chart reference zones.

This lesson is part of the **Chart Reading And Market Structure** learning path. It builds on consolidation and previous-day levels, and it connects chart gaps with catalyst, volume, and trade review.


## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain gap fills in chart-reading context.
- Identify where it appears on a realistic chart.
- Review the concept with levels, volume, liquidity, and risk in mind.
- Avoid treating it as a guaranteed trading signal.

A gap fill is not guaranteed. Some gaps fill quickly. Some take days or weeks. Some never fill. The important part is understanding why the gap happened, how price reacts near the gap area, and whether volume supports the move.

## What You Should Understand Before Reading This

Before studying gap fills, remember:

- A gap is an empty price area between sessions.
- Gap top, gap bottom, and prior close can become reference levels.
- The catalyst that caused the gap matters.
- A gap can hold, partially fill, fully fill, or continue away from the gap.
- Strong news can keep price in the new range.
- Thin liquidity can create gaps that behave differently from high-volume gaps.

The goal is not to assume price must return. The goal is to review the gap area and the trader's response.

## Quick Definition

A gap happens when a stock opens at a meaningfully different price than the prior session's close, sometimes outside the prior session's range, leaving an empty price area on the chart.

A gap fill happens when price later moves back through that empty area.

For example, if a stock closes at $5.00 and opens the next day at $6.00, there is a gap between $5.00 and $6.00. If price later trades back down through that area, traders may call it a gap fill.

![Candlestick chart showing a gap zone between prior close and current open with price moving into the gap area.](/academy/images/chart-reading/gap-fill-zone-map.svg)

Gaps can happen because of news, earnings, filings, market conditions, low liquidity, or overnight order flow.

## Why Gap Fills Matter

Gap fill trading matters because gaps can become important reference points.

A gap up may create excitement, but if the stock cannot hold the gap area, traders may watch whether it moves back toward the prior close or prior range.

A gap down may create fear, but if the stock recovers and starts filling the gap higher, traders may watch whether buyers can reclaim lost ground.

This is where traders can get into trouble. They assume every gap must fill right away. It does not.

## Gap Levels To Mark

Gap review usually starts by identifying the gap area.

Traders may mark:

- Prior session close.
- Current session open.
- Prior day high or low.
- Gap midpoint.
- Gap top.
- Gap bottom.
- Nearby support and resistance.
- Catalyst time and reason for the gap.

After that, traders watch how price reacts.

## Failed Gap Fill Or Gap Hold

A failed gap fill happens when price starts moving into the gap but cannot keep filling it.

![Candlestick chart showing price entering a gap area but failing to continue filling the gap and holding above the gap midpoint.](/academy/images/chart-reading/failed-gap-fill-hold-review.svg)

This does not mean the gap must hold forever. It means the fill attempt did not complete and the trader should review the behavior.

Useful questions include:

- What caused the gap?
- Did price enter the gap with volume?
- Did the move stall at the gap midpoint?
- Did the gap top or bottom act as support or resistance?
- Was the trader assuming the gap had to fill?
- Did the trader respect the level when the fill failed?

## Realistic Example Scenario

A stock closes at $4.00 and opens the next day at $5.25 after news.

The gap area is roughly between $4.00 and $5.25. If the stock fails to hold above $5.25 and starts fading, traders may watch whether it moves back toward $4.00.

But the trader still needs to ask:

- Was the news strong or weak?
- Did volume support the gap?
- Was the stock already extended?
- Did price lose key support?
- Is there liquidity for the trade?
- Is the gap fill idea early or already crowded?

A gap fill trade should not be based only on the gap. It should be based on price behavior around the gap.

## Common Mistakes

Common gap fill mistakes include:

- Assuming all gaps fill.
- Ignoring the catalyst that caused the gap.
- Shorting a gap up only because it gapped.
- Buying a gap down only because price looks cheaper.
- Entering after most of the gap has already filled.
- Ignoring volume during the fill attempt.
- Forgetting that a partial fill can fail.
- Reviewing only the result instead of the gap structure.

Some gaps can hold for a long time, especially if the catalyst changes how the market values the stock.

## Practical Checklist

Before using a gap fill idea in a plan or review, ask:

1. Was it a gap up or gap down?
2. What caused the gap?
3. Where is the gap top?
4. Where is the gap bottom?
5. Where is the prior close?
6. Did price enter the gap with volume?
7. Did the fill hold, fail, or partially fill?
8. Was the entry near a clear level or late after the move started?
9. What would invalidate the gap fill idea?
10. Did the trader assume the gap had to fill?

This checklist keeps gap fill review tied to price behavior instead of assumption.

## Apply This In Review

Gap fill trades should be reviewed with clear chart context.

Ask:

- What caused the gap?
- Was it a gap up or gap down?
- Where were the gap top and gap bottom?
- Did price hold the gap or move into it?
- Did volume confirm the move?
- Did I enter near a clear level or chase after the move started?
- Did the trade have a defined invalidation area?
- Did I respect the plan when the gap did not fill?

A good gap fill review helps traders see whether they were trading a structured level or just assuming price had to return.

## Trader Intelligence Bridge

Trader Intelligence is being built to help traders review their own trades with more structure.

For gap fill trades, that can mean reviewing:

- Whether gap levels were marked before the trade.
- Whether the catalyst was noted.
- Whether price entered, held, or rejected the gap.
- Whether the trader entered early, late, or after most of the gap was already filled.
- Whether volume supported the move.
- Whether failed gap fills repeat across trades.

The product connection is review, not prediction. Trader Intelligence should help traders study what they did around gaps, not tell them what to buy or sell.

## FAQ

### What is gap fill trading?

Gap fill trading is when traders watch for price to move back through a gap area left between trading sessions.

### Do all stock gaps fill?

No. Some gaps fill quickly, some fill later, and some do not fill at all.

### Why do gaps fill?

Gaps may fill when the initial reaction fades, volume dries up, traders reduce exposure, or price returns to a prior value area.

### Are gap fills better for day trading or swing trading?

Gap fills can matter to both. Day traders may focus on intraday gap behavior, while swing traders may watch whether gaps fill over multiple sessions.

### What can go wrong with gap fill trades?

The gap can hold, price can continue away from the gap, volume can shift, or the catalyst can be strong enough to keep price in the new range.

### How should I review gap fill trades?

Track the gap cause, gap levels, entry timing, volume, key support or resistance, invalidation level, and whether the trade followed the original plan.
