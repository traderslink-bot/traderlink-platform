# Bollinger Bands

This lesson is part of the **Technical Indicators And Tools** course. It follows [MACD](/academy/macd/) and leads into [ATR](/academy/atr/).

Bollinger Bands place volatility bands around a moving average. They help traders review whether price is compressed, expanding, or stretched compared with recent movement.

The bands can be useful, but a touch of the upper or lower band is not a command.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what Bollinger Bands measure.
- Understand volatility expansion and contraction.
- Review band touches without treating them as signals.
- Recognize squeeze, trend, and chop context.
- Use Bollinger Bands with price, volume, levels, and risk.

## Quick Definition

Bollinger Bands are volatility bands plotted above and below a moving average.

- The middle line is usually a moving average.
- The upper and lower bands are based on recent volatility.
- Bands widen when volatility expands.
- Bands narrow when volatility contracts.
- Price can ride a band during strong trends.

The bands describe recent volatility context. They do not predict the next move.

## What It Measures

Bollinger Bands measure price location relative to a moving average and recent volatility. They do not measure news quality, liquidity, float, dilution, or whether a trade has acceptable risk.

Bollinger Bands are commonly plotted with a middle simple moving average and upper/lower bands set a selected number of standard deviations away from that average. The common default is 20 periods and 2 standard deviations, but settings can be adjusted and should not be treated as universal trading rules.

## How Traders Use It As Context

Traders may use Bollinger Bands to review:

- Whether volatility was compressed before a move.
- Whether volatility expanded after a breakout or breakdown.
- Whether price was stretched from recent average movement.
- Whether price was trending along a band.
- Whether a trader chased after expansion.

## When It Can Mislead

Bollinger Bands can mislead when traders assume the upper band means an immediate drop or the lower band means an immediate bounce. Strong trends can keep pressing along a band, while choppy markets can repeatedly fake expansion.

## Realistic Example

A stock trades sideways with narrow bands before news volume enters and the bands expand. Price moves toward resistance while riding the upper band. A trader who fades only because price touched the upper band is ignoring trend and catalyst context.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is treating an upper band touch as a reason to expect an immediate drop.

Another mistake is treating a lower band touch as a reason to expect an immediate bounce.

Traders also make mistakes when they chase after bands expand far from a clean risk area.

Another mistake is using band squeezes without checking volume and catalyst context.

A final mistake is ignoring support and resistance because the bands look active.

## Practical Checklist

- I know whether bands were narrow or wide.
- I reviewed trend versus range context.
- I checked nearby support and resistance.
- I checked volume and catalyst context.
- I did not treat a band touch as a command.
- I reviewed whether volatility expansion created chase risk.
- I had risk defined outside of the indicator.

## Apply This In Review

- Were the bands expanding or contracting?
- Was price trending, ranging, or chopping?
- Did I chase after a volatility expansion?
- Did I assume a band touch meant reversal?
- Was there nearby support or resistance?
- Did volume support the move?
- Did Bollinger Bands clarify volatility or distract from structure?

Bollinger Band review works best when volatility context is connected to price behavior.

## FAQ

### What are Bollinger Bands?

Bollinger Bands are volatility bands placed above and below a moving average.

### What does a Bollinger Band squeeze mean?

A squeeze shows compressed volatility, but it does not guarantee the direction or size of a move.

### Does touching the upper band mean price will fall?

No. Price can continue along the upper band in a strong trend.

### Does touching the lower band mean price will bounce?

No. Price can continue lower if weakness remains.

### When can Bollinger Bands mislead?

They can mislead in strong trends, choppy markets, or when used without levels, volume, and risk.

### How should Bollinger Bands be reviewed?

Review whether they clarified volatility expansion, contraction, extension, or whether they caused a trader to chase or fade too early.
