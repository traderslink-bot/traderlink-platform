# Anchored VWAP

Anchored VWAP is a version of VWAP that starts from a chosen point instead of resetting only at the session open. Traders may anchor it to an event, high, low, breakout, earnings reaction, or major catalyst.

Because the anchor is chosen by the user, anchored VWAP requires extra discipline.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what anchored VWAP measures.
- Understand common anchor points.
- Use anchored VWAP as event-based context.
- Recognize anchor bias and false precision.
- Review whether the anchor actually mattered.

## Quick Definition

Anchored VWAP is a volume weighted average price calculated from a selected starting point.

- Anchored to a major low or high.
- Anchored to a breakout or breakdown.
- Anchored to earnings, news, or filing reaction.
- Anchored to a gap day.
- Anchored to the start of a major trend leg.
- Used to review average price from that chosen event.

The anchor should have a reason before the line is trusted.

## What It Measures

Anchored VWAP measures the volume weighted average price from the chosen anchor point forward. It does not prove that price must react there.

Anchored VWAP uses the same general VWAP idea but begins from a user-selected bar instead of the regular session start. Because the chosen anchor controls which price and volume data are included, different anchor choices can create meaningfully different lines on the same chart.

## How Traders Use It As Context

Traders may use anchored VWAP to review:

- Average price since a catalyst or event.
- Whether price is above or below an important event average.
- Whether pullbacks are respecting an event-based reference.
- Whether a trend is still holding above a meaningful anchor.
- Whether the chosen anchor was useful after the trade.

## When It Can Mislead

Anchored VWAP can mislead when traders keep moving the anchor until it fits the trade. It can also create false precision when price is actually reacting to nearby support, resistance, or liquidity instead.

## Realistic Example

A stock gaps up after earnings and trends for several days. A trader anchors VWAP to the earnings gap day and reviews whether pullbacks stay above the event average. That can be useful. But if the trader keeps changing the anchor to support a thesis, the tool becomes confirmation bias.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is choosing anchors after the trade to justify the decision.

Another mistake is treating anchored VWAP as exact support or resistance.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore obvious chart levels near the anchored line.

Another mistake is using too many anchors at once until the chart is cluttered.

A final mistake is not recording why the anchor was chosen.

## FAQ

### What is anchored VWAP?

Anchored VWAP is VWAP calculated from a selected starting point such as an event, high, low, breakout, or gap.

### How is anchored VWAP different from regular VWAP?

Regular intraday VWAP usually resets each session, while anchored VWAP starts from a user-selected point.

### Where should anchored VWAP be anchored?

Common anchors include major highs, lows, earnings gaps, catalysts, breakouts, or important trend starts.

### Can anchored VWAP be misleading?

Yes. It can be misleading if the anchor is arbitrary or moved to fit a desired trade.

### Is anchored VWAP a signal?

No. It is an event-based average price reference, not a standalone trade signal.

### How should anchored VWAP be reviewed?

Review why the anchor was chosen, whether price reacted near it, and whether it improved context alongside levels, volume, and risk.
