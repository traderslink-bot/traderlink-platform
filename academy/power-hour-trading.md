# Power Hour Trading

This lesson is part of the **Day Trading Workflow** course. It follows [Midday Trading](/academy/midday-trading/) and leads into [After-Hours Trading](/academy/after-hours-trading/).

Power hour usually refers to the final hour of the regular session. Traders watch this period because volume and movement can sometimes return after a slower midday.

Power hour is also a discipline test. The trader may be tired, green, red, frustrated, or tempted to force one more decision before the close.

![Candlestick chart showing a slow midday range followed by late-session volume return and power hour reassessment.](/images/academy/chart-reading/midday-power-hour-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define power hour as a late-session workflow segment.
- Understand why volume and emotion can change near the close.
- Review late-day setups without treating them as guaranteed.
- Recognize common final-hour mistakes.
- Decide whether power hour needs specific personal rules.

## Quick Definition

Power hour is the final hour of the regular trading session.

Traders may watch:

- Late-day volume return.
- High-of-day or low-of-day tests.
- Reclaims of important levels.
- Fades after morning strength.
- Stocks holding a range into the close.
- News or filings released late in the day.
- Whether the trader is forcing activity.

The last hour can be active, but the clock alone is not a setup.

## How This Fits The Workflow

Power hour is a reassessment phase.

The trader should ask:

- Did volume return after midday?
- Is the level still meaningful?
- Is the move extended from support or VWAP?
- Is there enough time to manage the trade?
- Am I protecting a good process or trying to fix the day?

The final hour can create reviewable decisions, but it should not become a rescue mission.

## When It Can Mislead

Power hour can mislead when traders think a late move has to continue because time is running out. A stock can push toward high of day and still fail. A weak stock can bounce and still remain weak. Late volume can support movement, but it does not guarantee direction.

It can also mislead emotionally. A trader who is red may feel urgency. A trader who is green may get careless.

## Realistic Example

A stock runs in the morning, holds a range through midday, and starts pushing toward high of day in the final hour. Volume returns, but price is extended from the nearest support zone.

A prepared trader reviews risk, level quality, time left in the session, and the plan for the close. The review question is whether the decision was based on structure or on the feeling that this was the last chance.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is forcing one more trade late in the day.

Another mistake is giving back earlier progress because the trader keeps pushing.

Traders also make mistakes when they hold into the close without a plan.

Another mistake is chasing late-day extension far from a risk area.

A final mistake is using power hour to recover emotionally from earlier mistakes.

## Practical Checklist

- Volume has returned or the level is clearly active.
- The setup still has a realistic risk area.
- The trader knows the plan for the close.
- The decision is not based on being green or red.
- Spread and liquidity are acceptable.
- The trade fits the watchlist or a clear late-day reason.
- Final-hour trades are reviewed separately.

## Apply This In Review

- Did I trade better or worse in the final hour?
- Did I force a trade because time was running out?
- Did I give back earlier progress?
- Did volume support the move?
- Did I have a plan for the close?
- Was the trade based on structure or emotion?
- Should power hour have stricter rules in my plan?

## FAQ

### What is power hour trading?

Power hour trading refers to activity during the final hour of the regular trading session.

### Why do traders watch power hour?

Traders watch it because volume and movement can sometimes return late in the day.

### Is power hour always active?

No. Some final hours are active, and others remain slow or choppy.

### What is the biggest power hour risk?

A common risk is forcing a late trade because of frustration, urgency, or the desire to change the day's outcome.
