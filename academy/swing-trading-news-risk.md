# Swing Trading News Risk

This lesson is part of the **Swing Trading Workflow** course. It follows [Swing Trading Earnings](/academy/swing-trading-earnings/) and leads into [Small Cap Swing Trading](/academy/swing-trading-small-caps/).

News risk is the risk that new information changes the trade while a swing position is open.

This matters because swing trades cross sessions. A trader can enter with a clean chart and wake up to a different setup after a headline, filing, sector event, or broad-market move.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define news risk in swing trading.
- Understand why overnight headlines can change a trade.
- Review news with price reaction, levels, volume, and filings.
- Separate known event risk from unexpected news.
- Avoid treating news as automatically good or bad.

## Quick Definition

Swing trading news risk is the risk that new information changes a stock while the position is open across multiple sessions.

News risk can come from:

- Press releases.
- Earnings reports.
- SEC filings.
- Offering announcements.
- Merger or acquisition updates.
- Clinical trial or regulatory news.
- Contract announcements.
- Analyst updates.
- Sector headlines.
- Broad-market news.

Some news helps a trade. Some news hurts it. Some creates a fast reaction that fades.

## Known Risk Versus Surprise Risk

News risk is easier to plan when the event is known.

Known risk includes scheduled earnings, expected FDA dates, planned shareholder votes, conference presentations, or known economic events.

Surprise risk includes sudden offerings, unexpected filings, analyst actions, sector headlines, or company updates released after hours.

Swing traders cannot know every future headline, but they can avoid pretending overnight risk does not exist.

## How News Changes A Swing Trade

News can change:

- The trade thesis.
- The next opening price.
- Liquidity and spread.
- Volume and volatility.
- Support and resistance behavior.
- Market sentiment.
- The risk of holding longer.

A support zone that mattered yesterday may matter less after a material filing or earnings surprise. That does not mean chart structure becomes useless. It means the chart must be reviewed in light of new information.

## Realistic Example

A trader enters a swing trade after a stock breaks above resistance and closes strong. After hours, the company files a registration statement that raises dilution concerns.

The next morning, the stock opens lower, loses the breakout zone, and volume is heavy.

The review should ask:

- Did the trader check filing risk before entry?
- Was the position size appropriate for overnight exposure?
- Did the filing change the thesis?
- Did price lose the level that supported the trade?
- Did the trader respect the changed context?

The point is not to blame the news. The point is to review whether news risk was planned or ignored.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is holding overnight without checking known events.

Another mistake is reacting only to the headline instead of reading the source.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore SEC filings because there was no press release.

Another mistake is assuming news risk only applies to small caps. Sector and broad-market headlines can affect larger stocks too.

A final mistake is keeping the same thesis after new information changes the trade.

## Practical Checklist

- Known events are checked before holding overnight.
- Unexpected news is reviewed against the original thesis.
- Source documents are reviewed when available.
- Price reaction is compared with key levels.
- Volume and liquidity are reviewed after the news.
- Filing risk is checked for small-cap and catalyst trades.
- The trader knows whether the original plan still applies.

## Apply This In Review

- Was there known news risk before entry?
- Did unexpected news appear while I was holding?
- Did I read the source or only react to the headline?
- Did the news support, weaken, or change the thesis?
- Did price hold or lose the key level after the news?
- Did liquidity or spread change?
- Did I change the plan for a valid reason?
- Did I ignore the news because I was emotionally attached to the trade?

News-risk review helps traders separate market events from avoidable planning gaps.

## FAQ

### What is swing trading news risk?

Swing trading news risk is the risk that new information changes a stock while a swing trade is open across multiple sessions.

### Why does news risk matter for swing trades?

Swing trades are held overnight, so news can appear while the trader may not be able to exit at the planned price.

### Is news always good for a swing trade?

No. News can create upside, downside, volatility, confusion, or a short-lived reaction that fades.

### What news should swing traders watch?

Swing traders often watch earnings, filings, press releases, regulatory updates, financing news, merger news, sector news, and broad-market headlines.

### Can traders remove news risk?

No. They can reduce or plan for some known risks, but unexpected news can still appear.

### How should news-risk trades be reviewed?

Review the news source, timing, market reaction, key levels, volume, liquidity, thesis change, and whether decisions followed the original risk plan.
