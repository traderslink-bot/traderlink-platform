# Setup Screenshot Review

Setup screenshots help traders preserve context. A chart after the fact can feel obvious. A screenshot taken near the decision point can show what the trader actually saw.

The goal is not to collect pretty charts. The goal is to make decisions reviewable.

![Dark trading dashboard screenshot-style chart showing planned zones, a decision area, realistic candles, volume bars, and before-during-after review notes.](/academy/images/chart-reading/setup-screenshot-review.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Use screenshots as review evidence.
- Capture planned levels and actual decisions.
- Separate what was known before the trade from hindsight.
- Review execution and behavior visually.
- Build a screenshot routine that is useful but not overwhelming.

## Quick Definition

Setup screenshot review means saving chart images before, during, or after a decision so the trader can review the context later.

Useful screenshots may show:

- Planned support and resistance zones.
- Premarket or previous day levels.
- Volume and liquidity context.
- The decision area.
- The actual trade location.
- The failed or valid part of the idea.
- Notes about what changed.

A screenshot should answer a review question.

## What To Capture

A strong screenshot has enough context to review the decision:

- The timeframe.
- The important levels.
- The candle or area where the decision happened.
- Volume bars.
- Any relevant VWAP or reference line.
- The planned invalidation area.
- A short note about the reason.

Avoid screenshots that only show the final result.

## Before, During, After

Screenshots can be taken in three stages:

- Before: what was the plan?
- During: what changed as the trade developed?
- After: what actually happened?

The "before" screenshot is often the most valuable because it reduces hindsight.

## Realistic Example

A trader marks resistance, support, and the planned decision area before the open. Later, price pushes into the area but fails to hold. The screenshot shows that the actual decision happened after price was already extended from the original risk area.

The review note becomes specific: "The setup was identified, but the decision was late relative to the planned zone."

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is saving only the winning-looking chart after everything happened.

Another mistake is taking screenshots without notes.

Traders also make mistakes when they crop out volume, levels, or time of day.

Another mistake is collecting too many screenshots and never reviewing them.

A final mistake is using screenshots to prove a bias instead of studying the decision.

## FAQ

### Why use screenshots for trade review?

Screenshots preserve the chart context and make it easier to compare the plan with the actual decision.

### When should screenshots be taken?

Screenshots are useful before, during, and after a decision, especially before the full outcome is known.

### What should be included in a setup screenshot?

Include levels, volume, timeframe, decision area, and a short note about the plan.

### Can screenshots create hindsight bias?

Yes, especially if they are taken only after the result is obvious. Before screenshots reduce that problem.
