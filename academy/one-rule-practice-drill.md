# One-Rule Practice Drill

This lesson is part of the **Practice And Improvement** course. It follows [Trade Grading](/academy/trade-grading/) and leads into [Forward Testing Trading](/academy/forward-testing-trading/).

A one-rule practice drill is a focused practice session where the trader works on one rule only. It is simple on purpose.

Many traders try to fix everything at once. That usually makes review blurry. One-rule drills make improvement specific enough to track.

A drill result should be treated as evidence from a sample, not proof that a behavior is permanently fixed.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Create a one-rule practice drill.
- Choose a rule that is specific enough to review.
- Track rule adherence without overcomplicating it.
- Use mistakes as feedback.
- Connect the drill to future live or simulated sessions.

## Quick Definition

A one-rule practice drill is a focused trading practice exercise built around one reviewable rule.

Examples:

- No simulated trade without defined risk.
- No new ticker unless catalyst and spread are reviewed.
- No midday trade unless volume returns.
- No decision after two consecutive rule breaks.
- Screenshot before decision, not after outcome.
- Grade every trade by process before checking the final result.

The rule should be observable.

## How To Build The Drill

Choose one behavior that keeps repeating.

Then write the drill like this:

- Rule: what behavior will be practiced?
- Session: when will it be practiced?
- Evidence: what shows the rule was followed?
- Review: what question will be answered after?
- Adjustment: what changes if the rule is broken?

The drill should be easy to repeat for several sessions.

## Good Rules Versus Vague Rules

Vague rules are hard to practice.

Vague:

- Be more disciplined.
- Stop chasing.
- Trade better.

Reviewable:

- Do not take a simulated decision unless the invalidation area is written first.
- Do not add a new watchlist name without catalyst, volume, spread, and level notes.
- Do not take a third trade after two low-grade trades in the same session.

Specific rules create better feedback.

## Realistic Example

A trader notices that most weak trades come from late scanner alerts. The one-rule drill becomes: "For five sessions, no new ticker from a scanner can be practiced unless catalyst, spread, liquidity, and nearest level are written first."

The review is simple. Did the trader follow the rule? Did the rule reduce reactive decisions? Did the trader feel less urgency?

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is choosing a rule that is too broad.

Another mistake is changing the rule after one session.

Traders also make mistakes when they track too many metrics for a simple drill.

Another mistake is treating a broken rule as failure instead of feedback.

A final mistake is practicing the rule only on easy charts.

## Practical Checklist

- The rule is specific and observable.
- The drill has a defined session or sample size.
- The trader knows what evidence to collect.
- The drill connects to a real mistake pattern.
- The rule is not changed after one trade.
- Broken rules are reviewed without shame.
- The drill ends with one adjustment or continuation decision.

## Apply This In Review

- What rule did I practice?
- Did I follow it?
- If not, when did it break?
- What triggered the break?
- Did the rule reduce a repeated mistake?
- Was the rule too vague or too strict?
- Should I repeat, adjust, or replace the drill?

## FAQ

### What is a one-rule practice drill?

It is a focused practice exercise where the trader works on one specific, reviewable rule for a defined sample.

### Why practice only one rule?

Practicing one rule makes improvement easier to measure and reduces confusion from trying to fix everything at once.

### What makes a good practice rule?

A good rule is specific, observable, connected to a real mistake pattern, and simple enough to repeat.

### What if I break the rule?

Review when and why it happened. A broken rule is feedback, not a reason to abandon the entire drill.
