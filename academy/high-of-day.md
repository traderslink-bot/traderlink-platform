# High of Day: How Traders Review HOD Levels

High of day is the highest price a stock has reached during the current trading session. Traders often shorten it to HOD.

For most intraday stock charts, HOD is reviewed inside the active regular session unless the chart or scanner is explicitly set to include extended-hours prints.

High of day is the current session reference level. New high of day, covered later, is the live event of price pushing above that reference level and often triggering scanner or momentum attention.

Goal: Learn how high of day acts as a current-session reference level and why it can attract chase behavior.

HOD is one of the most visible intraday levels. It can help traders review strength, breakout attempts, failed breakouts, and chase risk. It does not guarantee continuation.

## What To Know First

Before studying high of day, remember:

- HOD means the current session's highest traded price.
- HOD updates when price trades above the prior intraday high.
- HOD may overlap with premarket high, previous day high, or resistance.
- A HOD break can hold, fail, reject, or become extended.
- Volume, spread, liquidity, and nearby higher-timeframe levels matter.
- A HOD alert is context, not a trade plan.

The goal is to review how price behaved around the level, not to treat the level as a command.

## What It Means

High of day is the highest price traded so far during the current session.

If your scanner includes premarket or after-hours prints, confirm that before treating an alert as a regular-session HOD event.

Traders watch HOD because it can become:

- An intraday resistance level.
- A breakout reference area.
- A failed breakout area.
- A retest level after a break.
- A place where late traders may chase.
- A review point for extension risk.

![Candlestick chart showing high of day as an intraday reference level with nearby PMH and PDH context.](/academy/images/chart-reading/high-of-day-level-map.svg)

The visual above shows HOD as part of the intraday map. It is not isolated. Premarket high, previous day high, current volume, and the distance from support can all change the quality of the HOD test.

## Why HOD Matters

High of day matters because it tells traders where the current session's strongest price point is so far.

Useful review questions include:

- Is price approaching HOD after building structure below it?
- Is price already extended into HOD?
- Is volume increasing or fading into the test?
- Is HOD close to premarket high or previous day high?
- Does price hold above HOD after breaking it?
- Does price spike above HOD and fail?

For active traders, HOD can be exciting. That excitement is exactly why review matters.

## How HOD Forms And Updates

During the session, HOD updates whenever price trades above the prior intraday high.

For example:

1. A stock opens at $3.00.
2. It pushes to $3.45.
3. $3.45 becomes HOD.
4. It pulls back to $3.20.
5. If price later trades above $3.45, a new HOD forms.

The first HOD can become a reference level. A later new HOD is a breakout attempt through that reference level.

## Failed HOD Breakout

A failed HOD breakout happens when price pushes above high of day but cannot hold above it.

![Candlestick chart showing price breaking above high of day and failing back below the level.](/academy/images/chart-reading/high-of-day-failed-breakout.svg)

This does not mean the stock must reverse. It means the HOD interaction became a review point.

Useful questions include:

- Was HOD marked before the trade?
- Did price build a base under HOD or spike straight into it?
- Did volume expand on the break or fade?
- Did price hold above HOD after breaking it?
- Was there nearby PMH, PDH, or daily resistance above?
- Did the trader chase after the move was already extended?

Failed HOD breakouts are useful for reviewing FOMO because they often happen when traders react to a fresh high without checking location.

## Extension Risk Near HOD

A stock can be strong and still be extended.

Extension risk near HOD can show up when:

- Price is far above the nearest support or higher low.
- The move into HOD is vertical.
- Volume spikes late after most of the move is done.
- HOD sits directly under premarket high or previous day high.
- The spread is wide or liquidity is thin.
- The trade idea depends on immediate continuation.

Reviewing extension risk helps separate planned breakout trading from chasing.

## Example Chart Read

A stock opens at $3.00, runs to $3.45, pulls back to $3.20, then grinds back toward $3.45 with higher lows.

A trader may watch $3.45 as HOD and ask:

- Did price build support below HOD?
- Is volume increasing into the test?
- Is the spread manageable?
- If price breaks $3.45, does it hold above it?
- If it fails, where is the nearest support?
- Is PMH or PDH just above the level?

A clean HOD break and hold is very different from a quick spike above HOD that immediately rejects.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes around high of day include:

- Chasing a new HOD after a stock is already extended.
- Assuming every HOD break will continue.
- Ignoring nearby daily or premarket resistance.
- Treating a scanner alert as a full trade idea.
- Entering far above the level that made the trade attractive.
- Holding a failed HOD breakout with no plan.
- Ignoring volume, spread, or liquidity near the level.
- Calling any new high a quality setup without reviewing the structure below it.

HOD is useful only when it improves the plan and the review.

## What To Check On The Chart

Before using HOD in a plan or review, ask:

1. Where is the current HOD?
2. Was HOD marked before the trade?
3. Did price build structure below HOD?
4. Is price extended from the nearest support or higher low?
5. Is volume expanding or fading into the test?
6. Is PMH, PDH, or daily resistance nearby?
7. Did price hold above HOD after breaking it?
8. What was the plan if HOD failed?
9. Was the entry planned or triggered by an alert?
10. Did the trader adapt when the level failed?

This checklist keeps HOD review practical.

## Review This In Your Journal

High-of-day trades are worth reviewing because they often reveal chasing, FOMO, and breakout discipline issues.

Ask:

- Did I enter before the HOD break, on the break, or after the stock was already extended?
- Was there a base or consolidation below HOD?
- Did volume support the breakout attempt?
- Did price hold above HOD after breaking it?
- Did I ignore nearby resistance above HOD?
- Did I take profit into strength or hold through a failed breakout?
- Was this a planned trade or a reaction to a scanner alert?
- Do my worst trades happen after late HOD alerts?

Reviewing these details helps separate strong execution from emotional entry timing.

## FAQ

### What does high of day mean in trading?

High of day is the highest price a stock has traded at so far during the current trading session.

### What does HOD mean?

HOD stands for high of day. It is a common trading abbreviation for the current session's highest price.

### Why do traders watch high of day?

Traders watch HOD because it can act as an intraday resistance level, breakout reference area, failed-breakout review point, or momentum reference point.

### Does a new high of day mean a stock will keep going?

No. A new high of day can continue, but it can also fail quickly if volume fades, the stock is extended, or sellers step in.

### Is high of day the same as premarket high?

Not always. High of day usually refers to the current regular-session high, while premarket high refers to the highest price before the regular market opens.

### How should traders review HOD trades?

Review whether the entry was planned, whether price was extended, whether volume supported the move, and whether the breakout held or failed.
