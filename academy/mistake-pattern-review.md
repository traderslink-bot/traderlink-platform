# Mistake Pattern Review

This lesson is part of the **Trade Review And Improvement** course. It follows [Execution Review](/academy/execution-review/) and leads into [Building A Playbook From Reviewed Trades](/academy/building-a-playbook-from-reviewed-trades/).

Mistake pattern review is the process of looking across trades to find repeated behaviors. One mistake may be noise. A repeated mistake is a pattern that can be named, tracked, and improved.

The point is not to shame the trader. The point is to stop treating repeated decisions as random.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define mistake patterns in a practical way.
- Separate one-off mistakes from repeated behavior.
- Review common patterns such as chasing, overtrading, revenge trading, and early exits.
- Turn repeated mistakes into clearer rules or checklist items.
- Use review language that is honest without being vague or harsh.

## Quick Definition

A mistake pattern is a repeated trading behavior that weakens the trader's process.

- Late entries after alerts.
- Oversizing after losses.
- Moving stops.
- Holding losers after invalidation.
- Cutting winners without a plan.
- Overtrading during low-quality periods.
- Ignoring liquidity or spread.
- Changing the plan during emotional moments.

A mistake pattern is useful only if it is specific enough to review.

## Why It Matters

Most traders already know when a single trade felt bad. The harder part is seeing repetition across many trades. Pattern review helps traders find the small set of behaviors that may be causing a large share of frustration.

A clear pattern can become a rule. A vague complaint usually cannot.

## Practical Review Process

A useful mistake pattern review looks across multiple trades:

- Tag the mistake plainly after each trade.
- Group similar mistakes by behavior.
- Look for when the pattern appears: time of day, ticker type, after losses, after wins, or during news.
- Measure whether the pattern changed risk, execution, or management.
- Write a rule that would interrupt the pattern.
- Review whether the rule helped over the next set of trades.

Patterns should be reviewed over samples, not from one emotional trade.

## Realistic Example

A trader notices five trades in two weeks had the note "entered late." Looking closer, all five happened after scanner alerts on fast-moving small-cap stocks.

The pattern is not simply "bad entries." It is "late scanner entries after the first move is already extended."

That specific pattern can lead to a specific rule: no entry after the second extended candle unless price resets near a planned level and risk is defined.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is using labels that are too vague, such as "bad discipline." The review needs the actual behavior.

Another mistake is overreacting to one trade instead of waiting for a pattern.

Traders also make mistakes when they track mistakes but never create a rule from them.

Another mistake is treating all mistakes equally. Some mistakes are annoying; others expand risk dramatically.

A final mistake is reviewing with shame language. Shame makes review harder to repeat. Specific language works better.

## Practical Checklist

- The mistake is named as a behavior, not a personality trait.
- The same behavior appeared across multiple trades.
- The context of the pattern is recorded.
- The effect on risk, execution, or management is clear.
- A rule or checklist item is created from the pattern.
- Future trades are reviewed against that rule.
- The review stays specific and usable.

## Apply This In Review

- What mistake repeated?
- How many times did it appear?
- When did it appear?
- What triggered it?
- Did it change risk, entry, exit, or management?
- Was it connected to a specific ticker type or time of day?
- What rule would interrupt it?
- Did the new rule reduce the pattern?

The goal is not to eliminate every mistake immediately. The goal is to make the most important repeated mistakes visible enough to work on.

## FAQ

### What is a trading mistake pattern?

It is a repeated behavior that weakens trade quality, risk control, execution, or management.

### Is one mistake a pattern?

Not usually. One mistake can be reviewed, but a pattern appears when similar behavior repeats.

### How should mistakes be labeled?

Use specific behavior labels such as late entry, moved stop, oversized, chased, overtraded, or held after invalidation.

### Why avoid shame language?

Shame language is vague and discouraging. Specific behavior language is easier to review and improve.

### What should I do after finding a pattern?

Create a rule, checklist item, or session limit that directly addresses the repeated behavior.

### Can pattern review guarantee improvement?

No. It can improve awareness and feedback, but the trader still needs to apply the rules.
