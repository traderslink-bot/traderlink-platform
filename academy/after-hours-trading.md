# After-Hours Trading

This lesson is part of the **Day Trading Workflow** course. It follows [Power Hour Trading](/academy/power-hour-trading/) and leads into [Day Trading Session Review](/academy/day-trading-session-review/).

After-hours trading happens after the regular session closes. Stocks can move after the bell because of earnings, press releases, SEC filings, analyst updates, sector news, or trader reaction to the regular session.

After-hours can look exciting, but it is often thinner and less forgiving than regular-hours trading. The chart may move quickly while the actual liquidity is limited.

![After-hours candlestick chart showing headline reaction, sparse candles, wide spread review, and lower-volume context.](/images/academy/chart-reading/after-hours-liquidity-context.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define after-hours trading.
- Understand why liquidity and spreads can change after the close.
- Review after-hours headline reactions with source context.
- Recognize overnight-risk decisions.
- Avoid assuming after-hours movement will hold into the next session.

## Quick Definition

After-hours trading is extended-hours trading that happens after the regular market closes.

Traders often watch:

- Earnings reactions.
- Late press releases.
- SEC filings.
- Offering or dilution headlines.
- Biotech updates.
- Sector news.
- Sparse volume and wider spreads.
- Possible overnight exposure.

After-hours movement is context, not a guarantee for the next day.

Exact after-hours access, order types, routing, and available symbols can vary by broker and venue. Treat after-hours prices as extended-hours context, not as a promise of where regular-session trading will open.

## How This Fits The Workflow

After-hours is both a trading segment and a review segment.

The trader should ask:

- What caused the move?
- Was the source read or only the headline?
- Is liquidity good enough for the plan?
- How wide is the spread?
- Is there realistic exit quality?
- Would holding beyond the close create overnight risk?
- Does this become a watchlist item for the next session?

This part of the workflow connects news review, risk review, and next-day preparation.

## When It Can Mislead

After-hours can mislead when price moves on limited volume. A few prints can create a dramatic candle. A headline can create an initial reaction before traders read the full filing, guidance, or risk language.

It can also mislead when a trader assumes the reaction will hold into the next day. Regular-hours participation can change the chart completely.

## Realistic Example

A company releases earnings after the close. The stock spikes on the headline, then fades as traders read weaker guidance. Volume is active for a few minutes, then thins out. The spread widens.

A prepared trader reviews the source, spread, liquidity, and overnight plan before making any decision. The next-day watchlist note is based on the full reaction, not just the first candle.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is reacting to the headline before reading details.

Another mistake is ignoring wide spreads after the close.

Traders also make mistakes when they assume after-hours movement will continue the next day.

Another mistake is holding overnight without a defined plan.

A final mistake is overlooking filing or dilution risk in small-cap names.

## Practical Checklist

- The news or filing source is reviewed.
- The spread and liquidity are acceptable for the plan.
- Volume is checked after the initial reaction.
- The trader understands overnight risk.
- The move is not treated as a next-day guarantee.
- Any next-day watchlist note includes levels and risk context.
- The after-hours decision can be reviewed clearly.

## Apply This In Review

- What caused the after-hours move?
- Did I read the full source?
- Did spread affect execution?
- Was liquidity good enough?
- Did volume continue or fade?
- Did I hold overnight with a plan?
- Did after-hours activity fit my workflow or interrupt it?

## FAQ

### What is after-hours trading?

After-hours trading is extended-hours trading that happens after the regular market session closes.

### Why do stocks move after hours?

Stocks can move after hours because of earnings, filings, press releases, analyst updates, sector news, and trader reaction.

### Is after-hours trading risky?

Yes. After-hours trading can have lower liquidity, wider spreads, and sharper movement on fewer shares.

### Can after-hours moves reverse the next day?

Yes. Regular-hours volume can change or reverse the after-hours reaction.
