# Piercing Line

A piercing line is a two-candle rebound pattern. The green candle closes back into the prior red candle body.

![](/academy/images/chart-reading/candle-behavior/piercing-line-shape.svg)

## At A Glance

* **Name:** Piercing Line
* **Category:** Bullish Candle Patterns
* **Type:** Bullish Reversal
* **Number of candles:** 2 candles
* **Typical context:** After selling pressure or near a lower support area.

## How To Identify It

* **Body:** First candle is red. Second candle is green and closes meaningfully into the prior red body.
* **Wicks:** Wicks are secondary. The close into the prior body is the key.
* **Relationship:** The green candle does not need to cover the full red body like a bullish engulfing candle.

The fastest way to identify Piercing Line is to start with the shape first, then check whether the surrounding candles match the context.

## Context

This pattern is most useful when a weak open or lower push gets bought and price closes back into the prior selling candle.

Context does not replace the candle shape. It explains why the same shape can matter more in one area than another.

## What It Shows

Sellers had control first, but buyers recovered enough of the prior body to change the pressure.

## What To Watch Next

Watch whether price can continue above the midpoint or upper part of the prior red body.

The read weakens if price cannot hold inside the prior red body.

## Common Confusion

Piercing line is weaker visually than bullish engulfing because it only moves into the prior body.

## Key Takeaway

Piercing line shows a rebound into prior selling pressure.

## FAQ

### How many candles form Piercing Line?

Piercing Line uses 2 candles.

### What should I look at first?

Start with body size, wick direction, and where the candle sits in the recent price action.

### What makes the read weaker?

The read weakens when the next candles immediately move against the pattern and fail to respect the key candle area.
