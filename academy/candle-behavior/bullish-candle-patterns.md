# Bullish Candle Patterns

Bullish candle patterns show moments where buyers step in, absorb selling, or continue pushing price higher.

The useful read is the behavior inside the candle sequence:

* Did price sell down and get bought back up?
* Did a green candle close strongly after a red candle?
* Did buyers take back most or all of the prior candle body?
* Did a pause turn into several strong green candles?

## Bottoming Tail Family

A bottoming tail is a family term for candles with a long lower wick and a body near the upper part of the candle range.

The behavior is simple: price traded lower, then buyers pushed it back up before the candle closed.

This family includes lower-wick rejection candles such as hammers, bullish pin bars, and dragonfly-style lower rejection candles.

## Hammer

A hammer has a small body near the top of the candle range and a long lower wick.

The shape shows that sellers pushed price down during the candle, but buyers reclaimed much of that move before the close.

For beginners, the fastest way to identify it is:

* Small body near the high
* Long lower wick
* Little or no upper wick

## Inverted Hammer

An inverted hammer has a small body near the lower part of the candle range and a long upper wick.

It shows that buyers made an upside push after weakness, even if the candle did not close near the high.

The shape is different from a regular hammer. A hammer has the long wick below the body. An inverted hammer has the long wick above the body.

## Bullish Engulfing

A bullish engulfing pattern uses two candles.

The first candle is red. The second candle is green, and the green body covers the prior red body.

The behavior is a shift from seller control to buyer control across two candles.

## Piercing Line

A piercing line also uses two candles.

The first candle is red. The second candle opens lower or near the lower part of the prior candle, then closes back into the prior red body.

It does not need to cover the whole prior body like an engulfing candle. The key behavior is a strong rebound into the prior selling candle.

## Morning Star

A morning star uses three candles.

The first candle is a strong red candle. The second candle is small and shows a pause. The third candle is a strong green candle that pushes back into the first candle's range.

The sequence shows selling, hesitation, then buyer control.

## Three White Soldiers

Three white soldiers are three strong green candles in a row.

Each candle shows continued buyer pressure. The pattern is easiest to see when the candles have meaningful bodies and close near their highs.

## Related Lower-Rejection Behavior

Bullish pin bars and dragonfly-style lower rejection candles belong near the bottoming-tail family.

They share the same visual idea: a small body with a dominant lower wick. The name can change by charting style, market, or trader preference, but the behavior is lower rejection and buyer reclaim.

## How To Read The Group

Start with the candle shape before the name.

Bullish candle behavior usually comes from one of three visual ideas:

* A long lower wick that shows buyers stepping in after a push down
* A green candle that takes back a prior red candle
* A multi-candle sequence where selling slows and green candles expand

## Key Takeaway

Bullish candle patterns are easier to learn when grouped by behavior: lower rejection, reclaim, engulfing pressure, and multi-candle buyer continuation.

## FAQ

### Is a hammer a bottoming tail candle?

Yes. A hammer is a common type of bottoming-tail behavior because it has a small body near the top of the range and a long lower wick.

### What makes a bullish engulfing candle different?

It needs two candles. The green body of the second candle covers the body of the prior red candle.

### Are bullish pin bars related to bottoming tails?

Yes. A bullish pin bar is another lower-rejection shape with a small body and a dominant lower wick.
