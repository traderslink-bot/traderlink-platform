# Bearish Candle Patterns

Bearish candle patterns show moments where sellers step in, reject higher prices, or keep pressure on the stock.

The useful read is the behavior inside the candle sequence:

* Did price push up and get rejected?
* Did a red candle close strongly after a green candle?
* Did sellers take back most or all of the prior candle body?
* Did a pause turn into several strong red candles?

## Topping Tail Family

A topping tail is a family term for candles with a long upper wick and a body near the lower part of the candle range.

The behavior is simple: price traded higher, then sellers pushed it back down before the candle closed.

This family includes upper-wick rejection candles such as shooting stars, bearish pin bars, and gravestone-style upper rejection candles.

## Shooting Star

A shooting star has a small body near the lower part of the range and a long upper wick.

The shape shows that buyers pushed price higher during the candle, but sellers rejected that push before the close.

For beginners, the fastest way to identify it is:

* Small body near the low
* Long upper wick
* Little or no lower wick

## Hanging Man

A hanging man has a small body near the top of the range and a long lower wick.

Its shape can look like a hammer. The difference is how traders discuss the behavior around it: a hanging man appears after an upward move and shows a sharp dip inside the candle before the close.

The visual read is still the same candle shape: small body near the high, long lower wick.

## Bearish Engulfing

A bearish engulfing pattern uses two candles.

The first candle is green. The second candle is red, and the red body covers the prior green body.

The behavior is a shift from buyer control to seller control across two candles.

## Dark Cloud Cover

Dark cloud cover also uses two candles.

The first candle is green. The second candle opens higher or near the upper part of the prior candle, then closes down into the prior green body.

It does not need to cover the whole prior body like a bearish engulfing candle. The key behavior is a strong push down into the prior buying candle.

## Evening Star

An evening star uses three candles.

The first candle is a strong green candle. The second candle is small and shows a pause. The third candle is a strong red candle that pushes back into the first candle's range.

The sequence shows buying, hesitation, then seller control.

## Three Black Crows

Three black crows are three strong red candles in a row.

Each candle shows continued seller pressure. The pattern is easiest to see when the candles have meaningful bodies and close near their lows.

## Related Upper-Rejection Behavior

Bearish pin bars and gravestone-style upper rejection candles belong near the topping-tail family.

They share the same visual idea: a small body with a dominant upper wick. The name can change by charting style, market, or trader preference, but the behavior is upper rejection and seller pressure.

## How To Read The Group

Start with the candle shape before the name.

Bearish candle behavior usually comes from one of three visual ideas:

* A long upper wick that shows sellers rejecting a push higher
* A red candle that takes back a prior green candle
* A multi-candle sequence where buying stalls and red candles expand

## Key Takeaway

Bearish candle patterns are easier to learn when grouped by behavior: upper rejection, failed pushes, engulfing pressure, and multi-candle selling.

## FAQ

### Is a shooting star a topping tail candle?

Yes. A shooting star is a common type of topping-tail behavior because it has a small body near the low of the range and a long upper wick.

### Why does a hanging man look like a hammer?

They share the same basic shape. The difference is the surrounding chart behavior traders associate with the candle.

### What makes a bearish engulfing candle different?

It needs two candles. The red body of the second candle covers the body of the prior green candle.
