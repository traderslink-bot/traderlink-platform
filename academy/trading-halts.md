# Trading Halts

This lesson opens the **Halts And High-Volatility Events** course. It follows [Trading Improvement Plan](/academy/trading-improvement-plan/) and leads into [Volatility Halts](/academy/volatility-halts/).

A trading halt is a temporary pause in trading for a security or, in extreme cases, across the broader market. Halts can happen for different reasons, including volatility, pending news, regulatory concerns, order imbalances, or market-wide circuit breaker rules.

The important lesson is risk. A halt can interrupt a plan, freeze exits, widen uncertainty, and change execution conditions after trading resumes.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define a trading halt.
- Understand why halts can happen.
- Separate halts, delays, and SEC trading suspensions at a high level.
- Review halt risk without treating the halt as a signal.
- Connect halt events to risk, liquidity, spread, and trade review.

## Quick Definition

A trading halt is a temporary pause in trading.

Halts may relate to:

- Rapid volatility.
- Pending news.
- Order imbalance.
- Regulatory review.
- Exchange procedures.
- Market-wide circuit breaker rules.

The reason matters because not all halts mean the same thing.

## Halts Are Not All The Same

Different halt types can have different causes and procedures.

- A volatility halt may happen because price moved too quickly.
- A news-pending halt may happen before important information is released.
- A regulatory halt may involve questions or concerns about the issuer or trading.
- An SEC trading suspension is a separate regulatory action and can last longer than a typical exchange halt.
- A market-wide circuit breaker affects broad market trading during severe market declines.

Traders should check official exchange or regulatory notices instead of guessing from the chart.

Trading halts, trading delays, and regulatory trading suspensions are not the same thing. Listed-stock halts are generally issued by the listing exchange and observed across other U.S. markets, while regulatory trading suspensions are separate actions that can last up to 10 trading days. For OTC securities, quoting may not automatically resume after a suspension ends.

## Why Halts Matter To Traders

Halts matter because they interrupt control.

During a halt, normal trading may not be available. When trading resumes, price can gap, spreads can widen, liquidity can change quickly, and orders may not behave the way a calm chart suggests.

That makes halt review part of risk management, not only chart reading.

## When It Can Mislead

A halt can mislead when traders treat it as proof that a move will continue. A stock can halt after strong upward movement and reopen lower. It can halt after a sharp drop and reopen higher. It can reopen into a wide spread, thin depth, or choppy movement.

The halt itself does not guarantee direction.

## Realistic Example

A low-float stock runs quickly after a news headline. Volume spikes, candles expand, and the stock halts. During the halt, traders cannot rely on normal exits. When the stock resumes, the spread is wider and the first candles are unstable.

The review question is not "Was the halt good or bad?" The review question is whether the trader understood halt risk before the trade and whether the plan accounted for resume uncertainty.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is assuming a halt means continuation.

Another mistake is entering a fast move without considering the possibility of a halt.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore the official halt reason.

Another mistake is assuming resume liquidity will be clean.

A final mistake is using too much size in a name that can halt quickly.

## Practical Checklist

- The trader understands the possible halt type.
- Official halt or exchange information is checked when relevant.
- Position size accounts for interruption risk.
- Spread and liquidity are reviewed before and after the halt.
- The trader does not assume resume direction.
- The trade plan includes what happens if exits are delayed.
- The event is reviewed after the session.

## FAQ

### What is a trading halt?

A trading halt is a temporary pause in trading for a security or, in some cases, across a broader market.

### Does a halt mean a stock will continue moving?

No. A halt does not guarantee continuation, reversal, or any specific outcome.

### Can traders exit during a halt?

Normal trading is paused during a halt, so exit control can be limited until trading resumes.

### Where should traders check halt information?

Traders should check official exchange, FINRA, or SEC sources where applicable.
