# Day Trading

Day trading means opening and closing a position during the same trading session. It is a style built around intraday movement, execution quality, risk control, and review.

Day trading can look fast from the outside, but good day trading is not random speed. It is preparation, selection, execution, and stopping when the plan is gone.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define day trading as a trading style.
- Understand why intraday risk and execution matter.
- Recognize time-of-day and liquidity pressure.
- Separate planned day trades from reactive trades.
- Review day trades by plan, risk, execution, and behavior.

## Quick Definition

Day trading is a trading style where positions are opened and closed during the same market session.

- Trades may last seconds, minutes, or hours.
- The trader usually does not intend to hold overnight.
- Intraday levels, volume, spreads, and news can matter.
- Fast movement can increase slippage and emotional pressure.
- The session ends with review rather than open exposure.

The key is not activity. The key is structured intraday decision-making.

Account rules, margin requirements, and broker policies can affect day trading, so those details should always be checked against current broker and regulatory materials. This lesson focuses on the trading style itself: intraday planning, risk, execution, and review.

## How This Style Works

Day trading usually starts before entry:

- Build a watchlist or identify active names.
- Review catalysts, volume, liquidity, and levels.
- Choose the setup category being watched.
- Define risk before entry.
- Manage the trade during the session.
- Review whether trades were planned or reactive.

## What This Style Requires

Day trading requires attention and boundaries.

- A plan for what qualifies as a trade.
- Defined risk and position sizing.
- Awareness of spread and slippage.
- Time-of-day review.
- A stopping rule after mistakes or losses.
- Honest separation between planned trades and impulse trades.

## When It Can Mislead

Day trading can mislead when traders confuse activity with progress. Taking many trades does not mean the trader is improving. Fast movement can make low-quality decisions feel urgent.

## Realistic Example

A stock gaps up on news and appears on scanners. Before the open, the trader maps premarket high, support, liquidity, spread, and invalidation. At the open, price moves quickly. The review question is not only whether the trade made money; it is whether the trader followed the intraday plan or reacted after the move was already extended.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is trading every scanner alert.

Another mistake is entering before risk is defined.

Traders also make mistakes when they chase high-of-day or low-of-day moves after the clean entry is gone.

Another mistake is overtrading after a loss.

A final mistake is turning a failed day trade into an overnight hold.

## FAQ

### What is day trading?

Day trading is opening and closing a position during the same trading session.

### Is day trading the same as investing?

No. Day trading focuses on intraday decisions, while investing usually uses a longer-term ownership thesis.

### Why is day trading risky?

Intraday moves can be fast, spreads can widen, liquidity can change, and emotional decisions can compound quickly.

### What should day traders review?

Review plan quality, timing, execution, risk, spread, slippage, and whether decisions were planned or reactive.

### Can a day trade become a swing trade?

It can, but changing timeframe after the original idea fails is a common style-drift mistake.

### Does day trading guarantee profits?

No. Day trading can lead to losses and requires risk management and review.
