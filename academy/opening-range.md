# Opening Range

The opening range is the high and low formed during the first part of the regular session. Traders use it to organize the early volatility into reference levels they can review.

The opening range does not guarantee continuation or reversal. It gives the trader a cleaner way to ask what happened after the open.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define the opening range.
- Understand why the first minutes can be noisy.
- Mark opening range high and low areas.
- Review holds, failures, reclaims, and rejections without certainty.
- Avoid treating the range as a mechanical signal.

## Quick Definition

The opening range is the price range formed during an early part of the regular session, often measured by the first several minutes.

It can include:

- Opening range high.
- Opening range low.
- Volume burst at the bell.
- Premarket levels nearby.
- Previous day levels nearby.
- VWAP or other intraday references.
- Failed extension above or below the range.

The time window should be consistent enough to review.

## Why It Matters

The open can be chaotic. Orders, news reactions, premarket positioning, and fresh liquidity arrive together.

The opening range helps the trader slow the session down. Instead of reacting to every candle, the trader can ask:

- Where did the first range form?
- Did price hold inside it?
- Did an attempted move outside the range hold or fail?
- Was volume expanding or fading?
- Was the trade taken near a realistic risk area?

## When It Can Mislead

The opening range can mislead when traders treat the first break as automatic. A move outside the range can fail quickly, especially if the stock is extended, the spread is wide, or volume fades after the first push.

It can also mislead when the opening range is too wide to use responsibly. If the risk area is far away, the setup may not fit the trader's plan.

## Realistic Example

A stock gaps up and trades heavily at the bell. The first candles create a range between the premarket high area and a nearby support zone. Price briefly moves above the opening range high, but volume fades and the next candles fall back inside the range.

The review question is not whether the opening range "worked." The review question is whether the trader waited for structure, respected the failed hold, and sized for open volatility.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is reacting to the first candle without letting the range form.

Another mistake is using a random opening-range window that changes depending on the trade.

Traders also make mistakes when they ignore premarket high, premarket low, or previous day levels near the range.

Another mistake is entering far away from a realistic invalidation area.

A final mistake is staying involved after the opening-range idea has clearly failed.

## FAQ

### What is the opening range?

The opening range is the high and low formed during the first part of the regular trading session.

### Does an opening range break guarantee continuation?

No. Opening-range moves can fail, reverse, or become choppy.

### How long should the opening range be?

Traders use different windows. The important part is using a consistent window that can be reviewed.

### Why is volume important at the opening range?

Volume helps show whether attention is expanding or fading around early reference levels.
