# Day Trading Workflow

This lesson opens the **Day Trading Workflow** course. It follows [VWAP Reclaim](/academy/chart-patterns/vwap-reclaim/) and leads into [Premarket Trading](/academy/premarket-trading/).

A day trading workflow is the repeatable process a trader uses before, during, and after the session. It is not a secret setup. It is the structure that keeps decisions connected to preparation, risk, execution, and review.

Without a workflow, the trading day can become a stream of alerts, candles, headlines, and emotions. With a workflow, each part of the session has a job.

![Dark trading dashboard showing a day trading session workflow across premarket, open, midday, power hour, and after-hours review.](/images/learn/chart-reading/day-trading-session-map.svg)

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what a day trading workflow is.
- Understand the purpose of each major session segment.
- Separate preparation, execution, and review.
- Recognize why time of day can change risk.
- Use the workflow as a review structure after trades close.

## Quick Definition

A day trading workflow is a repeatable session process for deciding what to watch, when to wait, how to manage risk, and how to review completed trades.

It usually includes:

- Premarket preparation.
- Watchlist filtering.
- Market open observation.
- Opening range review.
- Midday filtering.
- Late-day reassessment.
- After-hours risk review.
- End-of-session trade review.

The workflow does not predict the market. It helps the trader stay organized while the market changes.

## The Core Session Flow

Day trading is not one continuous decision. The session has different conditions.

- Premarket is for context, levels, catalysts, liquidity, and spread awareness.
- The open is for observing how regular-hours volume reacts.
- The opening range is for organizing early volatility into reviewable levels.
- Midday is for filtering lower-quality activity and avoiding boredom trades.
- Power hour is for reassessing whether volume and structure have returned.
- After-hours is for reviewing headline reactions, liquidity, and overnight risk.
- Session review is for studying what happened versus what was planned.

This course walks through those parts in order.

## What A Good Workflow Does

A useful workflow should answer practical questions before pressure rises.

- What is actually worth watching?
- What is the catalyst or reason for attention?
- Where are the key reference levels?
- Is liquidity good enough for the trader's plan?
- What time of day is it?
- Is the decision planned or reactive?
- What will be reviewed after the trade?

The workflow is successful when it makes the session easier to review, even if the day was messy.

## When It Can Mislead

A workflow can mislead when it becomes too rigid. Markets do not follow a classroom schedule. Some days move early and then fade. Some days do nothing until late. Some names trade cleanly while others are too thin or spread out to review well.

The workflow should guide attention, not force trades.

## Realistic Example

A stock gaps up before the open after a press release. Premarket volume is active, but the spread is wider than usual. The trader maps premarket high, premarket low, VWAP, previous day levels, and the first area where the idea would be wrong.

At the open, price moves quickly above the premarket high and then fails back inside the range. The workflow gives the trader a review path: Was the catalyst understood? Was the spread acceptable? Did the opening move hold? Was the entry planned or late? Did the trader keep trading after the plan failed?

The lesson is not that the move was good or bad. The lesson is that the decision can be reviewed.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is building the plan after the move is already happening.

Another mistake is treating every scanner alert as equally important.

Traders also make mistakes when they use the same risk during premarket, the open, midday, and after-hours even though conditions are different.

Another mistake is skipping review after a winning trade because the outcome felt fine.

A final mistake is using workflow language to justify a reactive trade.

## Practical Checklist

- The session has a clear preparation step.
- The watchlist is filtered instead of endless.
- Key levels and liquidity are reviewed before entry pressure rises.
- The trader understands the time-of-day context.
- The trade has a clear failure condition.
- The trader knows when to stop or reduce activity.
- Completed trades are reviewed by plan, risk, execution, and behavior.

## Apply This In Review

- Which part of the workflow produced the trade?
- Was the trade planned before the candle moved?
- Did the time of day increase risk?
- Was liquidity acceptable?
- Did I follow the failure condition?
- Did I keep trading after my best setup was gone?
- What workflow step needs to be tightened next session?

Reviewing the workflow makes the trading day less random.

## FAQ

### What is a day trading workflow?

A day trading workflow is a repeatable process for preparing, observing, managing risk, and reviewing trades during an intraday session.

### Is a workflow the same as a trading setup?

No. A setup describes a specific market condition. A workflow describes how the trader organizes the full session.

### Does a workflow guarantee better trading?

No. A workflow does not guarantee outcomes. It creates structure so decisions can be reviewed more clearly.

### Why does time of day matter?

Liquidity, volume, spread, volatility, and trader behavior can change throughout the session.
