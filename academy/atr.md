# ATR

This lesson is part of the **Technical Indicators And Tools** course. It follows [Bollinger Bands](/academy/bollinger-bands/) and leads into [Volume By Price](/academy/volume-by-price/).

ATR stands for Average True Range. It measures how much a stock has been moving over a selected period, which makes it useful for reviewing volatility and risk distance.

ATR does not say whether price should go up or down. It helps traders understand how large recent movement has been.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what ATR measures.
- Use ATR as volatility context.
- Connect ATR to risk distance and position review.
- Recognize when volatility expansion changes trade quality.
- Avoid treating ATR as a direction signal.

## Quick Definition

ATR is a volatility indicator that averages the true range of price movement over a selected period.

- Higher ATR means recent candles or sessions had larger ranges.
- Lower ATR means recent candles or sessions had smaller ranges.
- ATR can expand after news, gaps, or volatility events.
- ATR can contract during quieter periods.
- ATR describes movement size, not direction.

ATR is about range. It is not a prediction.

## What It Measures

ATR measures average range over recent periods. It does not measure whether a setup is valid, whether a catalyst is strong, or whether a stock will continue in one direction.

ATR starts with true range, which accounts for the current high-low range and gaps from the prior close, then smooths that range over a selected period. ATR is a volatility measure, not a direction measure, and values can vary by timeframe, lookback, smoothing, data history, and whether the platform displays ATR as an absolute value or percentage variant.

## How Traders Use It As Context

Traders may use ATR to review:

- Whether a stock was unusually volatile.
- Whether the stop distance was realistic for the stock's range.
- Whether position size matched volatility.
- Whether the trader chased after a range expansion.
- Whether the trade required more risk than planned.

## When It Can Mislead

ATR can mislead when traders treat a high ATR as opportunity without respecting risk. A stock moving more can create more volatility, but it can also increase slippage, stop distance, and emotional pressure.

## Realistic Example

A stock usually moves $0.30 in a session, then news pushes its ATR much higher. A trader uses the same share size and tight stop as usual, then gets shaken out by normal volatility for that day. The issue is not ATR predicting direction; the issue is risk not being adjusted to volatility.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is thinking high ATR means a better trade.

Another mistake is using normal position size when volatility has expanded.

Traders also make mistakes when they place stops too tight for the stock's current range.

Another mistake is ignoring slippage and spread in volatile names.

A final mistake is using ATR without reviewing price structure and levels.

## Practical Checklist

- I know whether ATR was elevated or quiet.
- I reviewed range expansion before sizing.
- I checked whether my stop distance made sense.
- I considered spread and slippage.
- I did not treat ATR as a direction signal.
- I reviewed whether volatility changed the trade quality.
- I connected ATR to risk, not excitement.

## Apply This In Review

- Was ATR elevated, normal, or compressed?
- Did volatility expand before entry?
- Was my stop distance realistic for the range?
- Was position size appropriate for volatility?
- Did I chase because the stock was moving more?
- Did spread or slippage matter?
- Did ATR help explain the outcome after the trade?

ATR review should make risk more honest, especially when volatility changes quickly.

## FAQ

### What is ATR?

ATR, or Average True Range, is a volatility indicator that measures average price range over a selected period.

### Does ATR show direction?

No. ATR measures range and volatility, not direction.

### How can ATR help risk management?

ATR can help traders review whether stop distance and position size fit current volatility.

### Is high ATR good?

High ATR means larger recent ranges. It can mean more movement, but also more risk, slippage, and emotional pressure.

### Can ATR be used alone?

ATR should be reviewed with price structure, levels, liquidity, spread, and a risk plan.

### How should ATR be reviewed?

Review whether volatility expanded or contracted, whether sizing matched range, and whether the trade respected realistic movement.
