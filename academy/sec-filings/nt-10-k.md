# NT 10-K

This lesson is part of the **News, Catalysts And SEC Filings** course. It follows [Form PRE 14A](/academy/sec-filings/form-pre-14a/) and leads into [NT 10-Q](/academy/sec-filings/nt-10-q/).

The goal is not to turn NT 10-K into a trading signal. The goal is to understand what the filing says, what it does not say, and how it changes the review context around news, price, volume, liquidity, and risk.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what NT 10-K is used for.
- Identify the parts of the filing that matter most for traders.
- Review late annual report notices, expected delay reasons, financial-reporting risk, and follow-up filings without assuming a guaranteed market reaction.
- Connect the filing to related news, chart reaction, liquidity, and risk context.
- Use the filing as part of a structured review process instead of a standalone prediction tool.

NT 10-K is an SEC filing used when a company is notifying the market that its annual Form 10-K will be filed late. Traders may see NT 10-K filings when a company cannot file its annual report by the regular deadline.

An NT 10-K is not automatically bullish or bearish. It does not guarantee bad financials, dilution, delisting, or a price move. It is a late filing notice, and traders need to review why the annual report is delayed and what the eventual 10-K shows.

## What This Filing Means

NT 10-K is commonly tied to Form 12b-25, which is used when certain SEC reports will be filed late.

An NT 10-K may include:

1. The report being delayed.
2. The reason for the delay.
3. Whether significant financial changes are expected.
4. A statement about whether the company expects to file within the allowed extension period.
5. Management's short explanation of the issue.

For traders, the filing can be an early signal to watch the actual 10-K closely.

## Why Companies File It

Companies file NT 10-K when they cannot complete their annual report on time.

Common reasons may include:

1. Audit delays.
2. Accounting issues.
3. Internal control problems.
4. Restatement work.
5. Complex transactions.
6. Late financial information.
7. Management or auditor delays.
8. Business combination or restructuring work.

Some delays are administrative. Others can point to deeper financial reporting issues.

## What Traders Usually Look For

Traders often review NT 10-K filings for the reason behind the delay.

Important areas include:

1. Explanation for late filing.
2. Whether management expects significant changes.
3. Whether audit issues are mentioned.
4. Whether restatement language appears.
5. Whether going concern risk may be involved.
6. Whether the company has prior late filings.
7. Whether exchange compliance could be affected.
8. Whether the actual 10-K is filed soon after.

This is where traders can get into trouble. A late annual report can be routine, but it can also flag accounting or financial stress.

## What It Does Not Automatically Mean

NT 10-K does not automatically mean fraud, bankruptcy, or delisting. It also does not automatically mean the stock will drop.

The filing means the annual report is late. The reason for the delay and the eventual 10-K are what traders need to review.

## Dilution Or Risk Considerations

NT 10-K is not usually a dilution filing, but it can matter for risk review.

Traders may want to check:

1. Prior cash position.
2. Going concern language in older filings.
3. Recent financing activity.
4. Debt or default language.
5. Exchange compliance risk.
6. Whether the eventual 10-K shows share count changes.
7. Whether the company files additional 8-K updates.

The filing does not guarantee dilution, but it can increase uncertainty until the annual report is filed.

## Practical Checklist

Structured review notes can help connect NT 10-K details to trading decisions.

Useful review notes include:

1. Filing date.
2. Reason for the annual report delay.
3. Whether significant changes were expected.
4. Prior cash and risk context.
5. Whether the company filed within the extension period.
6. What the eventual 10-K showed.
7. Stock reaction after the NT 10-K.
8. Whether you traded while annual financials were still pending.

This helps traders avoid ignoring reporting risk during active trades.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes with NT 10-K include:

- Reacting to the form name without reading the filing details.
- Assuming the filing is automatically bullish or bearish.
- Ignoring related filings, exhibits, amendments, or prospectus documents.
- Forgetting to compare the filing with price, volume, spread, and liquidity.
- Treating social-media summaries as enough without checking the source document.
- Failing to review what happened after the filing instead of only what the headline suggested.

## FAQ

### What is NT 10-K?

NT 10-K is a late filing notice used when a company cannot file its annual Form 10-K on time.

### Does NT 10-K mean bad news?

Not automatically. It means the annual report is late. Traders need to review the reason for the delay and the eventual 10-K.

### Is NT 10-K the same as Form 10-K?

No. NT 10-K is a notice that the Form 10-K will be filed late. Form 10-K is the actual annual report.

### What should traders look for in NT 10-K?

Traders should review the reason for delay, whether significant changes are expected, audit or accounting language, and whether the company expects to file within the extension period.

### Can NT 10-K affect listing compliance?

It can in some situations, especially if reporting delays continue. Traders should review company and exchange notices for context.

### Why does the eventual 10-K matter most?

The eventual 10-K contains the full annual report. It may confirm whether the delay was minor or tied to larger financial or reporting issues.

## Apply This In Review

When reviewing a trade or watchlist idea connected to NT 10-K, ask:

- What exact filing was released, and what event or disclosure did it contain?
- Was the market reacting to the filing itself, a related press release, or a prior expectation?
- What did price and volume do before, during, and after the filing became visible?
- Did liquidity, spread, or volatility make the move harder to execute cleanly?
- Did I read the filing details, or did I rely on a headline, scanner alert, or social post?
- What would I want to notice faster the next time this filing appears?
