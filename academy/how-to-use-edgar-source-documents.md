# How To Use EDGAR Source Documents

This lesson is part of the **News, Catalysts And SEC Filings** course. It follows [SEC Filings: What Traders Should Know](/academy/sec-filings/) and leads into [Form 8-K](/academy/sec-filings/form-8-k/).

EDGAR is the SEC's public filing system. Traders use it to review official company filings instead of relying only on headlines, social media posts, scanner summaries, or copied snippets.

The goal is not to become a securities lawyer. The goal is to know how to find the source document, confirm the form type and date, open the right exhibit, and review the actual language before drawing conclusions.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Find SEC filings using official EDGAR search tools.
- Understand why source documents matter.
- Check form type, filing date, company name, CIK, and exhibits.
- Use full-text search for specific terms.
- Avoid relying on summaries without checking the underlying filing.
- Connect filing review to trade review without treating filings as signals.

## Quick Definition

EDGAR stands for Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval. It is the SEC system that gives the public access to company filings and other disclosure documents.

Official SEC starting points include:

- [SEC Search Filings](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search-and-access)
- [SEC EDGAR Full Text Search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/)
- [SEC How Do I Use EDGAR?](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/quickedgar.htm)
- [SEC About EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/aboutedgar.htm)

The SEC also notes that official filings are the documents submitted through EDGAR, and traders should review the official filing when they need to know what was filed.

## Why Source Documents Matter

Headlines simplify. Social posts shorten. Scanner summaries can miss details.

The source filing may include:

- Exact filing type.
- Filing date and acceptance time.
- Exhibits.
- Offering terms.
- Selling stockholder tables.
- Risk factors.
- Debt or warrant terms.
- Cash runway context.
- Vote proposals.
- Management discussion.
- Footnotes.

For traders, those details can change the context. A press release may say "strategic partnership," while the filing or exhibit may show no revenue terms. A headline may mention a shelf registration, while the actual offering terms may appear later in a prospectus supplement.

## Basic EDGAR Workflow

A practical EDGAR review workflow:

1. Start with the company name, ticker, or CIK.
2. Use SEC company search or full-text search.
3. Confirm the company identity.
4. Check the form type.
5. Check the filing date and accepted time.
6. Open the filing.
7. Review the filing detail page and document list.
8. Open the primary document and any relevant exhibits.
9. Search inside the document for key terms.
10. Write a short review note about what the filing actually says.

The review note should separate facts from assumptions.

## What To Check First

When opening a filing, check:

- Company name.
- Ticker and CIK if available.
- Form type.
- Filing date.
- Period date if applicable.
- Accession number.
- Document list.
- Exhibit list.
- Whether the filing amends an earlier filing.
- Whether the filing relates to a press release, offering, merger, proxy, or ownership change.

Beginners often open the first document and ignore exhibits. That can miss important information, especially when an 8-K has an attached press release, agreement, financial statement, or investor presentation.

## Using Full-Text Search

SEC full-text search can help find filings that contain specific terms. The current SEC EDGAR advanced search gives access to electronic filings since 2001 and lets users filter by form, company, date, and other fields.

Useful search terms might include:

- "at the market"
- "warrant"
- "cashless exercise"
- "going concern"
- "selling stockholder"
- "shelf registration"
- "material definitive agreement"
- "reverse split"
- "substantial doubt"

Search results are not the final answer. They are a way to locate source documents that still need to be read.

## Realistic Example

A small-cap stock announces a new financing. A social post says the company "raised cash" and the stock moves quickly.

A trader checks EDGAR and finds a Form 8-K with an exhibit. The exhibit links to a securities purchase agreement. A later 424B5 shows the offering price, warrants, gross proceeds, and use of proceeds.

The useful review is not whether the filing predicted the move. The useful review is that the trader verified the terms before assuming the headline was simple.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is relying on a screenshot of a filing without opening the official source.

Another mistake is confusing a registration statement with an actual offering.

Traders also ignore exhibits, where important details often live.

Another mistake is searching by ticker but not confirming company identity or CIK.

A final mistake is treating one scary or exciting filing phrase as a guaranteed market reaction.

## Practical Checklist

When using EDGAR, confirm:

- Official SEC source was opened.
- Correct company was selected.
- Form type was identified.
- Filing date and accepted time were checked.
- Primary document was reviewed.
- Relevant exhibits were opened.
- Key terms were searched.
- The filing was connected to related press releases or later filings.
- The trade review separated filing facts from market assumptions.

## Apply This In Review

Ask:

- Did I read the official filing or only a summary?
- Did I identify the form type correctly?
- Did I open the exhibits?
- Did I confuse registered shares with sold shares?
- Did I check whether a later filing changed the context?
- Did I trade a headline before reading the source?
- What filing term should I learn next?

## FAQ

### What is EDGAR?

EDGAR is the SEC's public filing system for company filings and other disclosure documents.

### Why should traders use EDGAR?

EDGAR lets traders review official source documents instead of relying only on headlines, social media, or summaries.

### What should I check first in an EDGAR filing?

Check company identity, form type, filing date, accepted time, primary document, and exhibits.

### Are EDGAR filings trade signals?

No. Filings provide information and context. They do not guarantee price movement or tell traders what to do.

### What is full-text search useful for?

Full-text search can help locate filings containing specific terms, but the trader still needs to open and read the source document.

### Do filings always explain the whole market reaction?

No. Market reaction also depends on expectations, liquidity, volume, chart context, risk, and trader behavior.
