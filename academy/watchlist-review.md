# Watchlist Review

This lesson is part of the **Practice And Improvement** course. It follows [Trade Replay Review](/academy/trade-replay-review/) and leads into [Setup Screenshot Review](/academy/setup-screenshot-review/).

Watchlist review is the process of studying the names you planned to watch after the session ends. It helps answer a simple question: did the watchlist improve your decision-making, or did it create noise?

A good watchlist is not judged only by whether a stock moved. It is judged by whether it gave the trader a clearer plan.

## Lesson Objective

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Review a watchlist after the session.
- Separate good preparation from lucky ticker selection.
- Identify names that created reactive trades.
- Improve watchlist filters over time.
- Connect watchlist review to practice and trade review.

## Quick Definition

Watchlist review means studying planned watchlist names after the session to see whether the selection process was useful.

It can include:

- Catalyst quality.
- Premarket levels.
- Relative volume.
- Liquidity and spread.
- Level clarity.
- Extension risk.
- Whether trades came from planned names.
- Whether reactive additions hurt the process.

The goal is better preparation, not perfect prediction.

## What To Review

Start with the reason each name was on the list.

- Was the catalyst specific?
- Was volume meaningful?
- Was liquidity acceptable?
- Were levels clean?
- Was the chart already extended?
- Did the name fit the trader's current skill set?
- Did the trader actually follow the plan?

If a name moved but was too thin, too spread out, or too unclear to trade responsibly, the watchlist still needs review.

## Planned Names Versus Reactive Names

Many mistakes happen when a trader abandons the watchlist after a fast scanner alert.

Reactive additions are not always wrong, but they need review:

- Why was the name added?
- Was the move already extended?
- Was the catalyst understood?
- Did the trader have time to map risk?
- Did the trade fit the session plan?

The review should show whether additions were thoughtful or impulsive.

## Realistic Example

A trader starts the morning with four planned names. Two have clear catalysts, one has thin volume, and one has a wide spread. During the open, the trader ignores the prepared names and jumps into a fifth ticker from a scanner alert.

The session review shows the fifth ticker caused the weakest decision. The watchlist improvement is not "watch better stocks." It is more specific: no new ticker from a scanner unless catalyst, spread, and level map are reviewed first.

## Common Mistakes

One common mistake is judging the watchlist only by whether a stock moved.

Another mistake is keeping too many names and watching none of them well.

Traders also make mistakes when they add tickers late without a review filter.

Another mistake is ignoring names that did nothing but still had good preparation.

A final mistake is changing watchlist criteria every day without a sample.

## Practical Checklist

- Every watchlist name had a written reason.
- Catalyst, volume, liquidity, and spread were reviewed.
- Key levels were mapped before pressure increased.
- Reactive additions were tracked separately.
- The trader reviewed names that worked and names that did not.
- The review produced one filter adjustment.
- The next watchlist uses the updated filter.

## Apply This In Review

- Which planned names produced clear decisions?
- Which planned names created noise?
- Which trades came from names not on the list?
- Did I add tickers too late?
- Did I ignore liquidity or spread?
- Did my watchlist match my current skill level?
- What one filter should change next session?

## FAQ

### Why review a watchlist after the session?

Watchlist review helps traders see whether preparation created better decisions or whether the list created distraction.

### Should a watchlist be judged only by which stocks moved?

No. A stock can move and still be a poor fit because of liquidity, spread, unclear levels, or excessive extension.

### Are reactive ticker additions always bad?

No, but they should be reviewed separately because they often come with less preparation.

### What is a useful watchlist review note?

A useful note is specific, such as "skip wide-spread names unless liquidity improves," instead of vague, such as "pick better stocks."
